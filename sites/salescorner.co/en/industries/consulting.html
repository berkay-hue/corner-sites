Sales Corner for consulting firms

In consulting you are not selling a product but trust; the client judges you by how well you understand their company in the first meeting. For teams in management, HR, finance and technology consulting, Sales Corner brings pre-meeting company and contact research, first-touch messages written for each decision-maker, call analysis and pre-proposal preparation into one panel. You decide the proposal and the consulting method; research and repetitive writing get faster.

Typical problems in consulting sales

  • The sales cycle is long; months pass between the first meeting and the signature, and the opportunity quietly cools in the meantime.
  • No single person decides: the managing director, the relevant function head, finance and sometimes procurement look at the same proposal with different questions.
  • Consultants often do the selling themselves; between project deliveries there is little time left for meeting prep and follow-up messages.
  • First messages list service headings and do not touch on the growth, restructuring or digitalisation agenda the target company is going through at the time.
  • The needs, budget and timing notes from the discovery call stay in one person’s head; details get lost when the proposal is written.
  • The most common objection after a proposal is "we will do this with our own team", and the answer is worked out from scratch every time.

Why is the long sales cycle so long?

For the client, buying a consulting service is a risk decision: they will open their internal processes to an outside team, set aside budget and share responsibility for the outcome. That is why the decision passes through several people’s approval, and each of them wants different evidence. Guides on where the B2B sales cycle stretches and on mapping who asks which question are on the Sales Corner blog.

In a long cycle, the winner is usually not the most memorable team but the one that brings new value at every touchpoint. Proper preparation before the call, a clear summary sent the same day after it and follow-up messages split by decision-maker may not shorten the process, but they keep the opportunity from cooling.

Which module, and how?

Meeting Brief: walk into the discovery call prepared

Researches the target company’s website, its industry and the profile of the person you will meet; scores the opportunity and prepares a briefing in deck format. You go into an introduction, service presentation, proposal presentation or follow-up meeting with questions about the company’s agenda.

Meeting Analysis (Fireflies): keep discovery notes from getting lost

Analyses calls recorded in Fireflies with methodologies such as SPIN, MEDDIC, Challenger, Sandler, BANT and GAP; extracts talk ratios, objections, budget signals and coaching tips, and drafts a follow-up or proposal email.

Sequence Writer: first touch tailored to the decision-maker

You point to your service presentation, your website or one of your webinar videos as the source, and you get a cold call script and LinkedIn message sequence tailored to the target person. By entering a role such as managing director, HR director or finance manager in the Additional Note field, you tune the message to that person’s priorities.

Competitor Analysis: put your difference into proposal language

Scans competing consulting firms’ websites; compares their service scopes, methodology claims and the industries they highlight, and produces a deck report. You prepare a concrete answer to the "why you" question in the proposal.

Monitors up to 6 competitors’ blog and RSS feeds, new pages in their sitemaps and LinkedIn company posts on a 1, 7, 14 or 30-day cycle, and emails a summary with AI commentary.

Provides an SEO score, keyword analysis, SERP data and a content draft for your service pages and articles. In a long cycle, the content a client finds while researching you warms up the sales conversation in advance.

Sample workflow

  1. Target accounts: Pick the companies that fit your service best and build a small list with an account-based outbound approach; sharpen the criteria with your ideal customer profile (ICP).
  2. Decision-maker map: In each account, identify the decision-maker, the influencer and the approver; score the opportunity with the BANT/MEDDIC scorecard.
  3. First touch: Generate a separate message for each role with Sequence Writer; before sending, check the text in the cold email checker.
  4. Discovery call: Run Meeting Brief before the call; after the call, feed the recording to Meeting Analysis so you no longer carry the note-taking load.
  5. Proposal: Build the proposal skeleton from the needs and budget signals in the analysis, structure it step by step and prepare your answers to price objections.
  6. Waiting period: When the decision drags on, share content that adds value through lead nurturing; watch for the signs of opportunities stalling in the pipeline.

Frequently asked questions

What work does Sales Corner take on at a consulting firm?

It takes on pre-meeting company and contact research, cold call scripts and LinkedIn sequences written for each decision-maker, analysis of calls recorded in Fireflies, and analysis and tracking of competing consulting firms. The consulting method, the pricing and the final proposal stay with your team.

How does it help keep an opportunity alive in a long sales cycle?

After every call, Meeting Analysis extracts objections, budget signals and the next step, and drafts a follow-up email. Competitor Tracking summaries and content produced with SEO Analysis make it easier to find value-adding topics to share during the waiting period.

Does it support building a decision-maker map?

Meeting Brief researches the profile of the person and the company you will meet; Meeting Analysis records the stakeholders named in the call and the signals about the approval process. Your team builds the final map; a decision-maker mapping guide and the BANT/MEDDIC scorecard are ready for this.

Does it help with writing proposals?

After the call, Meeting Analysis drafts a follow-up or proposal email and summarises the needs and budget signals that go into the proposal. Your team writes the core of the proposal, such as the scope, pricing and project plan.

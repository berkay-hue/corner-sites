    How is commission calculated?

    The calculator splits your sales into the tiers of the plan, multiplies each tier by its own rate and adds the result to base salary. All amounts must belong to the same period: if you compare a monthly base salary with an annual quota, the result is meaningless. The steps are:

    1. Quota attainment = closed sales ÷ quota
    2. Tier amounts: sales are split into the part up to quota and the part above quota; in a tiered plan, the part up to quota is further split into the 0–50% and 50–100% tiers.
    3. Tier commission = sales in the tier × tier rate. In flat-rate and bonus plans, the rate for the above-quota tier is the base rate × the accelerator.
    4. Target bonus (if selected) is added once when 100% of quota is reached.
    5. Cap: if you enter one, total variable pay is limited to that amount and the excess is shown on a separate line in the breakdown.
    6. Total gross earnings = base salary + variable pay; effective commission rate = variable pay ÷ closed sales.
    7. OTE is found by recalculating the same plan as if exactly 100% of quota had been reached; the base/variable split is also shown on an OTE basis.

    Illustrative example (fictional)

    The default values are a fictional AE plan: a monthly base salary of $6,000, a monthly quota of $50,000, an 8% commission rate and a 1.5x accelerator above quota. If the rep closes $60,000 in the month, the $50,000 up to quota earns $4,000 and the $10,000 above quota earns $1,200 at 12%. Variable pay is $5,200 and total gross earnings are $11,200. Quota attainment is 120% and the effective commission rate is about 8.7%. At exactly 100% of quota, earnings would have been $10,000; this plan's OTE is $10,000 and its base/variable split is 60/40.

    Which commission structure should you choose?

    A flat rate is the easiest structure to explain: every sales dollar is rewarded at the same rate. Reps can work out their commission in their heads, which builds trust; but when you pay the same rate below and above quota, there is no extra incentive to exceed quota. That is why flat-rate plans are usually paired with an accelerator.

    A tiered structure ties the rate to quota attainment. This calculator has three tiers: the first half of quota, the second half and above quota. Giving the first tier a low or zero rate makes reaching at least half of quota a precondition for payout; our commission plan guide uses 50% as an example of this kind of threshold. The above-quota rate is where extra performance is truly rewarded.

    A target bonus is a fixed amount paid on top of the base rate when quota is reached. When there is a clear moment of hitting quota, such as the quarter close, it focuses the rep's attention on the target. The downside is the cliff effect: a rep who ends at 99% of quota loses the whole bonus, which can create a tendency to push or delay deals at the end of the period.

    Accelerators and caps

    An accelerator raises the rate applied to sales above quota. In the example in our commission plan guide, the rate rises 1.5x between 100% and 130% of quota and 2x above 130%. This calculator uses a single multiplier for sales above quota; if you have several accelerator steps, enter the one most often reached to get an approximate result. If you leave the accelerator at 1, above-quota sales are paid at the base rate and the calculator flags this as a warning.

    A cap puts an upper limit on variable pay. It makes the budget predictable, but as our guide also notes, it is generally not recommended: a rep approaching the cap may prefer to push new deals into the next period. To try a cap, enter the amount and look at the cap deduction line in the breakdown.

    OTE and the base/variable split

    OTE is the number a plan should make clear at first glance: what does the rep earn on hitting quota? The base/variable split shows how much of the risk sits with the rep. Our sales commission plan guide gives these ranges as starting points: 50/50–60/40 for new-business AEs, 70/30–80/20 for SDR/BDR and Account Manager roles, and 80/20–90/10 for pre-sales. The longer the sales cycle and the less the outcome is in one person's control, the larger the base share is expected to be.

    The ratio of quota to OTE is another health indicator worth checking. Our commission plan guide notes that many B2B teams keep this ratio between 4x and 6x, and our sales quota guide states that annual quota is set at roughly 4–5 times OTE. If the ratio is too low, rep cost stays high relative to sales; if it is too high, quota becomes unreachable and the plan loses its motivating power. Also check whether the quota is realistic against your pipeline: the sales target calculator shows how many leads, meetings and proposals it takes to reach this quota.

    Limits and tips

    • Amounts are gross. Income tax, payroll taxes and social security contributions are not calculated; work with your accountant or payroll team for the net commission.
    • Define the commissionable amount. Is your plan tied to the signing date or to payment collection? Paying on collection protects cash flow; keep the amount you enter in the calculator consistent with your plan.
    • Use amounts excluding sales tax or VAT. An invoice total that includes tax inflates the commission base.
    • Fix the exchange rate date for foreign-currency deals. The plan should state whether the rate from the invoice, collection or payout date is used.
    • Clawback rules are not calculated. The plan should define whether commission is clawed back if, for example, the customer churns within 90 days.
    • Don't mix periods. If you use a quarterly quota with a monthly salary, convert the salary to a quarter; in periods of high inflation, check whether quota and base salary are updated on the same schedule.
    • If your plan works differently, the result will differ. Plans where a tier rate applies from the first dollar, plans with different rates by product and plans with bonuses tied to team targets are outside the scope of this calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    How is sales commission calculated?

    Split closed sales into the tiers of the plan and multiply each tier by its own rate. In a flat-rate plan, sales up to quota are multiplied by the base rate and sales above quota by the rate raised by the accelerator. Add the resulting commission to base salary to get total gross earnings for the period.

    What is OTE and how is it calculated?

    OTE (on-target earnings) is the total a rep earns on hitting exactly 100% of quota: base salary plus the variable pay earned at quota. For example, with a $6,000 base and $4,000 of variable pay at quota, OTE is $10,000 and the base/variable split is 60/40.

    In a tiered plan, does the rate apply to all sales?

    Not in this calculator. Tiers are marginal: each rate applies only to the sales that fall within its own tier, just like tax brackets. Some plans apply the rate of the tier reached to all sales from the first dollar; if yours works that way, the result will differ, so check the definition in your plan.

    Does the calculator account for taxes and payroll deductions?

    No. All amounts are gross; income tax, payroll taxes and social security contributions are not taken into account. Work out how the commission shows up in payroll, and the net amount, with your accountant or payroll team.

    Are the values I enter saved?

    No. The calculation runs entirely in your browser; values are not sent to a server and no sign-up is required.

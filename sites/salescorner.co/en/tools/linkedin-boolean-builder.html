    How is the Boolean query built?

    The tool combines the three inputs with three different kinds of logic. Job titles are different spellings of the same role, so they are joined with OR inside parentheses: the person only needs to have one of them. Required words are added with AND, and all of them must appear on the profile. Exclusions go at the end with NOT; they filter out profiles that get mixed up with the role you are looking for, such as interns, students or recruiters. Four rules are applied when the query is built:

    1. Quotes: Every term made of more than one word, or containing a character such as a hyphen, is wrapped in straight double quotes. Written without quotes, sales manager can bring back anyone who has the two words in different places on their profile.
    2. Parentheses: The OR group is wrapped in parentheses. On LinkedIn the order of operations is quotes, parentheses, NOT, AND, OR, so in a query without parentheses the AND condition applies only to the last title.
    3. Uppercase: AND, OR and NOT are written in uppercase. A lowercase “or” may be searched as an ordinary word instead of an operator.
    4. Words instead of symbols: On LinkedIn, use AND and NOT instead of + and -; partial matching with an asterisk (*) is not supported. The tool strips quotes, parentheses and asterisks from your input and adds the correct format itself.

    Illustrative / fictional example: If you enter “sales manager, head of sales” as job titles, “B2B” as a required word, “intern” in the exclude field and “New York” as the location, the LinkedIn query looks like this:

    ("sales manager" OR "head of sales") AND B2B AND "New York" NOT intern

    Why are the three queries different?

    The LinkedIn search box is a single text field. We add the location or industry word to the query with AND too; on the results page you can switch to the People tab and keep narrowing with filters.

    Sales Navigator offers fields such as location and industry as separate filters. That is why we leave the location out of the keyword query: we write the title group and the required words, and suggest you choose the location from the filter. If you don’t use Sales Navigator, you can still use the LinkedIn search box or Google X-ray queries.

    The Google X-ray query uses site:linkedin.com/in to target only public profile pages. On Google there is no need to write AND, because words are already searched together; instead of NOT, a minus sign (-) goes in front of the term, and OR is still written in uppercase. Because Google ignores everything after about 32 words in long queries, the tool shows this query’s word count separately. X-ray only finds profiles that Google has indexed; results may be incomplete or out of date.

    How do you build a good title list?

    First get clear on who you sell to: your ideal customer profile tells you which role owns the budget and which one is the day-to-day user. Then list the different spellings of the same role, its abbreviations (CMO, CTO, VP) and seniority variants (manager, director, head of). If you sell into markets where profiles are written in other languages, add the local-language titles as well; using only one language narrows the results unnecessarily.

    Avoid single-word generic titles: “manager” or “director” on its own brings back a large number of irrelevant profiles. If more than one person takes part in the buying decision, map the decision makers first and build a separate query for each role. If you run account-based outbound, pick the target companies first, then search for the roles at those companies.

    Limits and tips

    • The number of results you can see depends on your account type and connection degree; the same query can return different results on two different accounts.
    • The tool uses 300 characters as a practical warning threshold; this is not a limit published by LinkedIn. In long queries it gets harder to see which term is working, and splitting the list in two often gives better results.
    • If the results are too broad, first add a required word, then put words such as intern, student or freelance in the exclude list. If they are too narrow, add synonyms to the title group and cut back the required words.
    • When you process the data of the people you find, keep data protection law in mind, such as GDPR in the EU. Enriching the list with company data is a separate step after the search.
    • The tool does not connect to LinkedIn, does not pull profiles and does not run automated searches; it only produces text for you to copy and paste.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which operators work in LinkedIn Boolean search?

    Double quotes for exact phrases, parentheses for grouping, and AND, OR, NOT written in uppercase. The order of operations is quotes, parentheses, NOT, AND, OR. Use AND and NOT instead of + and -; partial matching with an asterisk (*) is not supported.

    Why does the tool produce three separate queries?

    The three search fields follow different rules. The LinkedIn search box is a single field, so the location goes into the query too. In Sales Navigator, location and industry are separate filters, so the keyword query keeps only the titles and words. On Google there is no need to write AND, and a minus sign (-) goes in front of the term instead of NOT.

    What should I do if my query is too long or too broad?

    If it is too broad, add a required word and fill in the exclude list; if it is too narrow, add synonyms to the title group and cut back the required words. Splitting a long title list into two separate queries also makes it easier to see which term brings results.

    Are the values I enter saved?

    No. The queries are built entirely in your browser; the values you enter are not sent to a server or saved, and no sign-up is required.

    Does this tool connect to LinkedIn?

    No. The tool does not connect to LinkedIn, does not pull profiles and does not run automated searches; it only produces text for you to copy and paste. LinkedIn and Sales Navigator are trademarks of LinkedIn Corporation; Sales Corner is not affiliated with LinkedIn.

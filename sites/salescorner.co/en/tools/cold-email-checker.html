        How is the score calculated?

        • Length (20 points): 50–125 words gets full points. Cold emails are mostly read on a phone; a long email gets deleted instead of answered.
        • Sentence length (15 points): An average under 15 words is ideal; each sentence over 25 words costs another 5 points.
        • Readability (15 points): The Flesch Reading Ease formula is used: 206.835 − 1.015 × (words ÷ sentences) − 84.6 × (syllables ÷ words). 70 and above is easy, 50–69 is moderate, and below 50 is hard to read.
        • Recipient focus (15 points): The ratio of "you, your, your team" to "we, our company, our product".
        • Clear call to action (10 points): The email ends with a question and a concrete next step (a call, 15 minutes).
        • Links (10), spam words (10) and exclamation marks (5): At most one link in a first email, zero spam triggers and a tone without exclamation marks.

        Syllables are estimated from vowel groups; acronyms and numbers can affect the score slightly. Use the score not as an absolute grade but to compare versions of the same email.

        Structure of a good cold email

        An effective cold email has four parts: a personal opening that shows why you chose the recipient, a concrete problem the recipient faces, a single piece of proof (a number or a similar customer) and an easy-to-answer question. The company introduction, the product feature list and multiple links belong not in the first email but in the conversation after a reply. Check your subject line too with the subject line scorer.

        Frequently asked questions

        How many words should a cold email be?

        For a B2B cold email, 50–125 words is a good range. At this length it can be read on a phone without scrolling, and it is enough for a personal opening, the problem, proof and a call to action.

        What is the Flesch Reading Ease formula?

        Flesch Reading Ease scores how easy a text is to read: 206.835 − 1.015 × (average words per sentence) − 84.6 × (average syllables per word). The higher the score, the easier the text; 70 and above is considered easy, below 50 difficult.

        Does adding links to a cold email hurt deliverability?

        Multiple links and tracking links draw the attention of spam filters. Using no link, or at most one link, in the first email is safer.

        Does it work for emails in other languages?

        The checker is tuned for English: syllable counting, the Flesch formula, the spam word list and the "you" and "we" lists all assume English text. Scores for emails in other languages are less reliable.

        Is the text I paste saved?

        No. The check runs entirely in your browser; the text is not sent to a server, it is not saved, and no sign-up is required.

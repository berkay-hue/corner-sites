    How does the planner work?

    Domain warm-up means ramping up volume gradually so a new sending setup earns a reputation with mailbox providers. The planner calculates two separate streams for each mailbox: warm-up traffic (a warm-up tool or friendly addresses emailing back and forth) and cold email (sends to real prospects). Both grow on business days only; on weekends either nothing is sent or, if you choose, warm-up continues at half volume.

    For every day from the start date, the schedule shows warm-up and cold emails per mailbox, their sum, and the total across all mailboxes. The first day cold sending reaches your per-mailbox target is marked as the target day; the table ends after one more week at target volume. Once cold sending reaches 20, warm-up traffic drops to 20 per mailbox per day and stays on, because the common practice is to keep warm-up running alongside cold sending.

    Assumptions by domain age

    Domain age sets the starting pace. New domains send warm-up traffic only for the first two weeks; domains in use for a few months can start cold sending earlier and at a higher number. The daily per-mailbox values the planner uses:

    These values follow ramps widely used in the industry: 5–15 warm-up emails a day in week one, 15–25 in week two, cold sending starting at 5–10 a day in week three, and a 30–50 a day band for a warmed-up mailbox. If your provider or warm-up tool has its own recommendations, follow those first.

    Before you start the schedule

    • Use a secondary domain. Sending cold email from a separate lookalike domain instead of your main company domain protects the main domain's reputation. Two to three mailboxes per domain is a common practice.
    • Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC. SPF should be a single record, DKIM signing should be enabled, and DMARC can start at p=none. Google's and Yahoo's bulk sender rules have made all three effectively mandatory.
    • Set up mailboxes like a real user. Add MX records, a real name, a photo and a signature; don't start heavy sending the day you create the mailbox.
    • Buy the domain early. Buying the domain 5–6 weeks before the campaign leaves time to warm it up; starting to warm one or two spare domains as well prevents interruptions.

    What to monitor during warm-up

    The schedule is a starting plan; your metrics decide the pace. Once cold sending starts, check your bounce rate every day; if it goes above 2%, pause sending and verify the list. Google's spam complaint threshold is 0.3%. Add your domain to Google Postmaster Tools; keep ramping while reputation is "High" or "Medium", and if it shows "Low" or "Bad", stop cold sending and go back to warm-up only. Not using the same template in every mailbox, removing duplicate addresses from the list and spacing your sequence steps sensibly also protect your reputation. If you are not getting replies, the problem may be the message rather than the volume. If you email recipients in other countries, also check the data-protection and anti-spam rules that apply there.

    Illustrative example

    Illustrative / fictional example: A team warming up 3 mailboxes on a new domain, targeting 30 cold emails per mailbox per day and not sending on weekends, starts the schedule on a Monday. In week one warm-up goes from 5 to 13 per mailbox, and in week two from 15 to 23. On the Monday of week three (business day 11) cold sending starts at 5 emails per mailbox. When cold sending reaches 20 at the start of week five, warm-up drops to 20. The target of 30 is reached on business day 28, the Wednesday of week six. At that point the team can send 90 cold emails a day; with 22 business days a month, that is a capacity of about 1,980 cold emails. One to two domains are enough for 3 mailboxes.

    Limits and tips

    • Grow volume by adding mailboxes. Instead of going above 50 a day per mailbox, the widely accepted approach is to add new mailboxes and domains and warm those up too.
    • Watch metrics, not dates. If bounces or spam complaints rise, drop back to the previous volume regardless of the date in the schedule.
    • Public holidays are not taken into account. If you won't send on holidays, shift the dates in the CSV.
    • Start with the warmest segments. In the first days of cold sending, starting with verified recipients who fit you best lowers the risk of negative signals.
    • Match capacity to targets. Use the sales target calculator to see how many meetings your monthly sending capacity can turn into, and compare it with the daily activity you expect from each rep.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long does domain warm-up take?

    In widely accepted practice, warm-up takes 3–6 weeks. On a new domain, only warm-up traffic is sent for the first two weeks, and cold email starts at around 5 a day in week three. For a new domain, the planner produces a schedule that reaches a target of 30 cold emails per mailbox per day in about 28 business days.

    How many cold emails can one mailbox send per day?

    For a warmed-up mailbox, the widely accepted safe range is 30–50 cold emails a day; more than 50 is considered risky. If you need more volume, it is safer to add new mailboxes and domains and warm those up too, rather than raising the number per mailbox.

    Should I send warm-up emails on weekends?

    Cold email is usually not sent on weekends. Instead of stopping warm-up traffic completely, keeping it going at low volume on weekends is a common practice; in the planner, this option keeps warm-up at half volume on weekends.

    Can I start warm-up without SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

    It is not recommended. Google's and Yahoo's bulk sender rules have made these three records effectively mandatory; warming up a domain with incomplete authentication does not build reputation. Set up the records first and start DMARC at p=none. For more on deliverability, see the Sales Corner blog.

    Should I turn off warm-up once I reach my target?

    No. The common practice is to keep warm-up traffic running at 10–20 emails per mailbox per day for as long as you send cold email. If reputation drops in Postmaster Tools, the recommended step is to stop cold sending and go back to warm-up only.

    Are the values I enter saved?

    No, everything is calculated in your browser. The values you enter are not sent to a server or stored, and the CSV file is also generated in your browser and downloaded straight to your computer. No sign-up is required.

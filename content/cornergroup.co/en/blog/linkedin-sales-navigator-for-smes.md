**Not every SME needs LinkedIn Sales Navigator. But for B2B SMEs whose target customers are active on LinkedIn, who run regular outbound sales and whose average deal value is high, it is one of the most efficient prospecting tools available in 2025.** In short, the decision shouldn't hinge on how powerful the tool is. It should hinge on how much your sales process depends on LinkedIn, and whether a single additional sale would cover the cost of the subscription.

Many SME leaders subscribe to Sales Navigator thinking it's "the premium version of LinkedIn," only to spend the next two months paying for a licence nobody opens. Others waste years on free LinkedIn search, manually combing through hundreds of profiles every week. In this article, we lay out in concrete steps what Sales Navigator actually does, what it costs, which kind of business it suits, how to calculate your return on investment and, if you've already bought it, how to get value from it in the first 30 days.

## What exactly does LinkedIn Sales Navigator do?

Sales Navigator is a separate interface that LinkedIn built for sales teams. It works alongside your regular LinkedIn account but offers far more advanced search, tracking and messaging features. Its core functions can be summarised as follows:

- **Advanced lead and account (company) search:** You can search for people and companies using dozens of filters, including industry, company size, geography, job title, seniority level, department, time in current company and recent job changes. Most of these filters aren't available on free LinkedIn.
- **Saved searches and alerts:** Once you set up search criteria, you're notified whenever new people match them. For example, you can save a search for "newly appointed purchasing managers at manufacturing companies in Istanbul with 50-200 employees" and see new names every week.
- **Lead and account lists:** You organise target people and companies into lists, add notes, and track their posts and job changes from a single screen.
- **InMail credits:** These let you message people you aren't connected to. The number of credits depends on your plan and is limited.
- **Buying signals:** The tool highlights signals such as growth at a target company, hiring activity, decision-makers changing companies, or engagement with your company page.
- **AI-powered summaries:** Features rolled out from 2024 onwards include summaries of company and individual profiles, identifying common ground, and help with drafting messages. Availability may vary by plan and region.
- **Team and CRM features (higher tiers):** Higher-tier plans offer features such as finding warm introduction paths through your teammates' connections (TeamLink), CRM sync and activity logging.

There's one critical point here: Sales Navigator **does not give you contact details (email, phone).** It helps you find who to target and reach them within LinkedIn. For email addresses and phone numbers, you need separate data tools. SMEs that subscribe without understanding this distinction end up disappointed.

## How much does it cost?

Sales Navigator has three main plans. Prices are in US dollars, change from time to time and differ depending on whether you pay monthly or annually. So treat the figures below as **approximate** and check LinkedIn's current pricing page before you buy.

- **Core:** The basic package for individual salespeople. It costs roughly $100 per user on monthly billing, or the equivalent of about $80 per month on annual billing. It includes advanced search, lists, alerts and a limited number of InMail credits per month.
- **Advanced:** For small teams. It costs roughly $130-150 per user per month. On top of Core, it offers features such as TeamLink, team management and Smart Links (seeing who opened the content you shared).
- **Advanced Plus:** Includes CRM integration and enterprise administration features. Pricing is usually quote-based, and most SMEs don't need it at the outset.

Here's the practical maths for an SME in Turkey: a single Core licence billed annually costs about $960. Assuming a hypothetical exchange rate of 40 TL, that comes to roughly 38,000-40,000 TL a year. Buying Core for a three-person sales team pushes the annual cost to around 115,000-120,000 TL. You also need to factor in VAT and currency fluctuations.

On its own, this figure is neither "expensive" nor "cheap." The real question is how many new customers it takes to cover the cost. We'll work through an example below.

## Which SMEs need it, and which don't?

Sales Navigator's value depends on whether your target audience is on LinkedIn, on your sales model and on your team's discipline. The checklist below will help you clarify your decision.

### Sales Navigator is probably right for you if

- Your customers are corporate companies, and your decision-makers (such as general managers, purchasing managers, IT directors or HR managers) have active LinkedIn profiles.
- Your average deal value is high; a single new customer easily covers the annual licence cost.
- Your sales team does regular weekly outbound, or plans to.
- You're expanding into international markets and want to reach foreign buyers in a way that's contextual rather than cold.
- You do account-based marketing (ABM), meaning you need to track a specific set of 50-200 companies in depth.

Export-focused manufacturers are the clearest example of this group. Sales Navigator's filters save a great deal of time when looking for foreign distributors and buyers. We covered the hands-on details of this topic in our article on [LinkedIn outreach and lead generation strategies for exporting manufacturers](/blog/linkedin-outreach-for-exporting-manufacturers-prospecting-strategies).

### You probably don't need Sales Navigator if

- Your customers are tradespeople, small retailers or field businesses that don't use LinkedIn.
- Most of your sales come through a dealer network, tenders or inbound enquiries.
- Your average order value is low and your sales are volume-driven.
- Your target market is so narrow that you already know every potential customer.
- No one on your team will be consistently active on LinkedIn. Buying a licence doesn't create a usage habit.

The last point is the one most often overlooked. Sales Navigator is a tool; if nobody uses it, its value is zero. ## How do you calculate the return on investment?

The soundest way to decide is to run a simple ROI calculation with your own numbers. The example below is entirely hypothetical; plug in your own data.

**Assumptions:**

- An SME selling industrial software buys a Core licence for one sales rep.
- Annual licence cost: approximately 40,000 TL.
- Average annual contract value: 250,000 TL.
- The rep reaches out to 40 new target contacts per week via LinkedIn (connection request + follow-up message).
- 25 percent of these contacts accept the connection, and 10 percent of those who accept turn into a meeting.
- 15 percent of meetings close as a sale.

**Calculation:**

1. 40 contacts per week × 25 percent acceptance = 10 new connections.
2. 10 connections × 10 percent = 1 meeting per week. Assuming roughly 45 working weeks a year, that's 45 meetings.
3. 45 meetings × 15 percent close rate = about 6-7 new customers.
4. 6 customers × 250,000 TL = 1,500,000 TL in new annual revenue.

In this scenario, the licence cost is a tiny fraction of the revenue generated. But run the same calculation for a business with an average deal value of 15,000 TL and the picture changes: you'd need at least 3 new customers a year just to cover the licence, and proving that those customers actually came through Sales Navigator becomes harder.

Don't forget to factor in the rep's time. The licence fee is usually a small part of the total cost; the real cost is the hours the rep spends on LinkedIn. ## How far can you get with free LinkedIn?

Before buying Sales Navigator, ask yourself whether you've genuinely pushed free LinkedIn to its limits. Many SMEs can get meaningful results from the free version in the first 2-3 months.

What you can do with free LinkedIn:

- Search for people using basic filters (location, current company, industry).
- Send connection requests and message your connections.
- Follow company pages and browse employee lists.
- Attract your target audience's attention by posting content regularly.

The main obstacles you'll hit with the free version:

- **Monthly search limit:** Heavy users eventually run into a "commercial use limit" warning.
- **Missing filters:** Without filters such as seniority level, company size and department, finding the right person takes hours.
- **Difficult tracking:** You're forced to keep target lists in Excel, and you miss job changes and posts.
- **Access to 3rd-degree connections:** Viewing full profiles of people outside your network is restricted.

Practical tip: First, spend 30 days trying to hit your weekly target number of contacts using the free version. If your team can sustain that discipline and keeps hitting the limits, switching to Sales Navigator makes sense. If there's no consistent activity even on the free version, the paid version won't solve the problem.

## What changed with Sales Navigator in 2025?

From a "trends" perspective, 2025 is the year Sales Navigator's role is being redefined. The key changes are:

### AI features became part of everyday use

LinkedIn added AI features to Sales Navigator, including company and individual summaries, common-ground detection and suggested message drafts. These features cut research time, but messages written entirely by AI are becoming increasingly easy for recipients to spot. The right approach is to use AI for research and a first draft, then always personalise the message.

### Connection and messaging limits matter more

LinkedIn caps the number of connection requests per week. This limit is widely reported to be around 100 per week, though it can vary depending on the account's history and acceptance rate. This has put an end to the "send connection requests in bulk and see what sticks" approach. The winning approach in 2025 is fewer but better-targeted touches.

### Tighter enforcement against automation tools

LinkedIn's terms of service prohibit third-party tools that automate actions on the platform. Accounts using such extensions are frequently restricted. Our recommendation for SMEs is to keep actions within LinkedIn manual and use automation for off-platform processes such as CRM logging, reminders and reporting. For example, with an automation tool like n8n, you can make sure that a lead you manually export from Sales Navigator lands in your CRM as a task and that a reminder is sent to the rep.

### Multichannel outbound became the standard

Outbound that relies on LinkedIn messages alone no longer delivers sufficient results. In 2025, high-performing teams build sequences that combine LinkedIn, email and phone. Sales Navigator is used to find and warm up target contacts, while email and phone are used to scale.

### Intent signals matter more

Signals such as company hiring activity, new executive appointments and content engagement deliver far higher conversion than mass-messaging cold lists. Setting up Sales Navigator's alert system around these signals is the most valuable way to use the tool in 2025.

## A step-by-step plan to get value from Sales Navigator in the first 30 days

If you've bought a licence or decided to, the first month will determine whether the tool creates value for you. Follow the plan below.

### Week 1: Targeting and setup

1. **Write down your ideal customer profile (ICP):** Pin down criteria such as industry, employee count range, geography, and the technologies they use or how they're structured.
2. **Define your buyer personas:** Who makes the decision, who influences it, who uses the product? List 2-3 job title variations for each persona (e.g. "Purchasing Manager", "Supply Chain Manager", "Procurement Manager").
3. **Enter your Sales Preferences:** Sales Navigator tailors its recommendations based on this information.
4. **Create 3-5 saved searches:** Set up a separate search for each persona and turn on weekly alerts.

Keep in mind that a search shows a maximum of 2,500 results. If your result count is far above that, narrow your filters; a list that broad isn't suitable for personalised outreach anyway.

### Week 2: Account lists and research

1. **Build a target account list of 50-100 companies.** These should be the companies you'll focus on over the coming quarter.
2. **Add 2-3 people from each company to a lead list.** Target both the decision-maker and the influencer so you're not dependent on a single person.
3. **Review their posts from the last 90 days.** Find a topic, problem or success you can reference in your message.

### Week 3: Outreach and messaging

1. **Engage first, connect second:** Leave a meaningful comment on one of the target person's posts, then send a connection request 1-2 days later.
2. **Don't sell in the connection request.** A short, contextual note is enough: a shared interest, an industry development or a reference to one of their posts.
3. **Once the connection is accepted, send a value-focused first message.** For example, an industry-specific insight, a short checklist or a relevant piece of content.
4. **Use InMail strategically.** Your credits are limited; reserve them for high-priority people who haven't accepted your connection request.

### Week 4: Measurement and decision

At the end of the month, pull the following metrics:

- Number of connection requests sent and acceptance rate
- Reply rate to the first message
- Number of meetings booked
- Number of opportunities added to the pipeline and their estimated value

Compare these figures with the previous period or with other channels. ## The most common mistakes SMEs make

Businesses that don't get the results they expect from Sales Navigator tend to repeat the same mistakes:

- **Buying a licence for everyone:** Rather than buying licences for an entire five-person team, start with the one or two people responsible for outbound. Expand as results come in.
- **Targeting too broadly:** "All general managers in Turkey" is not a target audience. Start with narrow, precise filters.
- **Sending a sales pitch the moment a connection is accepted:** This lowers your reply rate and increases the risk of your account being reported as spam.
- **Disconnecting from the CRM:** If leads found in Sales Navigator aren't entered into the CRM, follow-up gets lost and managers can't see the pipeline.
- **Relying solely on InMail:** InMail credits are limited, and cold InMails generally get lower reply rates than messages sent to warm connections.
- **Using prohibited automation extensions:** They may seem to save time in the short term, but an account restriction puts the network you've spent months building at risk.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free trial of LinkedIn Sales Navigator?

LinkedIn usually offers a one-month free trial of Sales Navigator, but the offer can vary by account and over time. If you prepare your ICP and target lists before starting the trial, you can use the 30 days to genuinely test whether the tool is right for you. If you don't cancel before the trial ends, billing starts automatically.

### Does Sales Navigator provide email addresses and phone numbers?

No, Sales Navigator doesn't provide people's contact details; it only lets you search, track and message within LinkedIn. If you need business email addresses or phone numbers, you'll have to use a separate B2B data tool. When using this data, it's important to comply with KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law) and the data protection rules in your target market.

### Is Core or Advanced better for an SME?

For sales teams of one or two people, Core is sufficient in most cases; advanced search, lists and alerts are already included in this package. If you want to find warm introduction paths through your teammates' connections and manage team usage centrally, Advanced is worth considering. Starting with Core and upgrading when the need arises is the lower-risk path.

### Is there a risk of my account being restricted when using Sales Navigator?

Because Sales Navigator is an official LinkedIn product, it poses no risk under normal use. The risk arises when bulk connection requests or messages are sent through third-party automation extensions, and when acceptance rates are low. Accounts that make targeted, personalised contact at reasonable volumes generally don't run into problems.

The KPIs a B2B sales team should track fall into four main groups: **activity metrics** (number of calls, emails and meetings), **pipeline metrics** (pipeline value, coverage ratio, sales velocity), **conversion metrics** (stage-to-stage conversion and win rate) and **revenue metrics** (quota attainment, average deal value, customer acquisition cost). The best approach is to choose 4-6 core KPIs for each role. These KPIs should cover both leading indicators (what is being done today) and lagging indicators (what was ultimately achieved). All of them should be monitored weekly through a single CRM.

The challenge, however, isn't knowing the list of metrics. It's choosing which ones are truly meaningful for your team. Many B2B companies either measure nothing at all or measure everything and end up focusing on nothing. In this guide, we group KPIs into categories, show how each one is calculated with examples, explain which metrics should take priority for each role, and share the steps for building a KPI system from scratch.

## What is the difference between a KPI and a metric?

Every KPI is a metric, but not every metric is a KPI. A **metric** is any data point you can measure: the number of emails sent, open rate, LinkedIn profile views and so on. A **KPI (Key Performance Indicator)** is a metric that is directly tied to a business goal and enables you to make decisions.

You can use a simple test: if you don't have a clear action in mind for the question "What would I do if this number dropped 20% tomorrow?", that number is probably not a KPI but simply a metric worth monitoring. For example, email open rate on its own is not a meaningful KPI. But when your "positive reply rate" drops, you know you need to review your messaging, your target audience or your data quality. That is what makes it a true KPI.

## Why should leading and lagging indicators be tracked together?

The most useful way to understand sales KPIs is to separate them along a timeline:

- **Leading indicators:** Metrics that predict future outcomes and that the team can directly control. The number of discovery calls held, new opportunities created and proposals sent fall into this group.
- **Lagging indicators:** These show results that have already happened. Closed revenue, quota attainment and customer churn rate are examples.

A sales manager who only looks at lagging indicators finds out at the end of the quarter that the target was missed, when there's nothing left to be done. A manager who only looks at leading indicators keeps the team "busy" but can't see whether that activity is turning into revenue. A healthy KPI dashboard includes both in a balanced way.

## Activity metrics: Is the team doing enough of the right work?

Activity metrics are the fuel of the sales engine. Especially in outbound-heavy teams, these numbers largely determine how full the pipeline will be.

### Core activity KPIs to track

- **Daily/weekly touch count:** The total of calls, emails and LinkedIn messages. It should be broken down by channel.
- **Connect rate:** The share of calls that turn into an actual conversation with a decision-maker.
- **Positive reply rate:** The share of interested replies to outbound messages sent. "Not interested" replies should be counted separately.
- **Meetings booked:** The most critical output of SDRs.
- **Show rate:** The share of booked meetings that actually take place.

### A concrete example

Let's say an SDR reaches 250 people per week. With a positive reply rate of 4%, that means 10 interested replies. If half of these convert into meetings, 5 meetings are booked per week. With an 80% show rate, 4 meetings take place. Knowing which link in this chain is weak shows you where to intervene. If the reply rate is low, the problem lies in messaging or targeting. If the show rate is low, pre-meeting reminders and the value proposition are weak.

There's one point that is often overlooked here: **data quality**. Incorrect email addresses and wrong job titles distort your activity metrics from the start. ## Pipeline metrics: Is your funnel healthy enough to carry the target?

Pipeline metrics are the most reliable predictor of future revenue. However, these metrics are only meaningful if your pipeline stages are clearly defined. If your stages aren't clear yet, we recommend first checking out [our step-by-step guide to setting up a sales pipeline in HubSpot](https://cornergroup.co/blog/hubspot-satis-pipeline-kurulumu-adim-adim-rehber).

### Pipeline value and pipeline coverage

**Pipeline coverage ratio = Total open pipeline value / Sales target for the period**

If your quarterly target is 3,000,000 TL and your open pipeline is 9,000,000 TL, your coverage ratio is 3x. A widely accepted rule of thumb in the industry is to aim for a range of 3x-4x, depending on your win rate. However, the right ratio should be derived from your own historical win rate. If your win rate is 25%, you theoretically need at least 4x coverage to hit your target.

### Pipeline velocity

Sales velocity is one of the most comprehensive metrics, showing how much revenue your pipeline generates per day:

**Sales velocity = (Number of opportunities × Average deal value × Win rate) / Average sales cycle (days)**

Sample calculation:
- 40 active opportunities
- 200,000 TL average deal value
- 25% win rate
- 60-day average sales cycle

(40 × 200,000 × 0.25) / 60 = **approximately 33,300 TL/day**

The power of this formula is that it reveals four levers at once. You can increase the number of opportunities, grow deal value, raise your win rate or shorten the cycle. For example, reducing the sales cycle from 60 days to 45 days, with everything else held constant, raises daily velocity to approximately 44,400 TL.

### Time in stage

Tracking the average number of days an opportunity stays in each stage reveals bottlenecks. Opportunities that sit in the proposal stage for an average of 25 days may point to a problem with decision-maker access or pricing. ## Conversion metrics: Where in the funnel are you losing deals?

Conversion rates are the bridge between activity and revenue. Each stage transition needs to be measured separately:

1. **Lead → Qualified lead (MQL/SQL) conversion**
2. **Qualified lead → Discovery call**
3. **Discovery call → Opportunity (open pipeline)**
4. **Opportunity → Proposal/Demo**
5. **Proposal → Closed-won deal (win rate)**

### Calculating win rate correctly

Win rate is one of the most frequently miscalculated KPIs. The correct formula is:

**Win rate = Won opportunities / (Won + Lost opportunities)**

Adding still-open opportunities to the denominator artificially lowers the rate. Marking records that were never real opportunities as "lost" also distorts it. That's why you need a clear definition of an "opportunity." For example, you can require that budget, decision-maker and timeline information be verified before a record counts as an opportunity.

### Loss reasons

Conversion metrics are incomplete without recorded loss reasons. Create a mandatory "loss reason" field in your CRM and keep the options limited: price, competitor chosen, timing, decision-maker change, no need, no response. ## Revenue and efficiency metrics: Is the sales team growing profitably?

These metrics are what the board and the finance team care about most.

- **Quota attainment rate:** Actual sales / target. It should be tracked at both the individual and team level. If only a small portion of the team is hitting quota, the problem most likely lies not with the individuals but with the quota itself.
- **Average deal value (ACV/ADS):** The average size of closed deals. If it's declining over time, the team may be closing deals through discounts.
- **Average sales cycle:** The number of days from first contact to close. It should be calculated separately by segment (SMB, mid-market, enterprise).
- **Customer acquisition cost (CAC):** Sales and marketing spend / number of new customers acquired. For example, if you spent a total of 600,000 TL in a quarter and won 12 customers, your CAC is 50,000 TL.
- **CAC payback period:** The number of months it takes to recover CAC from the monthly gross profit generated by a customer. It's a critical metric in subscription models.
- **Discount rate:** The average discount given relative to list price. If your commission plan only rewards revenue, this rate quietly creeps up.

It's essential that revenue KPIs are aligned with your commission structure. If what you reward your team for doesn't match what you measure, behavior will always follow the reward. ## Which KPIs should be assigned to which role?

Giving everyone the same set of KPIs is one of the most common mistakes. Each role controls a different area.

### SDR / BDR (Sales Development Representative)

- Weekly touch count (by channel)
- Positive reply rate
- Meetings booked and meetings held
- Number of qualified opportunities generated from SDR-sourced meetings

If you measure SDRs only by meeting count, you'll get low-quality meetings. That's why you should always add the number of "opportunities accepted by the AE."

### AE (Account Executive)

- Pipeline value managed and coverage ratio
- Opportunity → proposal and proposal → win conversion
- Average deal value and sales cycle
- Quota attainment rate

### Account Manager / Customer Success

- Net revenue retention (NRR)
- Upsell and cross-sell revenue
- Customer churn rate
- Renewal rate

### Sales Manager

- Team quota attainment
- Forecast accuracy: The difference between revenue forecast at the start of the period and actual revenue
- Team sales velocity
- Productivity per rep and time to reach full productivity after hiring (ramp time)

## How many KPIs should you track?

The short answer: **4-6 KPIs per role, and 8-10 KPIs on the management dashboard**. More than that is distracting; fewer creates blind spots.

A practical method is to use the following structure for each role:

- 2 leading indicators (activity or pipeline)
- 1-2 conversion indicators
- 1-2 outcome indicators (revenue or quota)

All other metrics can appear in reports, but they don't set the agenda for the weekly meeting. They're only used when investigating the root cause of a problem.

## How do you build a KPI system step by step?

You can follow these steps to build a KPI framework from scratch:

1. **Work backward from your revenue target.** If you have a quarterly target of 3,000,000 TL and an average deal value of 200,000 TL, you need to close 15 deals. With a 25% win rate, that means 60 opportunities. If your discovery-call-to-opportunity conversion is 50%, you need 120 discovery calls. On a weekly basis, that works out to roughly 9-10 calls.
2. **Put stage definitions in writing.** Concepts like "qualified lead," "opportunity" and "proposal stage" must mean the same thing to everyone. These definitions should be part of your sales playbook.
3. **Choose KPIs by role.** Using the structure above, assign 4-6 KPIs to each role.
4. **Measure your current state (baseline).** Collect at least 4-6 weeks of real data before setting targets. Unrealistic targets quickly destroy the team's trust in the KPI system.
5. **Make the CRM your single source of truth.** Data that lives in Excel, a notebook or a personal calendar doesn't exist in the KPI system. Any activity not logged in the CRM should be treated as if it never happened.
6. **Set up dashboards and a cadence.** Focus on leading indicators in weekly one-on-ones, conversion rates in monthly team meetings, and revenue metrics in quarterly reviews.
7. **Review once a quarter.** As the market, product and team structure change, KPIs should change too. Dropping a metric that isn't working is also part of a healthy system.

## What are the most common KPI tracking mistakes?

- **Focusing on vanity metrics:** A high number of emails sent looks impressive, but it means nothing if it doesn't convert into replies and meetings.
- **Rewarding quantity without measuring quality:** An SDR measured only by meeting count will fill the calendar with unqualified meetings.
- **Ignoring context:** Comparing the sales cycle of an AE working in the enterprise segment with that of an AE in the SMB segment isn't fair.
- **Using KPIs as a punishment tool:** When metrics aren't used for coaching and development, the team starts "polishing" the data and the system's reliability collapses.
- **Never updating them:** A KPI that made sense a year ago may be encouraging the wrong behavior today.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the single most important KPI in B2B sales?

There is no single "most important" KPI, but because it combines several metrics into one formula, **sales velocity (pipeline velocity)** is one of the most comprehensive indicators. It evaluates the number of opportunities, deal value, win rate and sales cycle together. This lets you see the overall health of your revenue engine in a single number.

### How often should sales KPIs be reviewed?

Activity and pipeline metrics should be reviewed weekly, conversion rates monthly, and strategic metrics such as revenue and CAC quarterly. Reviewing the KPI set itself at the end of each quarter is also good practice for adapting to changing market conditions.

### Should a small sales team track KPIs too?

Yes. In fact, KPI tracking is even more critical for small teams, because every lost opportunity has a noticeable impact on revenue. For a team of two or three people, starting with 4-5 core metrics such as weekly meetings, open pipeline value, win rate and average sales cycle is enough.

### How should KPI targets be set?

Targets should be set by working backward from your revenue target and based on at least 4-6 weeks of real performance data. When you use your historical conversion rates to translate your revenue target into the required number of opportunities, calls and activities, you get targets that are both realistic and measurable.

## Conclusion

The right B2B sales KPIs make visible what your team is doing and why, transforming intuition-based management into data-driven management. The secret to a successful KPI system isn't tracking a large number of metrics. It's building a small set that balances leading and lagging indicators, is role-specific, clearly defined and tracked automatically through your CRM. Work backward from your revenue target, measure your current state, choose KPIs by role and track them on a weekly cadence. Automation and AI, in turn, keep this system sustainable.

In B2B influencer collaborations, every post made in exchange for money, products, licenses, commissions or any other benefit **must be clearly labeled as "Reklam" (Advertisement) or "İşbirliği" (Collaboration)**. In Turkey, this requirement was clarified by the **Guidelines on Social Media Influencers**, published in 2021 by the Ministry of Trade's Advertising Board. A "professional" target audience does not exempt you from this rule: a SaaS promotion on LinkedIn must be just as transparent as a cosmetics post on Instagram. The label must appear at the beginning of the content, in Turkish, and be clear and easy to see.

Many B2B companies still view influencer marketing as "thought leadership" or "a friendly recommendation," and often dismiss disclosure as a problem for consumer brands. Yet a LinkedIn post in which an industry expert praises your software in return for a fee carries a legal risk of **covert advertising** if it is published without a label. In this guide, we walk you step by step through which collaborations count as advertising, how and where to place the label, who is held liable, and which clauses to add to your contracts.

> **Note:** This article is for general information purposes only and does not constitute legal advice. Administrative fine amounts and the relevant regulations are updated regularly. We recommend confirming the current legislation with a legal advisor before launching a campaign.

## Which rules govern influencer advertising in Turkey?

Influencer advertising in Turkey is not governed by a single law but by several complementary regulations. Here is the core framework B2B marketers need to know:

- **Consumer Protection Law No. 6502:** Requires commercial advertising to be honest, accurate and distinguishable. It prohibits covert advertising.
- **Regulation on Commercial Advertising and Unfair Commercial Practices:** Specifies that an advertisement must be recognizable as such and details the scope of covert advertising.
- **Guidelines on Commercial Advertising and Unfair Commercial Practices by Social Media Influencers (2021):** The Advertising Board's social-media-specific interpretation. It sets out in concrete terms which wording to use, where to place the label and when it is mandatory.
- **Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102, unfair competition provisions:** Particularly important for B2B. Even if the audience is not made up of consumers, gaining an advantage over competitors through misleading or concealed advertising may constitute unfair competition. This gives rival companies the right to file claims for damages and injunctions.
- **Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 (KVKK):** If you collect leads through an influencer as part of a campaign (forms, webinar registrations, e-book downloads), personal data processing rules apply.

In short, the defense "Our audience is companies, not consumers" fails to protect you on two counts. First, content on LinkedIn and Instagram is public and reaches consumers too. Second, on the B2B side there is an additional risk of unfair competition.

## Which B2B collaborations count as "advertising"?

The Guidelines' key criterion is the existence of a **commercial relationship** (a tangible or intangible benefit). The absence of payment does not automatically exclude a post from being advertising. Common B2B scenarios can be classified as follows:

### Must always be labeled

- **Paid posts or videos:** You paid a sales trainer for a LinkedIn carousel explaining your CRM software.
- **Free software licenses or premium accounts:** You gave a marketing consultant a free 12-month enterprise license, and they praised your tool. Gifted products and services are also benefits.
- **Affiliate and commission models:** Models in which the influencer earns a share of every sale made through a dedicated link or discount code they share.
- **Paid webinar or podcast appearances:** You sponsored a show hosted by the influencer, and your product is mentioned in the content.
- **Event invitations:** Glowing posts about an event by an industry expert whose accommodation, travel and attendance fees you covered.
- **Brand ambassadorship agreements:** Long-term contracts involving regular posts. Under this model, every product-related post must be labeled for the duration of the contract.

### Gray areas

- **Employee advocacy:** Your sales director praising their own company's product is generally not a problem if the relationship is clear from their profile. However, if the company pays employees a bonus per post or distributes ready-made copy, explicitly disclosing the relationship is the safest approach.
- **Content swaps:** "You come on my podcast, I'll come on your webinar" arrangements also involve mutual benefit. If a product is promoted, a label is required.
- **Influencers who are existing customers:** If the person has paid for and used your product for years and praises it of their own accord, this is not advertising. But if you offered them a fee or discount after that post, subsequent posts fall within a commercial relationship.

### No label required

- Independent reviews made on the influencer's own initiative, with no commercial relationship whatsoever.
- General industry content that does not mention your product or brand at all. For example, a presentation at an event you sponsored that makes no reference to you.

The practical rule is this: if the answer to **"Would this person have posted the same thing, in the same way, if they had no relationship with us?"** is "I'm not sure," label it.

## How and where should the ad label be placed?

The most concrete part of the Guidelines covers the format of the label. The goal is for the average follower to recognize the content as advertising **at first glance**.

### Acceptable wording

The Guidelines accept clear expressions such as:

- **#reklam** or **Reklam** (Advertisement)
- **#işbirliği** or **İşbirliği** (Collaboration)
- Expressions that explicitly name the brand, such as "**[Marka adı] ile işbirliği**" (Collaboration with [Brand name]) or "**[Marka adı] reklamıdır**" ([Brand name] advertisement)
- For gifted products or services, statements such as "**[Marka] tarafından hediye edilmiştir**" (Gifted by [Brand])

Vague abbreviations or foreign-language terms are not considered sufficient on their own. Tags such as **#ad, #spon, #sp, #collab, #partner** are not clear enough for a Turkish-speaking audience. Ambiguous phrases like "Thanks @brand" also fail to disclose the advertising relationship.

### Placement by platform

**LinkedIn posts:** The label must appear in the first line of the post, before the "...see more" cut-off. Because LinkedIn collapses long text, a #reklam tag added at the end of the paragraphs will most likely go unread. A correct example looks like this:

> **Reklam | [Marka] işbirliği** (Ad | Collaboration with [Brand]) – Here's how my team and I streamlined pipeline management over the past three months...

**LinkedIn carousels and documents:** In addition to the label in the caption, it is good practice to add a visible "Reklam" notice on the first slide.

**Instagram posts and Reels:** The platform's "Paid partnership" label can and should be used. However, this label alone may not be enough. The caption should also begin with "#reklam" or "#işbirliği," and Reels videos should include legible on-screen text.

**Instagram Stories:** Each story frame is treated as a separate piece of content. If the label appears only on the first frame of a five-frame promotional series, someone who swipes in and starts watching from the middle will not see it. That is why a visible label must appear on **every frame**.

**YouTube and video podcasts:** The platform's "Includes paid promotion" feature should be enabled, a verbal disclosure should be made at the start of the video, and the label should be added at the beginning of the description. In long videos where the sponsored segment falls in the middle, a verbal or written reminder at the start of that segment is also recommended.

**Newsletters and blog posts:** The statement "Bu içerik [Marka] ile işbirliği kapsamında hazırlanmıştır" (This content was produced in collaboration with [Brand]) should appear at the very top of the sponsored section or article.

### 5 checks for label visibility

1. **Is it up front?** Is the label visible in the first line or the first few seconds?
2. **Is it separate?** Is it not buried among 15 other hashtags?
3. **Is it legible?** In Stories, is it large enough and in sufficient contrast with the background?
4. **Is it in Turkish?** Is it in a language the target audience understands?
5. **Is it on screen long enough?** In videos, does the label stay on screen for a sufficient length of time?

## Who is held liable for an unlabeled post?

The chain of liability in advertising law is broad, and this is the point B2B companies most often overlook. According to the Guidelines, liability does not rest with the influencer alone:

- **The advertiser (you):** You bear primary responsibility for the advertisement's compliance with the law. The defense "The influencer forgot to add the label" does not automatically protect you.
- **The advertising or influencer agency:** Liable as the party that designs and manages the campaign.
- **The influencer, as a media outlet:** Liable for advertising published on their own account.

When the Advertising Board finds a violation, it may order **suspension of the advertisement**, impose an **administrative fine** and, where necessary, issue an **interim suspension order**. Fine amounts are updated annually in line with the revaluation rate and vary according to the type of media. Another consequence as significant as the fine is **reputational damage**. Advertising Board decisions are made public, and in the B2B sales cycle, trust is everything. A buying committee finding a "covert advertising" ruling against you on Google can delay a deal that is about to close.

## Does labeling hurt conversion in B2B?

This is the question marketing teams ask most often. The concern is understandable: there is a fear that writing "Ad" will make the content lose its authenticity. But B2B dynamics work differently:

- **B2B buyers already know.** Executives who make purchasing decisions are aware that well-known names in their industry work with brands. The absence of a label raises suspicion, while its presence conveys professionalism.
- **Trust comes from content quality.** A labeled but genuinely instructive case story generates more qualified leads than unlabeled, superficial praise.
- **The risk of later exposure is more costly.** When an unlabeled collaboration is exposed by a competitor or follower, both the influencer's and the brand's credibility suffer.

## Which clauses should be added to the contract?

Because liability is shared, the influencer contract is your first line of defense. Verbal agreements or collaborations run solely through DMs are risky from both a legal and an operational standpoint. A B2B influencer contract should include at least the following:

1. **Labeling obligation:** It should state explicitly which wording (e.g., "Reklam | [Marka] işbirliği"), at which point in the content, and with which platform tools (e.g., Instagram "Paid partnership") will be used.
2. **Pre-approval process:** Content must be sent to the brand before publication for a label check. A concrete timeframe can be set, such as "draft shared at least 2 business days before publication."
3. **Accuracy commitment:** The influencer must have genuinely used the product and must not make untested claims. If a statement such as "It tripled our sales" is to be used, it must be documentable.
4. **List of prohibited claims:** Competitor comparisons, guarantee statements, and unprovable superiority claims such as "Turkey's best."
5. **Right to correction and removal:** If the label is published incompletely, the influencer is obliged to correct it within a set period (e.g., 24 hours).
6. **Penalty clause and recourse:** A provision allowing the brand to seek recourse against the influencer for any fine imposed on the brand due to the influencer's failure to follow the labeling rules.
7. **KVKK and data processing:** If the influencer collects forms or registrations through their own channel, the contract should specify who the data controller is, where the privacy notice will appear and how the data will be transferred.
8. **Content usage rights:** If you will use the content on your own channels, in ads or in sales presentations, the duration, media and scope should be specified. The advertising nature must also be preserved in repurposed content.
9. **Post-contract posts:** The contract should state what will happen to past content after the collaboration ends, and that labels on past content must not be removed.

## What should you watch for under KVKK when collecting leads through influencers?

A significant share of B2B influencer campaigns directly target lead generation: free e-books, joint webinars, demo requests or exclusive discount codes. At this point, the ad label alone is not enough; the data side must also be compliant:

- **Privacy notice:** The form page should clearly state who the data controller is (you, the influencer or both).
- **Explicit consent and commercial message approval:** If you will send marketing content to the email addresses collected, you must obtain separate consent under the commercial electronic messaging legislation and fulfill the related registration obligations.
- **Data transfer:** If the influencer collects data with their own tool (e.g., their own form tool) and transfers it to you, the legal basis and security of this transfer should be defined in the contract.
- **Purpose limitation:** Before adding data collected for a "webinar registration" to a cold sales sequence, make sure the scope permits it.

## Step by step: How to set up a compliant B2B influencer campaign

The workflow below is based on a hypothetical scenario in which a mid-sized SaaS company runs a three-month collaboration with a sales trainer. The plan includes 3 LinkedIn posts, 1 joint webinar and 6 Instagram stories.

### 1. Define the commercial relationship

Is there a payment, a license or a commission? Put everything in writing. In this scenario there are two separate benefits: a fixed fee and a free 12-month license. Both make labeling mandatory.

### 2. Create a labeling plan by platform

Prepare a simple table:

- **LinkedIn posts (3):** "Reklam | [Marka] işbirliği" in the first line
- **Webinar (1):** On the registration page, on the opening slide and in the host's verbal disclosure in the first minute
- **Instagram stories (6 frames):** "#reklam" and the platform's paid partnership label on every frame

### 3. Add labeling rules to the brief

Open a "Legal requirements" section on the first page of the creative brief. Most influencers skim briefs quickly. If you put the rules on the last page, they may be overlooked.

### 4. Apply a pre-approval checklist

Run the 5-point visibility check above for every piece of content. Keep a record of the approval process in email or your project management tool. This is the easiest way to prove you "exercised due diligence" in the event of an investigation.

### 5. Post-publication audit

Check every piece of content live within 24 hours of publication and archive a screenshot. Influencers sometimes edit captions after posting. Running another random check after the first week is good practice.

### 6. Measurement and CRM logging

Tag campaign leads in your CRM with a consistent source value such as "Influencer – [name] – [campaign]." This structure both simplifies ROI analysis and lets you instantly answer the question "Where did this data come from?" under KVKK.

### 7. Automated follow-up

If your team uses an automation tool such as n8n, you can connect influencer content publication dates to a calendar and automatically create a "Run label check in 24 hours" reminder. In multi-brand setups working with several influencers at once, this small automation significantly reduces oversights caused by manual tracking.

## What are the most common labeling mistakes?

The mistakes most often seen in practice stem from carelessness rather than a lack of technical knowledge:

- **Burying the label at the end of a hashtag pile:** A sequence like "#sales #b2b #crm #pipeline #leadership ... #reklam" effectively makes the label invisible.
- **Relying only on the platform label:** Instagram's "Paid partnership" label is useful but appears small on mobile. It should be backed by an in-text label.
- **Labeling only the first frame of a story series:** Each frame is treated as independent content.
- **Treating a gifted license as "not advertising":** A free SaaS license is the most common form of covert benefit in B2B.
- **Forgetting old content:** Removing labels or editing content once a collaboration ends can create a retroactive violation.
- **Using English abbreviations:** #ad or #sponsored is not clear enough for a Turkish-speaking audience.
- **Concealing the relationship in employee posts:** An employee whose profile shows no company information praising the product as if they were an "independent user" is misleading.

## How do you build labeling rules into your strategy?

Compliance should not remain a checklist item added at the end of a campaign; it should become part of your strategy. Here are three practical recommendations:

- **Review compliance history when selecting influencers:** Examine a candidate's collaborations over the last 6 months. Someone who labels consistently and correctly has a professional work discipline.
- **Turn transparency into a content format:** An opening such as "I've been working with [Brand] for 3 months, and here's my real experience" makes the label part of a natural narrative.
- **Train your internal teams:** Marketing, sales and social media teams should all know the same rules. In particular, when the sales team encourages LinkedIn testimonials from customers, it must be careful about whether any benefit is being offered.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are B2B posts on LinkedIn also subject to ad labeling rules?

Yes. The Advertising Board's Guidelines do not distinguish between platforms. If an influencer promotes a product or service in exchange for a fee, a free license, a commission or any other benefit from a brand, a label is mandatory on LinkedIn as well. The label must appear in the first line, before the collapsed part of the text.

### We only provided a free software license. Do we still need to say "advertisement"?

Yes. Free products or services are considered part of a commercial relationship and a benefit. In this case, a clear expression such as "#reklam," "#işbirliği" or "[Marka] tarafından hediye edilmiştir" (Gifted by [Brand]) should be used.

### If the influencer doesn't add a label, who gets fined?

Liability is shared. The advertising brand, the agency (if any) and the influencer can each be held liable. That is why adding a labeling obligation, a pre-approval process and a recourse clause to the contract is the most effective way for a brand to protect itself.

### Are English tags like #ad or #sponsored sufficient?

For content aimed at audiences in Turkey, they are generally not considered sufficient. The Guidelines are based on Turkish expressions that the average follower can easily understand. The safe choice is to use "Reklam" or "İşbirliği," adding the brand name where necessary.

## Conclusion

In B2B influencer collaborations, the ad label is not an obligation that weakens a campaign's impact; it is a prerequisite for trust. If there is a commercial relationship, a label is required. The label must be up front, in Turkish and clear. Liability lies not only with the influencer, but with you. When you embed these three principles into your contracts, briefs and post-publication checks, you both reduce legal risk and become a more trustworthy brand in the eyes of the executives who make purchasing decisions.

For most B2B SaaS companies, the answer is **yes**: your pricing page should show at least starting prices or a clear price range. Hiding pricing entirely only makes sense for high-value enterprise deals where the scope is built from scratch for each customer. For many companies, the healthiest approach is a hybrid model: transparent pricing on the lower and mid-tier plans, plus a "Talk to sales" option for the enterprise plan.

In this article, we won't settle for a single right answer. We'll look at how showing or hiding prices affects your sales funnel, lead quality, sales team efficiency and brand perception. Then we'll wrap up with a framework to help you decide based on your own situation, a step-by-step pricing page structure and some considerations specific to the Turkish market.

## Why does the pricing page debate never seem to end?

In B2B SaaS, the pricing page is one of the most visited pages on your site and one of the clearest signals of purchase intent. Someone reading a product page is curious. Someone landing on the pricing page is most likely working out a budget, building a comparison table to present to their team, or weighing you against competitors.

The debate stems from tension between two different goals:

- **The marketing team** wants to reduce friction. A visitor who sees the price either rules themselves out or gets convinced, and either way, time is saved.
- **The sales team** wants to protect the opportunity for a conversation. A prospect who sees the price too early may decide it's "too expensive" and leave before sales has a chance to explain the value.

Both sides have a point. The right answer depends on your product, target customer segment, average contract value and sales motion. So the real question isn't so much "Should we show pricing?" as "Which price should we show, to which customer, and in how much detail?"

## What are the benefits of showing prices openly?

### The buyer journey now takes shape before any sales conversation

B2B buyers do most of their research on their own before speaking with a sales rep. They browse websites, read comparison content, ask colleagues and check review platforms. A buyer who can't find pricing information during this process either moves on to a competitor or leaves you off the shortlist.

Let's take a concrete scenario. An operations manager is looking for work management software for a team of 20. They open the pricing pages of three vendors. Two clearly list a monthly per-user price; yours says "Get a quote." While building the comparison table for their manager, they leave your row blank. Chances are, that row gets dropped from the list at the next meeting.

### Lead quality improves and your sales team's time is protected

Someone who sees the price and still requests a demo has already passed the budget filter. This cuts down on opportunities where sales can't get an answer to "What's your budget?", drag on for months, and ultimately close lost due to a budget mismatch.

Here's a simple calculation: if your sales team runs 60 demos a month and a third of them go nowhere because of budget misalignment, 20 demos a month are wasted. If each demo takes an average of 2 hours including prep and follow-up, that's roughly 40 hours lost per month, equal to a full work week for one sales rep. Pricing transparency won't eliminate this loss entirely, but it will reduce it significantly.

This is also a good time to revisit your lead definitions. Someone who visits the pricing page, reviews multiple plans and then fills out a form sends a very different intent signal than someone who just downloads an e-book. When clarifying [the difference between MQL and SQL](/blog/what-is-mql-mql-vs-sql-b2b-saas), make sure to include pricing page engagement in your scoring model.

### Trust and brand perception get stronger

When a company hides its pricing, buyers tend to think one of two things: "It's probably very expensive" or "They charge every customer differently, so I'll have to negotiate." Both erode trust. This is especially true in the SMB segment, where decision-makers have limited time and patience for sales calls.

A company that shows its pricing openly sends the message: "We're confident in our value and have nothing to hide." This approach is one of the most visible steps in [building ethical, transparent trust in B2B SaaS sales](https://saascorner.co/blog/b2b-saas-satista-guven-i-nsasi-etik-ve-seffaf-stratejiler).

### SEO and comparison visibility increase

Searches like "[product category] pricing" or "[competitor name] alternative pricing" carry high purchase intent. A page without pricing struggles to compete for these searches. On top of that, AI assistants and comparison sites pull pricing information from public sources. If your pricing isn't available, you either won't be mentioned at all or you'll be mentioned with incorrect or outdated information.

## When does it make sense to hide pricing?

Not showing pricing isn't always a mistake. Hiding it, or sharing only "starting at" information, can be a smart move under the following conditions:

1. **Your average annual contract value is high.** If your annual deals are in the tens or even hundreds of thousands of dollars and every deal goes through a multi-stakeholder buying process, a fixed price list won't reflect reality anyway.
2. **Pricing depends heavily on customer-specific variables.** If the number of integrations, data volume, implementation scope, custom development and SLA levels significantly change the price, a single number will be misleading.
3. **You're still in the price discovery phase.** If you're an early-stage startup looking for the right price point with your first 10–20 customers, committing to a public number reduces your flexibility.
4. **Competition is driven by price.** If you're in a commoditized market where competitors will see your price and immediately undercut it, not showing pricing may offer short-term protection. But this isn't a long-term strategy. The real solution is differentiation.
5. **You're doing account-based selling.** If you're targeting a specific list of enterprise accounts and offering a tailored value proposition to each one, the pricing page isn't central to your sales process anyway. In this kind of approach, the website's role isn't first contact but supporting a relationship that started with outbound.

The last point is especially important. With enterprise targets, demand usually comes not from your website but from proactive outreach to the right accounts at the right time. In this model, [data-driven B2B sales and lead generation](https://salescorner.co) processes play a far more decisive role than the pricing page.

### The real cost of hiding pricing

If you decide to hide pricing, go in knowing the price you'll pay:

- You'll lose some self-directed buyers without ever seeing them.
- Some people will fill out the form just to find out the price, filling your sales team's calendar with low-intent meetings.
- Your sales cycle gets longer, because the pricing conversation slips to the second or third meeting.
- Your visibility in comparison tables and AI summaries decreases.

## The hybrid model: The most balanced option for most B2B SaaS companies

In practice, the structure that works most often is this: clear pricing on the lower and mid-tier plans, and "Contact us" on the top plan. Here's an example setup:

- **Starter:** $15 per user per month, up to 10 users, core features.
- **Professional:** $35 per user per month, advanced reporting, integrations, priority support.
- **Enterprise:** "Custom pricing." SSO, custom SLA, dedicated customer success manager, advanced security and compliance.

(The figures are examples only, provided to illustrate the structure.)

This structure has three benefits:

1. **SMB buyers** can make decisions on their own, and can even sign up directly.
2. **Mid-market buyers** see the price range and can work out their budget.
3. **Enterprise buyers** understand that there's a tier above the standard plans. And your sales team retains the opportunity to have value-focused conversations with these accounts.

### Middle-ground options within the hybrid model

There are other options between full transparency and full secrecy:

- **Showing a "starting at" price:** A phrase like "Plans starting at $500 per month" shows buyers the floor but leaves the details to sales.
- **Pricing calculator:** Visitors select the number of users, modules and volume to get an estimated price. This tool both provides transparency and serves as a strong intent signal. A visitor who uses the calculator and wants the result sent by email is an extremely qualified lead.
- **Price range:** A range like "$10,000–$40,000 per year" helps buyers position themselves, even for enterprise products.

## Which model is right for you? A five-question decision framework

Work through the following five questions with your team when making the decision.

### 1. What is your average contract value?

As a general rule, the lower your annual contract value, the more essential transparency becomes. Organizing a sales call for a product that costs a few hundred dollars a month isn't economically sustainable. The cost of sale may exceed the revenue the customer brings in during the first year. For high-value enterprise deals, on the other hand, sales involvement is both necessary and pays for itself.

### 2. Is your sales motion product-led or sales-led?

If you have a product-led model that grows through free trials or freemium, hiding pricing is inconsistent. If users are trying the product on their own, they should be able to take the payment step on their own too. If you have a demo-led model, you have more flexibility. We covered the differences between these two approaches in detail in [our article on free trial vs. demo](/blog/free-trial-vs-demo-b2b-saas). Your pricing page decision should align with this choice.

### 3. What are your competitors doing?

If most competitors in your category show their pricing, not showing yours automatically puts you at a disadvantage. If everyone hides it, being the first company to show pricing openly can be a significant differentiation opportunity.

### 4. How standardized is your pricing?

If the vast majority of your customers fit into one of three or four standard plans, there's no strong reason not to show pricing. If nearly every deal is built from scratch, consider simplifying your pricing first. This complexity is often a symptom of a positioning problem. It's hard to resolve the pricing page debate without first clarifying your [B2B SaaS product positioning](/blog/b2b-saas-product-positioning).

### 5. What is your sales team's capacity?

If your sales team is small and calendars are full, pricing transparency protects the team's focus by filtering out low-intent requests. If the team is large and has spare capacity, hiding pricing in the enterprise segment to generate more conversations may make more sense.

**Practical takeaway:** If at least three of your five answers point toward "transparency," move to at least a hybrid model.

## How do you build an effective B2B SaaS pricing page?

The real work begins once you've decided to show pricing. A poorly structured pricing page can do even more harm than not showing pricing at all. Let's go step by step.

1. **Limit the number of plans.** Three or four plans is ideal. Any more creates choice overload. Describe who each plan is for in one sentence, such as "For sales teams of 5–20 people."
2. **Choose the right pricing metric.** Per user, usage-based, module-based or flat fee. The metric should grow with the value the customer gets from the product. If user count doesn't reflect value, per-user pricing will hold back growth.
3. **Highlight one plan.** Spotlighting the middle plan with a "Most popular" label gives undecided visitors a reference point.
4. **Show monthly and annual options.** If you offer a discount for annual payment (for example, two months free), display it with a clear toggle. It's good for your cash flow, too.
5. **Add a feature comparison table.** A detailed table below the plans makes life easier for whoever is doing the technical evaluation. Use outcome-focused language instead of jargon.
6. **Design a different call to action for each plan.** "Try for free" on Starter, "Start a trial or get a demo" on Professional, and "Talk to sales" on Enterprise.
7. **Address objections on the page.** Add a short FAQ section at the bottom of the pricing page: "Can I switch plans?", "Is there a contract term?", "Is there a setup fee?", "Which currency are invoices issued in?"
8. **Add social proof.** Customer logos, short quotes or links to relevant case studies make the value behind the price tangible.
9. **Don't leave hidden costs.** If there are setup fees, training fees, add-on modules or overage charges, state them clearly. Costs that surface later destroy trust in an instant.

### How should leads from the pricing page be handed off to sales?

A request from the pricing page is one of the hottest leads on your site and should be treated accordingly. Set a separate response time for these requests. For example, you might set a goal of responding to enterprise plan inquiries within the same business day. In your CRM, tag the lead source as "pricing page" and record which plan they viewed. Creating a [lead handoff SLA between marketing and sales](/blog/lead-handoff-sla-marketing-sales-alignment) is a good starting point for putting the rules of this process in writing.

## What should you watch out for on pricing pages in the Turkish market?

Companies selling B2B SaaS in Türkiye face some specific situations.

### Currency: TRY, USD or EUR?

Exchange rate volatility is the biggest pricing page headache for SaaS companies in Türkiye. Here are the options:

- **Foreign currency pricing, invoiced in TRY:** Showing prices in USD or EUR and converting to TRY at the exchange rate on the invoice date is a common choice for companies whose costs are in foreign currency. However, it creates uncertainty for SMB customers who plan their budgets in TRY.
- **TRY pricing with regular updates:** Showing prices in TRY and updating them at set intervals is easier for customers to understand. You do, however, need to state your update policy transparently on the page.
- **Annual TRY price lock:** Locking the price in TRY for annual upfront payments gives customers predictability and gives you cash.

Whichever route you choose, the page should include a clear explanation, such as: "Prices are in USD and are invoiced in TRY at the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye's foreign exchange selling rate on the invoice date."

### VAT and tax display

B2B buyers usually evaluate prices excluding VAT. Even so, clearly write "excl. VAT" next to every price. Confusion like "Did this figure include VAT?" during budget approval needlessly drags out the purchasing process.

### Separate local and global pages

If you sell both in Türkiye and abroad, you might consider two separate pricing pages or a display that varies by region. In that case, though, pay attention to consistency. Any price difference for the same product between the two markets should be based on a sound rationale.

## How do you measure pricing page performance?

A pricing page isn't something you build once and forget. Here are the key metrics you should track:

- **Pricing page visit rate:** What share of total site visitors reach the pricing page? If this rate is very low, visitors are either coming from the wrong audience or the page is hard to reach.
- **Pricing page conversion rate:** What share of people who see the page start a trial, request a demo or make a purchase?
- **Plan distribution:** Which plan is demand concentrated in? If everyone chooses the lowest plan, the middle plan's value proposition may be weak.
- **Sales cycle length:** Do opportunities coming through the pricing page close faster than those from other sources?
- **Budget-related loss rate:** How does the share of opportunities lost due to "price/budget" in your CRM change over time?

### Testing price display

Make the decision to show pricing based on data, not assumptions. A simple approach could look like this:

1. Collect 2–3 months of data on your current state: number of demo requests, qualified opportunity rate, close rate and average sales cycle.
2. Switch to the hybrid model or start showing a price range.
3. Track the same metrics over the same period.
4. Look not just at the number of requests but at **closed revenue**. Demo requests may drop after you show pricing, but if close rates and sales cycles improve, the overall result is positive.

The most common mistake here is seeing the drop in demos and panicking back to the old setup. Fewer but more qualified conversations usually mean more revenue.

## The most common pricing page mistakes

- **Hiding everything behind a "Get a quote" button:** For an SMB-focused product, this approach causes you to lose a significant share of buyers.
- **Too many plans and feature rows:** Six plans and an 80-row feature table don't make deciding easier; they make it harder.
- **Illogical jumps between plans:** If the Starter plan is $20 and the next one is $400, the customer segment in between is left out.
- **Copying your pricing page from competitors:** A competitor's metric and plan structure may not fit your value proposition.
- **An outdated page:** If prices have changed but old information remains on the page or in search results, sales conversations start with an argument.
- **Not communicating value alongside price:** A page that only lists numbers and features pushes buyers toward price comparison. Explain which problem each plan solves and what outcome it delivers.
- **Leaving the sales team out of the process:** The pricing page isn't a decision the marketing team should make alone. Objections the sales team hears in the field should be reflected in how the page is structured.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does showing pricing reduce the number of leads in B2B SaaS?

In the short term, form submissions or demo requests may drop slightly. But because incoming requests have already passed the budget filter, the qualified opportunity rate and close rate usually improve. Success should be measured by closed revenue and sales cycle length, not raw lead count.

### Should I show the price of the enterprise plan too?

Since the enterprise plan usually involves customer-specific scope, you can use "Custom pricing" instead of a fixed price. Even so, giving a floor such as "starting at X per year" or a price range helps buyers position themselves. This approach also preserves your sales team's opportunity for a value-focused conversation.

### What if competitors see my price and undercut it?

A product whose only differentiator is price will get dragged into a price war whether its pricing is hidden or not. Competitors already have plenty of ways to find out your pricing. Real protection comes not from hiding your price but from clear positioning, strong case studies and the measurable value your product delivers.

### Should I show prices in TRY or foreign currency in Türkiye?

If your costs are largely in foreign currency, showing prices in foreign currency and invoicing in TRY protects you from exchange rate risk. SMB customers, however, plan their budgets in TRY, so TRY pricing with an annual price lock option is more attractive to them. Whichever route you choose, state your exchange rate and VAT policy clearly on the page.

## Conclusion

Whether to show prices on your B2B SaaS pricing page is more than a marketing choice. This decision directly affects your sales motion, lead quality, sales team efficiency and the trust people place in your brand. For most companies, the best starting point is the hybrid model: transparent pricing on the lower and mid-tier plans, and value-focused sales conversations in the enterprise segment. Make the decision based on data, not assumptions. Test your pricing page regularly and reflect the objections your sales team brings back from the field.

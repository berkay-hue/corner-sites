**For B2B SaaS companies selling to SMEs, local businesses and industrial firms in Turkey, finding B2B leads on Google Maps is a low-cost, highly targeted prospecting method.** The process has five steps. First, translate your target customer profile into Maps categories. Then search by province and district and collect each business's name, phone number, website, address and review data. Next, clean the list and enrich it with decision-maker information. Finally, run personalized outreach by phone and email. It's the most practical way to reach thousands of Turkish businesses that have no LinkedIn presence but are almost always listed on Google.

In Turkey, a dental clinic, auto repair shop, boutique hotel, CNC workshop or wholesale food company is most likely not active on LinkedIn. It also often shows up incomplete in traditional B2B databases. But it has almost certainly set up a Google Business Profile so its customers can find it. In this post, we walk you step by step through turning Google Maps from a scattered search tool into a systematic lead source, taking Turkey's legal and practical conditions into account.

## Why is Google Maps a powerful B2B lead source in Turkey?

SMEs and small, owner-operated businesses make up a large share of the Turkish economy. Their digital footprint usually comes down to three things: a Google Business Profile, an Instagram account and, though not always, a simple website. Enterprise B2B databases, on the other hand, mainly cover companies that are visible on LinkedIn and above a certain headcount.

This gap gives Google Maps three concrete advantages:

- **Coverage:** Small and mid-sized businesses missing from traditional data tools are listed on Maps. No other source gives you such structured access to the hundreds of manufacturers in an organized industrial zone.
- **Freshness:** Businesses that shut down are usually marked "permanently closed," and new ones are added quickly. Review dates also show whether a business is still active.
- **Qualification signals:** Review count, rating, number of photos, opening hours, whether there's a website and the number of locations all hint at a business's size and digital maturity. You won't find these signals together in any other free source.

Of course, Maps isn't a sufficient channel on its own. To see the strengths and weaknesses of different channels, check out our [efficiency comparison of B2B lead generation channels](/blog/b2b-lead-generation-channels-compared). Still, for a SaaS company with the right ICP, Maps can be the main fuel for its outbound engine.

## Which B2B SaaS companies is this method right for?

Google Maps doesn't deliver the same results for every B2B SaaS company. The method works very well if your target customers operate from a physical location. If your target is "CTOs of fintech companies with 500+ employees," Maps won't help you much.

Examples of high-fit scenarios:

- **Appointment and clinic management software:** dental clinics, aesthetic centers, physiotherapy centers, veterinary clinics, beauty salons.
- **Restaurant and café technology:** QR menus, POS, inventory and procurement software.
- **Hotel and hospitality software:** boutique hotels, guesthouses, apart-hotels.
- **Field service and fleet management:** auto repair shops, air conditioning and boiler servicing companies, logistics firms.
- **Manufacturing and ERP solutions:** machinery, plastics, textile and metalworking manufacturers in organized industrial zones (OSBs).
- **Real estate and construction software:** real estate agencies, contractors, building inspection companies.
- **Educational institutions:** tutoring centers, language schools, driving schools.

Here's a rough rule of thumb: **If your target customer's customers search for them on Google, you can find them on Google Maps.**

## Step 1: Translate your ICP into Google Maps terms

Most teams start by opening Maps, typing "restaurant" and drowning in thousands of irrelevant results. The right starting point is to convert your ideal customer profile (ICP) into variables Maps understands.

A typical ICP definition looks like this: "Private dental clinics in Istanbul, Ankara and Izmir with more than two dentists and high monthly patient traffic."

Here's the Maps equivalent:

- **Category/keyword:** "dental clinic," "oral and dental health polyclinic," "implant center," "orthodontic clinic"
- **Geography:** 3 provinces, broken down by district
- **Size signal:** review count (e.g., 50+), multiple locations, having a website
- **Activity signal:** received reviews in the last 3 months
- **Exclusions:** public hospitals, (public) oral and dental health centers, permanently closed businesses

Putting this table in writing saves hours during data collection. It also means there's no need to debate later which records made the list and which were excluded.

## Step 2: Build a keyword and category matrix

In Turkey, businesses register themselves on Google under very different names. You might find the same type of business listed as "auto service," "auto repair," "independent garage," "auto electrics" or "body and paint." Searching with a single keyword means missing a significant part of your potential list.

Here's a practical method:

1. Search for the main category on Maps and note the Google category of the first 30–40 results.
2. Look at your competitors' customer references to see how these businesses describe themselves.
3. Add Turkish spelling variations. For example, include common typos such as "poliklinik" vs. "polikinlik," or closely related categories such as "tekstil" (textiles) and "konfeksiyon" (apparel manufacturing).
4. Try English equivalents too. Businesses in tourist regions in particular may be registered under English categories.

At this stage, asking a language model like ChatGPT for a list of synonymous categories works quite well. We covered this in more detail in our post on [ways to find B2B leads with ChatGPT](/blog/chatgpt-b2b-lead-generation-turkey).

For a SaaS company selling industrial ERP, a sample matrix might look like this:

| Main segment | Keywords |
|---|---|
| Metalworking | CNC machining, precision machining, sheet metal, laser cutting |
| Plastics | plastic injection molding, mold making, plastic packaging |
| Textiles | apparel manufacturing, knitted fabric, dyehouse, knitwear |

This table has 3 segments and 11 keywords. Combined with the right geographic breakdown, it yields a universe of thousands of candidate businesses.

## Step 3: Split the geography into small pieces

Google Maps shows a limited number of results per search. When you search "Istanbul dental clinic," you don't see every clinic in the city, just a small subset that Google highlights. The way to expand coverage is to split your searches geographically.

- **In large cities, search by district or even neighborhood.** Istanbul has 39 districts. Searches like "Kadıköy dental clinic" or "Ataşehir dental clinic" give you much broader coverage.
- **For industry-focused ICPs, target OSBs and industrial estates.** Areas like "İkitelli OSB," "Dudullu OSB," "Gebze OSB," "Bursa Nilüfer OSB," "Manisa OSB" and "Kayseri OSB" each host hundreds of manufacturers.
- **For tourism-focused ICPs, drill down to district and town level.** In Muğla, for example, search Bodrum, Fethiye, Marmaris and Datça separately.

Let's do a quick calculation: searching 4 keywords across Istanbul's 39 districts gives you 156 search combinations. Even if each combination returns only a few dozen unique results on average, after deduplication you'll have a substantial list of thousands of records.

## Step 4: Collect the data: manually, with a tool or via API

There are three main ways to collect the data. Which one you choose depends on your list volume and technical resources.

### Manual collection

If 50–100 records per week is enough, an SDR or intern can search Maps and copy the information into a spreadsheet. The fields to collect are:

- Business name
- Google category
- Address, district and province
- Phone
- Website
- Rating and review count
- Date of latest review
- Google Maps link

The manual method is slow, but data quality is high. Because the SDR looks at every record, qualification happens at the same time. It makes sense to start with this method when testing a new segment.

### Google Places API

If you need scale, the most robust and compliant route is Google's official Places API. You can use text search to list businesses in a given area, then use a details request to pull fields like phone number, website and opening hours. If you have a technical team, a simple script can be ready in a few days. If you don't, you can connect the API to a spreadsheet using no-code automation tools. Our post on [automating your lead generation operation](https://saascorner.co/blog/b2bde-uykusuz-gecelerin-ciktisi-lead-generation-operasyonunu-otomatige-baglamak) is a good starting point for setting up workflows like these.

Keep two things in mind when using the API. First, usage pricing varies by request type, and Google updates this pricing from time to time. Check the current rates and free usage limits before starting your project. Second, the API terms restrict permanent storage of some data. Confirm in the terms which fields you can keep and for how long.

### Third-party scraping tools

There are many browser extensions and cloud-based tools that pull Maps data in bulk. They're fast, but Google's terms of service generally restrict automated scraping. Assess the risks with your legal counsel before using these tools. Use the official API whenever possible.

## Step 5: Clean and deduplicate the list

Raw Maps data comes in messy. The same business appears two or three times under different keywords, some records have closed, and others don't fit your ICP at all. During cleanup, do the following:

1. **Deduplicate:** Use the unique ID Google assigns to each business (place ID) or the phone number as the key. Don't deduplicate by business name, since different businesses can share the same name.
2. **Remove closed businesses:** Filter out records marked "permanently closed" or "temporarily closed."
3. **Standardize phone formats:** Convert numbers to a single format starting with +90. Separating landlines and mobile numbers into different columns makes call planning easier.
4. **Group chain locations:** Instead of contacting 12 branches of the same brand separately, flag the head office as a single account. The number of locations is a strong signal of purchasing power.
5. **Eliminate irrelevant categories:** Filter out records such as dental supply stores or dental labs that show up in a "dental clinic" search. Of course, if these are also part of your ICP, move them into a separate segment.

In our experience, a considerable share of the raw list gets eliminated at this stage. That's not a bad thing. You've weeded out, up front, the records that would waste your sales team's time.

## Step 6: Find the decision-maker and enrich the list

Google Maps gives you the business, but often not the decision-maker. The phone number usually goes to a switchboard, a reception desk or an employee's mobile. To shorten the sales cycle, you need to enrich the list.

Enrichment sources include:

- **The business's website:** "About Us," "Our Team" and "Contact" pages list the names of founders, doctors or managers along with corporate email addresses.
- **LinkedIn:** At mid-sized and large businesses (especially manufacturers), the general manager, plant manager or procurement manager is on LinkedIn. Search by company name and find the relevant title.
- **B2B data tools:** For companies with a website, tools like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) save a lot of time by surfacing decision-maker emails and job titles from the domain. We looked at which tool offers better coverage in the Turkish market in our [Apollo vs. Lusha comparison](/blog/apollo-vs-lusha-b2b-data-tool-turkey).
- **Trade registry and chamber records:** Public trade registry announcements and chamber membership lists can help verify a company's authorized representative, particularly for manufacturers.
- **Instagram:** In segments like clinics, restaurants and beauty salons, the owner is usually active on Instagram and named in the bio.

At small businesses, the decision-maker is usually the owner. That makes the phone number on Maps valuable, because you can reach the decision-maker directly within a few attempts.

## Step 7: Prioritize leads using Maps signals

Say you have a clean list of 3,000 records. Calling them all with the same priority is inefficient. Maps data lets you build a simple scoring model at no extra cost.

A sample scoring table:

- **100+ reviews:** +3 points (high customer traffic, likely has budget)
- **30–100 reviews:** +2 points
- **Has a website:** +2 points (indicator of digital maturity)
- **Multiple locations:** +3 points
- **Received reviews in the last 3 months:** +1 point (active business)
- **Rating below 4.0 and reviews contain complaints about "appointments," "couldn't get through" or "waiting":** +2 points (they may already be experiencing the problem you solve)

The last item is especially valuable. If you sell appointment software and a clinic's reviews repeatedly complain that "you can never get through by phone," your outreach hook is ready-made. To apply this logic across your entire sales funnel, see our [guide to building a B2B lead scoring model](/blog/b2b-lead-scoring-model).

After scoring, split the list into three groups:

- **Group A:** Roughly the top 20% by score. Run personalized, multichannel outreach.
- **Group B:** The middle segment. Use semi-templated email and phone outreach.
- **Group C:** Low-priority records. Keep them in a pool for nurturing and future retries.

## Step 8: Personalize outreach with Maps data

The biggest advantage of Maps data is that it gives you real, concrete context about each business. Outreach that doesn't use this context in the message is no different from ordinary spam.

### Phone

In Turkey's SME and small-business segment, the phone is still the most effective channel. Emails often go unread, but calls get answered. Call landlines during business hours, at times when the business is least busy. For example, it's more effective to call restaurants outside lunch and dinner service, and clinics first thing in the morning. The opening hours on Maps feed directly into this planning. For call scripts, objection handling and follow-up cadence, see our [B2B cold calling guide](/blog/b2b-cold-calling-guide).

Sample opening line:

> "Hello, I was looking at your clinic's Google reviews in Ataşehir. Your patients are very happy with the quality of treatment, but a few reviews mention that it's hard to book an appointment by phone. We work with a system that solves exactly that. Could I have two minutes of your time?"

### Email

For businesses with a website and a corporate email address, email is the second main channel. Use a detail from Maps in the subject line and first sentence: the district, number of locations, a featured service or a theme from the reviews. Keep it short. Three or four sentences and one clear call to action are enough.

### Expectations in a sample funnel

Exact figures vary by industry and offer. For planning purposes, though, consider this scenario: from a 1,000-record A+B list, you'll connect with the decision-maker at a portion of the businesses you reach by phone. A small share of those will turn into demos or meetings. The key metrics to measure are:

- Connect rate (share of calls that reach the decision-maker)
- Meeting rate
- Conversion to sales by segment

If you track these metrics separately for each segment, within a few weeks you'll see which categories and regions perform best for you. You can then shape your next lists accordingly.

## Legal framework: What do KVKK, commercial messaging rules and Google's terms say?

Every team doing outbound with Maps data in Turkey needs to know about three areas. The following is general information. Be sure to get advice from legal counsel for your own operation.

- **KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law):** A company's trade name and corporate switchboard number alone may not count as personal data. But for sole proprietorships, the owner's name, mobile number and personal email are personal data. You need to document the legal basis on which you process this data, how long you retain it and how you fulfill your obligation to inform data subjects.
- **Commercial electronic messaging legislation and İYS:** The legislation sets different rules for commercial emails and SMS sent to tradespeople and merchants than for individual consumers. Even so, there are obligations, such as clearly offering the right to opt out in every message and processing opt-out requests promptly. Be especially careful with bulk SMS or WhatsApp campaigns.
- **Google's terms of service:** Automatically scraping Maps data and storing API data in ways the terms don't permit carry a risk of breach of contract. The official API and a compliant retention policy are the safest route in the long run.

## How much does it cost, and when does it pay off?

The direct cost of finding leads on Google Maps is low compared with other data sources. The real cost is time and people.

- **Manual method:** No software cost. Depending on the category and level of detail, an SDR can collect and do basic qualification on roughly 15–30 records per hour.
- **API method:** Requires usage-based fees and a few days of technical work for setup. For mid-sized lists, API costs often come in below the price of a data tool subscription. Still, don't draw up a budget without checking Google's current pricing.
- **Enrichment:** Data tools used to reach decision-makers are a separate subscription cost.
- **Outreach:** SDR salaries, calling infrastructure and email sending tools.

As for time to results, with a well-built Maps list, the first meetings usually start coming in during the first few weeks of outreach. However, to see the channel's true performance, you should plan a test period of at least a few months. ## What are the most common mistakes?

- **Searching with a single keyword at the province level:** Most of the list slips through the cracks. Drill down to district and OSB level.
- **Handing the raw list straight to the sales team:** Duplicate, closed and irrelevant records quickly drain SDR motivation.
- **Sending the same message to the entire list:** Not using the context that makes Maps most valuable (reviews, locations, geography) means wasting the channel.
- **Making review count the only criterion:** In some B2B sectors, especially manufacturing, even good companies have very few reviews. For industrial ICPs, focus on website quality and facility size rather than review count.
- **Not importing into your CRM:** Lists left in spreadsheets don't get followed up. Import every record into your CRM with a source tag (e.g., "Google Maps – İzmir – CNC"). That way, you can measure which segments convert to sales.
- **Putting off legal obligations:** Define up front what you'll do when an opt-out request comes in.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it legal to collect leads from Google Maps?

Although business information on Google Maps is publicly available, how you collect and use it matters. Using the official Places API in line with its terms is the safest route. For personal data belonging to sole proprietorships, you must comply with KVKK obligations and commercial messaging rules. We recommend getting advice from legal counsel for your own operation.

### Which channel is best for contacting Google Maps leads?

In Turkey's SME and small-business segment, the phone usually has the greatest impact, because the number on Maps often goes directly to the business or its owner. For larger businesses with a website and corporate email, combining email and LinkedIn with phone calls improves results. Whichever channel you choose, use the local context from Maps data in your message.

### How many records should I start with?

When testing a new segment, a well-cleaned and scored list of 200–500 records is enough. At this scale, you'll see connect and meeting rates within a few weeks. If the segment works, you can scale by applying the same category and keyword matrix to new provinces and districts.

### Does Google Maps replace sources like Apollo or LinkedIn?

No, these sources complement each other. Maps is strong at finding local and SME-scale businesses. Apollo and LinkedIn are better at finding the decision-maker's name, title and contact details. The most efficient setup is to pull the business list from Maps and enrich it with decision-maker information from other sources.

## Conclusion

For B2B SaaS companies selling to SMEs, local businesses and industrial firms in Turkey, Google Maps is one of the most underrated lead sources. What makes the difference isn't the tool itself but the system you build around it. Translate your ICP into Maps categories, split your geography by district and OSB, collect data in compliance with the terms, clean it and enrich it with decision-maker information, prioritize using Maps signals, and personalize outreach with real context. A team that applies these steps with discipline can reach thousands of prospects who don't appear in any other database, ahead of its competitors.

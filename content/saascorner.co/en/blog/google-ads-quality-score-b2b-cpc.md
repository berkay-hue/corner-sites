Google Ads Quality Score is a diagnostic metric, rated from 1 to 10, that shows how well your keyword, ad and landing page fit together. **The most lasting way to lower cost per click in B2B is to strengthen the fit between these three, not to raise your bids.** The better the fit, the less you pay to show your ad in the same or a better position. You also pay for fewer irrelevant clicks.

For B2B SaaS companies, this has a direct impact on cash flow. Keywords such as "CRM software", "sales automation" and "accounting software" have high costs per click. Search volume is low, and sales cycles take months. In this environment, the cost of each click becomes a significant part of your customer acquisition cost. In this post, we explain how Quality Score is calculated, why it tends to be low in B2B accounts, and how to improve it step by step, with concrete examples.

## What is Quality Score and why does it matter more in B2B?

Quality Score is a metric shown at the keyword level in the Google Ads interface. Google uses it to summarize how relevant your ad is compared with other advertisers bidding on the same keyword. One important detail: **Quality Score is not used directly in the auction.** At auction time, Google calculates real-time quality signals for every search. Quality Score is a historical summary of those signals. In other words, it is a diagnostic tool. However, when you improve the diagnosis, the real-time quality signals usually improve as well.

This metric carries more weight in B2B than in B2C, for several reasons:

- **Search volume is limited.** For a niche B2B SaaS product, a keyword with a few hundred searches a month can be the backbone of the entire campaign. Paying a high cost per click on that keyword eats up most of the budget.
- **Competition is intense and bids are high.** Software, finance and consulting are among the most expensive categories in Google Ads.
- **Search intent is mixed.** The person searching "what is CRM" could be an executive making a purchasing decision. The same search could also come from a student working on an assignment or a job seeker. If relevance is weak, you pay for their clicks too.
- **The conversion cycle is long.** In B2C, a bad click shows up immediately. In B2B, wasted spend only surfaces months later, in the pipeline report.

## How does Quality Score affect cost per click?

To determine your ad's position, Google calculates a value called **Ad Rank**. Its main components are:

- your bid,
- ad quality at the time of the auction,
- Ad Rank thresholds,
- the context of the search (device, location, time, etc.),
- the expected impact of assets (formerly called extensions).

The cost per click you actually pay is not your maximum bid. You pay just enough to beat the Ad Rank of the advertiser below you. If your quality signals are strong, you can hold the same position while paying less.

Let's look at a simplified, hypothetical example. Two SaaS companies are competing for the keyword "sales tracking software":

| | Company A | Company B |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum bid | TRY 40 | TRY 25 |
| Quality Score | 4 | 8 |
| Simple rank value (bid × quality) | 160 | 200 |

In this simple model, Company B ranks above Company A despite the lower bid. Company B pays just enough to beat A: 160 / 8 ≈ TRY 20. Company A, meanwhile, would have to raise its bid significantly to reach the same position.

The real auction is far more complex than this table. Still, the logic holds: **When quality is high, you pay less for the same position.** On a search budget of tens of thousands of lira per month, a lasting drop in average cost per click means noticeably more visitors for the same budget.

## The three components of Quality Score

Google bases Quality Score on three components. It rates each one as "Above average", "Average" or "Below average". When optimizing, focus on these three components rather than the overall score.

### Expected click-through rate (Expected CTR)

This component estimates how likely your ad is to be clicked when shown. It is calculated with external factors such as ad position stripped out. In B2B, expected CTR tends to be low for two reasons. First, ad copy is generic: phrases like "Leading solution" or "Grow your business" say nothing. Second, broad match causes the ad to show on irrelevant searches, where it doesn't get clicked.

### Ad relevance

This component measures how closely your ad copy matches search intent. If you mix different intents such as "CRM software", "customer management software", "sales pipeline tool" and "proposal software" in a single ad group, no ad copy can be relevant to all of them at once.

### Landing page experience

This component assesses how well the page the user lands on answers their search intent. Page speed, mobile-friendliness, transparency and ease of navigation are also taken into account. The most common mistake in B2B SaaS accounts is sending all traffic to the homepage or a generic demo form. The user searches for "CRM with e-invoice integration" and lands on a homepage that says "Your digital transformation partner".

## Where can you find your Quality Score?

Quality Score is not shown in the table by default. To see it, follow these steps:

1. In Google Ads, go to the **Keywords** tab.
2. From the **Columns** menu, open the "Quality Score" group.
3. Add these columns: Quality Score, Exp. CTR, Ad relevance, Landing page exp. If you want to compare past periods, also add the historical columns marked "(hist.)".
4. Save the view so you look at the same table every month.

For some keywords, you may see a "—" instead of a score. This means there isn't enough impression data. It happens often with low-volume B2B keywords. Rather than evaluating these keywords one by one, it's more reliable to assess them at the ad group level alongside keywords with similar intent.

## Why is Quality Score low in B2B accounts?

When we audit B2B SaaS accounts, the same few problems usually sit behind low Quality Scores:

- **Overly broad keywords.** Single-word terms like "software", "CRM" or "automation" cover very different intents. They drag down both click-through rate and relevance.
- **Crowded ad groups.** A single ad group contains 40-50 keywords and just one ad.
- **Missing negative keywords.** Terms such as "free", "crack", "job posting", "salary", "what is pdf" and "internship" aren't blocked. The search terms report isn't reviewed regularly.
- **A single landing page.** Every ad points to the same demo page.
- **Slow, heavy pages.** The page is loaded with large images, autoplaying videos and numerous third-party scripts.
- **Ads written in corporate jargon.** The ads use the company's internal language rather than the user's.

Almost all of these are structural problems. That's why bid adjustments won't fix them.

## How to improve Quality Score step by step

The plan below is a practical sequence you can apply to an existing B2B Google Ads account. We recommend sticking to the order. Optimizing copy before fixing the structure leaves the job half done.

### Step 1: Measure the current state, weighted by spend

Looking at individual keyword scores can be misleading. Calculate an impression-weighted average at the account level:

**Weighted Quality Score = Σ (Quality Score × Impressions) / Σ Impressions**

Export the keyword report and run this calculation in a spreadsheet. Then sort the keywords by spend. **Keywords that account for most of your spend and have a score of 5 or lower** are your first priority list. Working on a low-scoring keyword that gets a handful of clicks a month is a waste of optimization time.

For each priority keyword, note which of the three components is "Below average". That note tells you which step to focus on.

### Step 2: Narrow ad groups by intent

Each ad group should serve a single search intent. As a practical rule, every keyword in an ad group should be meaningfully answerable by the same headline.

For example, instead of one group called "sales software", these groups work better:

- **Pipeline management:** "sales pipeline software", "sales funnel tracking software"
- **Proposal management:** "proposal software", "online quote software"
- **Field sales:** "field sales app", "field team tracking software"
- **Competitor searches:** searches containing competitor brand names, in a separate campaign

Prepare two or three strong ads and a dedicated landing page for each group. The number of groups goes up, but management gets easier. You also see clearly which intent converts.

### Step 3: Clean up search terms and build a negative list

The biggest factor dragging down expected click-through rate is your ad appearing on the wrong searches. Review the search terms report at least once a week. Add irrelevant terms to your negative list. B2B accounts typically block these categories:

- job seekers: "job posting", "careers", "salary", "internship"
- students: "assignment", "thesis", "pdf", "lecture notes"
- freebie seekers: "free download", "crack", "full version"
- wrong segment: if your product is for enterprises, "individual", "personal", "for home"

We covered this topic in detail in our post on [preventing B2B budget leaks with negative keywords in Google Ads](/blog/google-ads-negative-keywords-b2b). You can adapt the template list there directly to your account.

### Step 4: Write ad copy in the language of the search

Responsive search ads let you write 15 headlines and 4 descriptions. In B2B, we recommend using that space like this:

- **Include the keyword itself or a close variant in at least 2-3 headlines.** For example, "Proposal Software" or "Online Quote Software".
- **State a concrete benefit.** "Create proposals in minutes" says far more than "Boost your productivity".
- **Name your audience explicitly.** Phrases like "For sales teams" or "For companies with 50+ employees" keep poor-fit users from clicking. This may lower click-through rate somewhat, but it raises the quality of your clicks.
- **Add a trust element.** Region-specific benefits such as Turkish-language support, local data hosting, e-invoice integration or KVKK compliance matter to B2B buyers.
- **Use a call to action.** For example, "Book a demo" or "Try it for 14 days". If you're not sure which model suits you, see our post on [the free trial vs. demo question](/blog/free-trial-vs-demo-b2b-saas).

If needed, pin your most critical headline to position 1. However, pinning every headline prevents Google from testing combinations and lowers ad effectiveness.

### Step 5: Build a landing page for each intent

Landing page experience is the component most often rated "Below average" in B2B accounts. It is also the one that takes longest to improve. The minimum standards are:

- **The headline should echo the search intent.** Someone searching for "proposal software" should see that phrase on the first screen of the page.
- **The first screen should answer what, for whom and why.** What does the product do, who is it for, and what makes it different?
- **The page should load fast on mobile.** Check it with PageSpeed Insights. Remove unnecessary scripts, uncompressed images and autoplaying videos.
- **Keep the form short.** For a first touch, name, work email, company and perhaps team size are enough. You can collect the rest during the sales call.
- **Be transparent.** Provide easy access to pricing, the privacy policy and contact details.
- **Simplify navigation.** Every link in the main menu is a door that leads visitors away from the conversion goal.

For page structure, you can follow the steps in our [B2B SaaS landing page guide](/blog/how-to-build-b2b-saas-landing-page). A practical starting point is to build a separate page for each of your 3-5 highest-spending ad groups. You can initially point the remaining groups to the closest existing page.

### Step 6: Make full use of assets

Assets (sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, call assets, image assets) are not a direct component of Quality Score. However, their expected impact is factored into Ad Rank, and they increase click-through rate. Recommendations for B2B:

- **Sitelinks:** Pricing, Integrations, Case Studies, Request a Demo
- **Callouts:** "Turkish-language support", "Onboarding support included", "e-Invoice integration"
- **Structured snippets:** "Modules: CRM, Proposals, Reporting, Field Sales"

### Step 7: Monitor changes and be patient

Quality Score updates slowly, especially for low-volume B2B keywords. After making a change, you usually need to wait a few weeks before judging the result. Record the dates of your changes in a note (or using the notes feature in Google Ads). Don't change ad copy, landing pages and bid strategy all at once. Otherwise, you won't be able to tell which change worked.

## What are the common mistakes in Quality Score optimization?

Quality Score is a useful indicator, but it isn't a goal. Common mistakes include:

- **Pausing converting keywords to raise the score.** If a keyword with a score of 4 brings in qualified demo requests, improve it instead of pausing it.
- **Using misleading promises to inflate click-through rate.** Writing "Free CRM" and sending users to a paid product's page lifts click-through rate in the short term. But it hurts landing page experience, conversion rate and brand trust.
- **Being misled by high scores on brand keywords.** Brand searches usually score 8-10, which makes the account average look better than it is. Calculate the weighted average separately for brand and non-brand campaigns.
- **Focusing only on cost per click.** If lead quality drops along with cost per click, there's no real gain.

## Does a lower cost per click always mean better leads?

No. In B2B, the real question isn't "How much am I paying per click?" It should be "How much am I paying per qualified sales opportunity?" That's why you should always track your Quality Score work alongside the bottom of the funnel:

1. **Cost per click (CPC):** Expected to fall as Quality Score improves.
2. **Cost per lead (CPL):** Should fall thanks to more relevant traffic and better pages. You can find the calculation method in our post on [how to calculate cost per lead in B2B](/blog/b2b-cost-per-lead-cpl-calculation).
3. **Cost per qualified lead (MQL/SQL):** The true efficiency indicator.
4. **Pipeline and closed revenue:** Shows the campaign's contribution to the business.

The way to build this chain is to import lead stages from your CRM back into Google Ads as offline conversions. That way, smart bidding strategies optimize for qualified opportunities rather than form fills. For this to work, marketing and sales need to agree on a definition of "qualified lead". ## Example scenario: What does a 90-day improvement plan look like?

The scenario below is hypothetical and is intended to illustrate the workflow in a typical B2B SaaS account.

**Starting point:** A sales automation software company spends most of its monthly search budget on 6 ad groups. The impression-weighted Quality Score on non-brand keywords is around 4. Landing page experience is "Below average" for most keywords. All traffic goes to the homepage.

**Days 1-30:**
- The search terms report for the last 90 days is reviewed. Around 150 negative keywords are added for irrelevant searches.
- The 6 ad groups are split into 14 intent-based groups.
- Two responsive search ads with the keyword in the headline are written for each group.

**Days 31-60:**
- Dedicated landing pages go live for the 4 highest-spending groups.
- Images are compressed to improve page speed, and unnecessary scripts are removed.
- The number of form fields is cut from 8 to 4.

**Days 61-90:**
- The SQL stage is imported from the CRM into Google Ads as an offline conversion.
- Underperforming ad headlines are replaced with new ones.
- The weighted Quality Score is recalculated and compared against the starting value, along with CPL and cost per SQL.

The expected outcome of this kind of work is a weighted score several points higher on non-brand keywords and a meaningful drop in average cost per click. More importantly, cost per qualified lead is expected to fall. Exact figures vary by industry, competition and starting point, so measuring with your own data is essential.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good Quality Score?

For non-brand B2B keywords, 7 and above is generally considered healthy. A score of 5 or lower signals that improvement is needed. Brand keywords naturally score in the 8-10 range, so when interpreting the account average, you need to evaluate brand and non-brand keywords separately.

### How long does it take for Quality Score to change?

The score updates as enough impression data accumulates. For high-volume keywords, changes can show up within a few days. For low-volume B2B keywords, it can take several weeks. That's why making changes one at a time and monitoring them for at least 2-4 weeks gives more reliable results.

### Does raising my bid increase Quality Score?

No. Quality Score is calculated independently of your bid, based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Raising your bid may improve your position, but it means paying more per click. The real savings come from improving relevance.

### Should I pause a keyword with a low Quality Score that still converts?

Usually not. If the keyword is generating qualified leads and pipeline, keep it running. In the meantime, focus on improving the weak component; this is most often the landing page or the ad copy. Quality Score is a diagnostic tool. Your ultimate decision criterion should be cost per qualified opportunity.

## Conclusion

Google Ads Quality Score is one of the most powerful levers B2B SaaS companies have for lowering cost per click. But this lever works through structure, not bid settings. When intent-based ad groups, regularly cleaned search terms, ads written in the language of the search and intent-specific landing pages work together, the score goes up. And when the score goes up, you reach more qualified visitors with the same budget. Don't limit this work to cost per click. When you combine it with qualified opportunity data from your CRM, you can see whether your Google Ads spend is actually turning into revenue.

B2B prospecting with MERSIS and chamber of commerce data is a method of building target customer lists from the sector, founding date, capital, address and management information of officially registered companies in Türkiye. Its biggest advantage is that the data comes from official registry records, not self-reported claims. You know that every company on your list actually exists, which line of business it is registered in and what changes it has gone through recently. The limitation is just as clear: these sources give you the "company," not the "decision-maker." Set up correctly, though, this is the most solid starting point for reaching thousands of Turkish SMEs that don't show up in global databases.

Most B2B SaaS companies start prospecting with LinkedIn or global data platforms. These tools work well for tech companies in Istanbul. The picture changes, however, when you're targeting a machinery manufacturer in Konya, a textile firm in Gaziantep or a furniture exporter in Kayseri. Many of these companies have a weak digital footprint. Yet every one of them appears in the official registry and chamber records. In this article, we walk through what these sources offer, how to filter them, the legal boundaries and the steps to make your list sales-ready, using a concrete example.

## Why is official registry data so valuable for B2B prospecting?

The biggest problem with purchased or scraped lists is reliability. Closed companies, incorrect industry tags and addresses that haven't been updated in years stay on the list. Official registry and chamber records are different in three ways:

- **Entity verification:** The company is registered. If it is in liquidation or has been deregistered, that also shows up in the records.
- **Activity classification:** Companies are classified using NACE codes. This makes the definition of "industries that fit our ICP" measurable.
- **Time-stamped changes:** Incorporations, capital increases, address changes, executive appointments and branch openings are announced with dates. These are extremely valuable **trigger events** for sales.

Global platforms' coverage of Türkiye is particularly limited for companies with 10-200 employees, non-exporters and businesses with weak corporate websites. And that is exactly the sweet spot for many B2B SaaS products: basic bookkeeping, field team management, production planning, B2B e-commerce, HR and payroll software. If you want to compare the overall efficiency of different channels, our [comparison of B2B lead generation channels](/blog/b2b-lead-generation-channels-compared) offers a good framework. Official registry data should be thought of as a "foundation layer" that feeds all of these channels.

## What sources are available, and what does each offer?

There are several main sources of company data in Türkiye. Each has different strengths and weaknesses.

### MERSIS (Central Registry Recording System)

Operated by the Ministry of Trade, MERSIS is the central system through which trade registry transactions are processed. Every company is assigned a 16-digit MERSIS number. This number is the most reliable unique key you can use to match a company across other data sources.

An important note for prospecting: MERSIS is not a "bulk list download" tool. Its public query options are limited, and the system is designed for one-by-one verification. That's why it's more realistic to position MERSIS not as the source of your list, but as a **verification and deduplication layer**. Once a company's trade name, registry status and MERSIS number are verified, classic problems such as the same company appearing as three separate CRM records under three different spellings disappear.

### Turkish Trade Registry Gazette

This is the real gold mine for sales teams. The Trade Registry Gazette publishes announcements on:

- New company incorporations
- Capital increases and decreases
- Address changes and headquarters relocations
- Board of directors and managing director changes
- Branch openings
- Changes of legal form (for example, converting from a limited liability company to a joint-stock company)
- Mergers, demergers and liquidation notices

Each of these announcements can be the start of a sales story. A company that increases its capital is investing in growth. A firm opening a new branch now needs multi-location operations management. And at a company with a new executive, the newcomer is usually open to questioning the existing tools.

### Chambers of commerce and industry

Many chambers of commerce and industry in major cities such as Istanbul, Ankara, Izmir and Bursa offer member directories or company search pages containing basic information about their members. The content varies from chamber to chamber. It typically includes the trade name, address, professional group, field of activity and sometimes a phone number or website.

The value of chambers lies in their **professional group** classification. Professional committees such as "plastic packaging manufacturers" or "medical device wholesalers" provide a segmentation that is more practical than NACE codes and closer to sales language. In addition, the lists of top industrial enterprises published by chambers of industry are a serious starting point for SaaS companies targeting the enterprise segment.

### TOBB and capacity report data

The industry database of TOBB (the Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Türkiye) contains information on manufacturers that hold capacity reports. Details such as production capacity, product groups and headcount sharpen targeting, especially for companies selling production, ERP, quality management or energy monitoring software.

### KAP (Public Disclosure Platform)

For publicly traded companies, KAP provides regular information on financial statements, investment decisions and management changes. If your target audience is the enterprise segment, KAP disclosures offer richer triggers than the Trade Registry Gazette.

## How do you build an ICP list from this data, step by step?

Knowing the sources isn't enough. Turning raw data into a list your sales team can use requires a disciplined process. The steps below summarize a workflow that works in the field.

1. **Define your ICP in registry language.** "Mid-sized manufacturing companies" is a vague definition. Instead, define it like this: "Activities under codes 22, 25 and 28 in NACE Section C; joint-stock or limited liability company; at least 5 years old; headquartered in the Marmara and Aegean regions; paid-in capital above a certain threshold." This definition can be translated directly into filters in your data sources.

2. **Build your total universe.** Collect the companies that fit your ICP from chamber directories, industrial lists and capacity report data. At this stage, the goal is coverage, not perfection.

3. **Verify and deduplicate with MERSIS.** Check each company's registry status. Remove companies that are deregistered or in liquidation. Merge duplicate records of the same company, whether from different chambers or listed as branches, into a single master record.

4. **Add signals from the Trade Registry Gazette.** Scan announcements from the last 12-18 months. Flag companies with capital increases, branch openings and executive changes.

5. **Enrich at the company level.** Add information such as website, estimated employee range, technologies used and export status.

6. **Enrich at the contact level.** Identify 1-3 decision-makers per company. We cover this step separately in the next section.

7. **Score and tier.** Divide companies into tiers A, B and C. Tier A deserves a personalized, multichannel approach. Tier C goes into a more automated flow. When building your scoring logic, you can use our guide on [how to build a B2B lead scoring model step by step](/blog/b2b-lead-scoring-model). The firmographic criteria described there map directly onto registry data.

8. **Import into your CRM.** Use the MERSIS number as a unique field. Create separate fields for source, signal type and signal date.

## Which registry signals turn into sales opportunities?

Trigger-based prospecting generates far more engagement than sending cold emails to a static list. The reason is simple: you deliver the right message at the right time. Here are the main signals you can read from the Trade Registry Gazette and their potential SaaS counterparts:

- **Capital increase:** Can indicate growth, investment or a new partner coming on board. A strong signal for finance, ERP, HR and sales software. Sample message: "During a growth phase, managing operations with manual processes is usually the first bottleneck."
- **New branch or headquarters relocation:** Creates a need for multi-location management. A good fit for field team, stock, inventory and communication tools.
- **Executive change:** A new general manager or board member is open to change in their first 6-9 months. This is an opportunity to replace the incumbent vendor.
- **Conversion from limited liability to joint-stock company:** Signals institutionalization and a growing need for auditing and reporting.
- **New incorporation:** Valuable for SaaS companies offering setup packages to firms building systems from scratch. However, purchasing power may be limited, so evaluate it together with capital information.
- **Merger:** Integrating two different systems comes onto the agenda. This is a long but large opportunity for enterprise software.

As a practical rule, signal "freshness" matters. The first 60-90 days after a capital increase announcement is the window in which your message is most relevant. Referring to an announcement from a year ago makes your outreach feel insincere.

## How do you get from company data to the decision-maker?

Official sources give you the company. But sales happen with people. There are several ways to close this gap, and the best results usually come from combining them.

**Executives in the registry record:** Managing directors of limited liability companies and board members of joint-stock companies appear in the announcements. In the SMB segment, these are usually the direct decision-makers. At a manufacturing company with 20-50 employees, the owner often makes the software decision.

**Company website:** "About Us" and "Our Team" pages often list department heads, especially at mid-sized companies.

**LinkedIn:** By searching by company name, you can find purchasing, IT, finance or operations managers. We covered whether Sales Navigator is truly necessary at this stage in detail in our article on [whether LinkedIn Sales Navigator is necessary for B2B prospecting](/blog/linkedin-sales-navigator-b2b-prospecting).

**B2B contact data platforms:** Tools like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) save significant time by letting you upload company domains in bulk and find people with relevant titles along with their work email addresses. However, match rates can be low for small companies in Türkiye. Always run the emails you find through a verification tool.

**Phone:** Switchboard numbers in chamber directories are still an effective channel for identifying the decision-maker. A short call asking "Who makes your IT decisions?" can deliver a clearer answer than hours of digital research. For scripts you can use at this stage, check out our guide on [how to do B2B cold calling step by step](/blog/b2b-cold-calling-guide).

A word of caution: contact-level enrichment rates vary widely by segment. While you can find most decision-makers at enterprise companies through digital sources, it's hard to move forward at small family businesses without confirming by phone. Plan accordingly.

## Is it legal to use MERSIS and chamber data for sales?

This is the most frequently asked question and the one that requires the most care. The following is a general framework and does not constitute legal advice. We recommend consulting a KVKK (Turkish Personal Data Protection Law) specialist before setting up your operation.

**Legal entity data vs. personal data:** A company's trade name, address, field of activity and capital belong to the legal entity and are not considered personal data under KVKK. However, executives' names, personal phone numbers or email addresses containing a person's name are personal data. In sole proprietorships, company data and personal data are largely intertwined.

**Publicly disclosed data:** Information announced in the Trade Registry Gazette is public. However, under KVKK, data made public by the data subject may only be processed in line with the purpose for which it was made public. Using this data for marketing purposes is open to interpretation. That's why it's important to document your legitimate interest assessment and fulfill your obligation to inform.

**Commercial electronic messages:** Under Law No. 6563 and the related regulation, prior consent may not be required for commercial electronic messages sent to merchants and tradespeople. However, every message must give the recipient an easy way to opt out. Be sure to check how İYS (Message Management System) obligations apply to your situation.

**Source terms of use:** Chamber directories and public query pages may have terms of use that prohibit automated data extraction. Read these terms before doing any bulk scraping. Where possible, use official data request channels.

Practical best practices include:

- In every email, clearly state who you are, where you obtained the data and how to unsubscribe.
- Permanently suppress anyone who opts out across all your systems (CRM, email tool, call list).
- Remove personal email addresses (gmail, hotmail, etc.) from outbound flows.
- Set a data retention period and delete personal data with no engagement for 12-18 months.

## A realistic example: selling production tracking software to manufacturers in the Aegean region

To make this concrete, let's walk through a hypothetical scenario. The figures below are not results from a real campaign; they are illustrative examples designed to show the planning logic.

**Company:** A SaaS company selling cloud-based production tracking software to mid-sized manufacturers.
**ICP:** Joint-stock or limited liability companies in Izmir, Manisa and Denizli; in metalworking, plastics and machinery manufacturing; with 20-250 employees; at least 5 years old.

**Step 1, universe:** Approximately 1,500 companies matching the ICP are collected from chamber directories and industrial data.

**Step 2, cleanup:** After MERSIS verification and deduplication, companies in liquidation, duplicated as branches or with mismatched fields of activity are removed. About 1,100 companies remain.

**Step 3, signals:** Around 180 companies with a capital increase, branch opening or executive change announced in the Trade Registry Gazette in the last 18 months are flagged as "Tier A."

**Step 4, contact enrichment:** In Tier A, an average of 2 decision-makers per company are targeted. Using digital sources and phone confirmation, at least one verified contact is reached at 140 of the 180 companies.

**Step 5, multichannel sequence:** A 3-week sequence is set up for Tier A:
- Day 1: Personalized email referencing the signal ("When production tracking gets split across two locations with your new facility in Manisa...")
- Day 3: LinkedIn connection request
- Day 6: Phone call
- Day 10: Follow-up email with a short case story
- Day 17: Final call attempt and a "If you're not the right person, who is?" email

**Step 6, measurement:** The campaign is evaluated by tracking reply rate, meeting rate and meeting-to-opportunity conversion. Tiers B and C proceed with lighter, industry-specific messaging.

The strength of this setup is that the message is based on an event the company is actually experiencing, rather than a generic "boost your efficiency" promise. Signal-based sequences generally get noticeably better responses than static lists in the same segment. You'll start to see your own rates realistically after the first 300-500 touches.

## How much does it cost?

Many of the official sources are free or low-cost. The real cost lies in time and tools. A rough planning framework might look like this:

- **Data collection and cleanup:** For a universe of 1,000 companies, manual work by an experienced researcher takes a few business days. Semi-automated tools shorten this, but the verification step still requires human review.
- **Contact enrichment:** Data platform subscriptions and email verification credits are the most notable indirect costs. In the SMB segment, where phone confirmation is needed, SDR hours become the main factor.
- **Signal tracking:** Once it becomes routine, scanning the Trade Registry Gazette weekly takes a few hours per week.
- **Maintenance:** The list isn't static. Companies close and executives change. Budget for quarterly updates.

## What are the most common mistakes?

Here are the traps teams trying this method frequently fall into:

- **Blindly trusting NACE codes:** Companies register a broad field of activity at incorporation and never update it. The "main activity" may differ from the actual business. Confirm with a website check.
- **Treating branches as separate companies:** Having three SDRs contact the same company through three different branches damages your brand reputation. Build a hierarchy based on the MERSIS number or the main trade name.
- **Not using the signal in your message:** Selecting companies by signal and then sending a generic template wastes half the effort.
- **Relying solely on email:** In Türkiye's traditional industries, phone and LinkedIn often perform better than email.
- **Leaving the legal framework for later:** Your opt-out mechanism and privacy notice should be ready before the campaign launches.
- **Building the list once and forgetting it:** Six months later, a significant portion of the list will be outdated. Plan your update cycle from the start.

## How should you structure this data in your CRM?

The value of this data multiplies when it's structured correctly in your CRM. Here's the recommended minimum field structure:

- **Company object:** MERSIS number (unique), official trade name, short name, NACE code, professional group, province/district, year founded, company type, capital range, ICP tier (A/B/C)
- **Signal fields:** Latest signal type, signal date, signal source (such as the Trade Registry Gazette announcement number)
- **Contact object:** Role (executive in registry, department head, etc.), data source, verification date, contact permission and opt-out status
- **Campaign fields:** Which sequence they entered, number of touches, last touch date

With this structure, you can create a view like "decision-makers at Tier A companies in the Aegean region that increased their capital in the last 90 days and haven't been contacted yet" with a single click. This is the point where official registry data turns from an ordinary list into a continuously running opportunity engine.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you download bulk company lists from MERSIS?

MERSIS is designed for registry transactions and individual verification, not for downloading bulk lists. In prospecting, the most efficient use is verifying the companies on a list you've built from other sources and deduplicating them by MERSIS number. For building a bulk company universe, chamber directories, industrial lists and the Trade Registry Gazette are better starting points.

### Is it appropriate to use information from the Trade Registry Gazette in sales emails?

A brief, respectful reference to a public development such as a company's capital increase or branch opening makes your message relevant. However, you should avoid going into personal details, be transparent about your data source and offer an opt-out in every message. KVKK compliance for personal data processing should be assessed with a specialist.

### Which SaaS products is this method best suited for?

It's best suited for SaaS products whose target audience is SMEs and mid-sized companies in Türkiye and that sell to traditional industries such as manufacturing, logistics, wholesale and construction. Basic bookkeeping, ERP, field team management, HR and B2B e-commerce software fall into this category. For products that target only tech companies, global data platforms alone may be sufficient.

### How long does an outbound campaign built on registry data take to deliver results?

List building and enrichment usually take 2-4 weeks. The first replies and meetings start coming in within a few weeks after the sequence launches. For a meaningful performance evaluation, a few hundred touches should be completed and the length of your sales cycle should be taken into account.

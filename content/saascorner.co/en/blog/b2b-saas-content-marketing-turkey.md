The best way to start B2B SaaS content marketing in Turkey is to first pin down your ideal customer profile and the questions your sales team hears most often in the field, then begin with a small number of in-depth pieces that answer those questions. Two to four high-quality pieces a month, a consistent distribution plan and a feedback loop with your sales team are enough of a foundation for the first 90 days. The goal isn't to collect traffic. It's to build the trust that speeds up the buying decision.

Many Turkish SaaS companies see content marketing as "launching a blog and publishing a post every week." When the traffic doesn't come after a few months, or the traffic that does arrive doesn't convert into sales, they shelve the effort. The problem usually isn't the content itself. It lies in which question the content answers, who it speaks to and how it connects to the sales process. In this article, we walk step by step through how a B2B SaaS company starting from scratch can build its content marketing, taking into account the unique conditions of the Turkish market.

## Why is B2B SaaS content marketing different in Turkey?

Most global SaaS content guides are written for the US market. Turkey differs in several ways, and a content strategy that overlooks these differences can easily go to waste.

**The Turkish content gap is still wide.** In areas such as CRM, sales automation, HR software and finance software, English-language resources are abundant. In-depth, up-to-date content in Turkish, however, is limited. That gives companies producing high-quality Turkish content an opportunity to establish themselves relatively quickly, both in search engines and in buyers' minds.

**Decision-making is relationship-driven.** In Turkey, trust, references and personal relationships are decisive in B2B purchasing decisions. Content alone doesn't close deals. Its job is to make your company recognizable, understandable and trustworthy before a sales rep ever sits down at the table.

**Local regulations and business practices create content opportunities.** Topics such as e-invoicing, e-archiving, KVKK (Turkey's data protection law), SGK (social security) processes and local banking integrations are areas your global competitors never touch. For an HR software company, a piece on "How do you store candidate data in compliance with KVKK?" is far more valuable than a generic "HR software benefits" article.

**The buyer pool is smaller but more connected.** Decision-makers in a given industry often know one another. A good piece of content can spread quickly on LinkedIn, in WhatsApp groups and at industry events. A poor or superficial one can damage your reputation just as quickly.

## What should you clarify before you start creating content?

Before publishing your first article, you should have written answers to three fundamental questions. Companies that skip this step usually end up writing "for everyone" and ultimately reach no one.

### 1. Who is your ideal customer?

Define the customer group for which you create the most value in terms of industry, company size, geography and technological maturity. For example:

- **Industry:** Logistics and distribution companies
- **Size:** 50-500 employees
- **Region:** Istanbul, Kocaeli, Izmir, Bursa
- **Current situation:** Running operations through Excel and email, not yet using an integrated system

This clarity directly determines which topics you'll write about.

### 2. Who is involved in the buying decision?

There is no single buyer in B2B SaaS sales. There is typically a **user** (the person who will use the product every day), a **decision-maker** (the executive who approves the budget) and an **influencer** (the IT, finance or legal department). Each of them has a different question in mind:

- Operations manager: "Will this system actually make my team's day-to-day work easier?"
- General manager or CFO: "How long will it take to see a return on the investment?"
- IT manager: "Will it integrate with our existing ERP, and where will the data be stored?"

Your content plan should dedicate at least a few pieces to each of these three people.

### 3. What do you expect from content?

"Brand awareness" is a vague goal that's hard to measure. Set more concrete targets instead, such as the number of demo requests, qualified leads coming from content, a shorter sales cycle or the number of content pieces your sales team uses in proposals. ## What topics should you write about?

The most productive way to find topics isn't brainstorming at your desk. It's listening to your existing customer touchpoints. The four sources below are usually more than enough to fill your content calendar for the first six months.

### Sales calls

Hold a 45-minute meeting with your sales team and ask these questions:

1. What questions come up most often during demos?
2. What objections were raised most often in lost deals?
3. Which competitors and alternatives do customers compare us with?
4. Which topic do you have to explain from scratch every single time?

This meeting usually yields 15-25 content ideas. Questions like "How are we different from Competitor X?", "How long does migration take?" and "How do you import data from Excel into the system?" turn into content that directly speeds up the sales process.

### Customer support tickets

Recurring questions sent to your support team reveal what both existing customers and prospective buyers want to know. Some of these questions can become knowledge base articles, while others can be expanded into broader guides.

### Search data

Search volumes in Turkish are lower than in English. That makes phrases with 50-200 monthly searches but high purchase intent valuable. Terms like "field sales tracking software" or "dealer management software" bring in the right buyer even with low volume. ### Industry news

A new regulation, a transformation in the industry or a macroeconomic development creates opportunities for well-timed content. During periods of high inflation, for example, the question "How do you reflect subscription costs in your budget?" is highly relevant for finance managers.

## Which content types should you start with?

For a team with limited resources, trying every format at once is inefficient. In the early stages, the following order of priority makes sense for most B2B SaaS companies.

### Priority 1: Sales-adjacent content (bottom of the funnel)

This content answers the questions of buyers who are close to a purchase and delivers the fastest return:

- **Comparison content:** "Software X or Excel? When should you make the switch?"
- **Pricing and cost content:** Even if you don't share your exact pricing, clearly explain the factors that affect cost.
- **Implementation and migration guides:** "How many weeks does setup take, and who on the team needs to be involved?"
- **Use cases:** Step-by-step examples showing how you solve a specific problem for a specific industry.

Turkish buyers appreciate pricing transparency. Instead of a "Call us for pricing" approach, content that explains the variables affecting cost both builds trust and brings in higher-quality leads.

### Priority 2: Problem-focused guides (middle of the funnel)

The buyer is aware of the problem but hasn't yet started looking for a solution. Example titles:

- "How do you measure your field team's visit efficiency?"
- "5 root causes of delays in dealer orders"
- "Why don't sales forecasts in Excel add up?"

This content doesn't sell your product directly, but the way you define the problem positions you as an expert.

### Priority 3: Thought leadership (top of the funnel)

This is content in which founders or senior executives share their views on the industry. In Turkey, this kind of content has a particularly strong impact on LinkedIn when shared through personal profiles. On its own, however, it takes time to generate sales, so it makes more sense to focus on it after the first two priorities are in place.

### A note on case studies

Customers in Turkey are often reluctant to have their names and figures shared. In that case, anonymous case studies work well: with descriptions such as "an Izmir-based food distribution company with 120 employees," you can share the real process and the improvements achieved, with the customer's approval. Even when anonymous, a concrete account of the process is far more persuasive than generic words of praise.

## How should you plan the first 90 days?

The plan below is a realistic starting framework that can be run by one content lead with part-time support.

### Days 1-30: Foundations and first pieces

1. Document your ideal customer profile and 2-3 personas in writing.
2. Interview your sales and support teams to gather at least 20 topic ideas.
3. Classify the topics as bottom, middle and top of the funnel.
4. Publish 2 bottom-of-the-funnel pieces in the first month (for example, one comparison and one cost piece).
5. Set up basic measurement on your website: form conversions, demo requests and content-level source tracking.

### Days 31-60: Cadence and distribution

1. Move to a cadence of 3-4 pieces a month.
2. Prepare a distribution plan for each piece (we go into detail below).
3. Run a short session for your sales team on how to use the content.
4. Review the performance of the first pieces and note which topics are gaining traction.

### Days 61-90: Measurement and improvement

1. Tag content-generated leads in your CRM and assess their quality with the sales team.
2. Update and expand the 2 best-performing pieces.
3. Repurpose one piece into different formats: a LinkedIn post, an email newsletter, a short video or a webinar.
4. Reshape the next quarter's plan using the data from the first 90 days.

By the end of 90 days, traffic may not be high yet. Organic visibility for Turkish B2B searches typically takes 4-6 months to take hold. The real indicators to watch during this period are whether your sales team is using the content and the quality of the few leads coming in.

## How should you distribute content once it's written?

The most common mistake in content marketing is thinking the job is done once you hit publish. In practice, at least a third of your time should go to distribution. A guide that took 10 hours to create delivers far more value with 3-4 hours of distribution effort.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn is the most effective organic channel for reaching B2B decision-makers in Turkey. However, sharing a link from your company page isn't enough on its own. Approaches that work better include:

- Sharing the core idea of the piece from the founder's or a subject-matter expert's personal profile as a post that stands on its own, before adding a link
- Turning a long guide into a 5-7 slide carousel
- Presenting a data point or framework from the piece as a single visual

### Sales team

Your sales team is often your most powerful content distribution channel. A link a rep sends after a call, saying "We have a detailed guide on the migration process we discussed, feel free to take a look," can have more impact than any ad. ## How should content and sales work together?

Content and sales teams working in separate worlds is one of the most common reasons content marketing fails. Here are a few practical ways to build that connection:

- **A 30-minute monthly sync meeting:** The sales team shares new objections and questions, and the content team presents published and planned content.
- **A sales content library:** A simple table showing sales reps which content to send at which stage. For example, the "pricing objection" row lists the relevant cost piece, and the "integration concerns" row lists the technical guide.
- **Content tracking in your CRM:** Recording which pieces of content an opportunity has engaged with gradually reveals which content genuinely contributes to sales.
- **Connecting to product usage data:** For companies with a free trial or freemium model, accounts that read content and actively use the product are the hottest opportunities. ## How much does it cost?

The cost of content marketing varies widely depending on how you resource it. Rather than quoting figures, it's more useful to make the resource requirements concrete.

**In terms of time:** An in-depth B2B guide of 1,500-2,500 words typically takes 8-15 hours, including research, expert interviews, writing, editing and visuals. For 4 pieces a month, you'll need 35-60 hours for production alone, or 50-80 hours including distribution. That's roughly a workload somewhere between a part-time and a full-time position.

**In terms of team structure, there are three options:**

1. **An in-house content lead:** This model offers the best understanding of the product and the customer, but it's hard for one person to manage strategy, production and distribution all at once.
2. **Freelance writers:** Flexible and scalable, but finding Turkish-language writers who specialize in B2B SaaS takes time. Oversight from an in-house editor or subject-matter expert is essential.
3. **An agency or external consultancy:** Provides strategy, production and measurement together. Well suited to getting started quickly, but you'll need to set aside regular time to transfer product knowledge to the agency.

For most early-stage companies, the most efficient model is a hybrid structure: one in-house person who owns the strategy, with external support for production. To weigh your content allocation against your overall marketing budget, our article discussing [what percentage of revenue a B2B SaaS marketing budget should be](/blog/b2b-saas-marketing-budget-percentage-of-revenue) can serve as a guide.

The key point to remember is this: unlike paid advertising, content is a compounding investment. When you stop your ad budget, the traffic stops, but a well-written guide can keep getting read and generating leads for years.

## How should you measure success?

When measuring content performance, it helps to think of metrics in three layers:

**Early indicators (first 1-3 months):**
- How often your sales team uses content in conversations
- Qualified engagement on LinkedIn content posts (comments from people in your target audience)
- Email newsletter open and click-through rates

**Mid-term indicators (3-6 months):**
- Organic rankings for target keywords
- Demo and contact form conversions from content pages
- Time on site and pages per session for visitors who read content

**Revenue indicators (6 months and beyond):**
- Number and value of opportunities where content was the first or last touchpoint
- Sales cycle length for opportunities that engaged with content
- Difference in win rate between opportunities that engaged with content and those that didn't

Because the B2B buying journey is long and involves many touchpoints, attributing revenue directly to a single piece of content is often misleading. Looking at the overall behavior of opportunities that engaged with content gives a more realistic picture.

## What are the most common mistakes Turkish SaaS companies make?

- **Publishing a product brochure as a blog post:** Content that mentions the product name in every paragraph and describes features rather than the problem doesn't get read or shared.
- **Translating English content word for word:** Translated content that lacks local examples, regulations and business practices feels foreign to Turkish readers.
- **Focusing on volume:** Three in-depth guides a month often deliver more value than 12 superficial posts.
- **Leaving the sales team out of the process:** Content that sales doesn't use realizes only a small fraction of its potential.
- **Giving up before six months:** Organic content compounds over time. Making a decision based on the first three months' results is usually premature.
- **Not updating old content:** Content about pricing, regulations or product features becomes outdated over time. Reviewing your most important pieces every quarter protects both reader trust and search visibility.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does B2B SaaS content marketing start delivering results?

The first effects can be seen within a few weeks once your sales team starts using the content. Meaningful visibility in Turkish organic search, however, typically takes 4-6 months. To properly assess the impact on revenue, you should plan for a period of at least 6-12 months.

### How many pieces of content should you publish per month?

In the early stages, 2-4 in-depth pieces a month is enough for most B2B SaaS companies. What matters isn't the number, but that each piece answers a real buyer question and is distributed consistently. Increasing volume once your cadence is established and performance data has accumulated leads to healthier results.

### Should I create content in Turkish or English?

If your target customers are in Turkey, Turkish content should be the priority, because the gap in Turkish B2B content gives you a competitive advantage. If you also sell internationally, localizing your best-performing Turkish content into English is more efficient than producing two separate sets of content from scratch.

### Does content marketing replace outbound sales?

No, the two complement each other. Outbound lets you proactively reach the right accounts, while content increases the credibility of those touchpoints and makes sales conversations easier. Outbound messages supported by valuable content are generally better received than messages that simply pitch the product.

## Conclusion

You don't need a large team or a big budget to start B2B SaaS content marketing in Turkey. What you need is a clear ideal customer profile, real questions gathered from your sales and support teams, a small number of pieces that answer those questions in depth, and a disciplined distribution plan that gets that content to the right people. Start with sales-adjacent content, put your sales team at the center of the process, treat the first 90 days as a learning period and let the data guide you.

The right way to choose a marketing agency for B2B SaaS is to look past the agency's creativity and focus on **how it contributes to your pipeline and revenue metrics**. A good B2B SaaS agency understands long sales cycles, subscription economics (MRR, CAC, LTV) and how to work hand in hand with a sales team. Start by clarifying your own goals and ideal customer profile, then compare agencies on industry experience, reporting transparency, CRM integration and contract flexibility.

Many SaaS companies treat choosing an agency like any other vendor purchase. They collect a few proposals and pick the one with the most persuasive pitch or the lowest price. A few months later, they have polished designs, rising clicks and packed reports, but the sales team complains that "most of the incoming leads aren't a fit for us." This article walks you through how to avoid that scenario step by step. We cover what to do before you start looking for an agency, the evaluation criteria, pricing models, red flags and how to manage the first 90 days of the partnership.

## Do you really need an agency?

This is the first question to ask before choosing an agency. An agency isn't always the right answer. For some companies, one or two good hires make more sense. For others, a short-term consulting project is the better fit.

An agency is usually the right choice in these situations:

- **You need to move fast:** Building an in-house marketing team can take months once you factor in hiring, onboarding and tool selection. An experienced agency can get campaigns live within a few weeks.
- **You need multiple areas of expertise:** Hiring separate specialists for SEO, paid ads, content, marketing automation and analytics is a major expense for an early-stage SaaS company.
- **You're testing a new channel or market:** If you're expanding from Turkey into Europe, for example, working with an agency that knows that market shortens the trial-and-error period.
- **You have an in-house team, but it lacks capacity:** An agency can add execution power on top of your existing team.

There are also situations where an agency isn't the right answer. If you haven't found product-market fit yet, your ideal customer profile is unclear, or your sales team isn't in a position to follow up on incoming leads, your agency budget will most likely go to waste. At this stage, it's far more valuable for founders to talk directly with customers, run a few manual outbound experiments and sharpen the messaging.

## What should you clarify before you start looking for an agency?

Companies that walk into a first agency meeting unprepared make it easy for the agency to sell its standard package. Don't request proposals until you've put the following four topics in writing.

### 1. A measurable business goal

"Increasing brand awareness" is a wish, not a goal. In B2B SaaS, goals should be tied to the sales funnel. Examples of goals include:

- Raising the number of qualified demo requests per month from the current level to a specific target over the next 6 months
- Adding a specific value of marketing-sourced opportunities to the pipeline
- Lowering customer acquisition cost in a specific segment (e.g., logistics companies with 50–500 employees)

Clarifying the distinction between MQLs and SQLs is critical here. An agency might say "we delivered 200 leads a month," but the real question is how many of those leads became opportunities your sales team accepted. If you haven't discussed this distinction with your team yet, [our guide to the difference between MQLs and SQLs in B2B SaaS](/blog/what-is-mql-mql-vs-sql-b2b-saas) is a good place to start.

### 2. Ideal customer profile (ICP) and buyer personas

However good an agency is, it can't know who you're selling to as well as you do. Gather the following information in a one-page document:

- Target industries, company size and geography
- The roles involved in the buying decision (decision-maker, user, approver, technical evaluator)
- Why customers choose you, and the most common objections in deals you've lost
- Average contract value and sales cycle length

### 3. Budget and expected payback period

Plan the agency fee and the advertising/media budget separately. If you haven't yet set your total marketing budget as a share of company revenue, [our article on B2B SaaS marketing budget as a percentage of revenue](/blog/b2b-saas-marketing-budget-percentage-of-revenue) will help you run the numbers. Also accept that in B2B SaaS, results usually take months to become visible, especially in channels like SEO and content. Don't expect your pipeline to explode in the first month.

### 4. Internal resources and division of responsibilities

Working with an agency doesn't mean "handing the work off." Someone inside the company needs to manage the agency, give approvals, share product knowledge and act as a bridge to the sales team. Make sure this person can dedicate at least a few hours a week to the agency partnership.

## Which type of agency is right for you?

"Marketing agency" is a broad umbrella. These are the main types of agencies B2B SaaS companies come across:

- **Full-service agencies:** They offer strategy, content, design, advertising and social media under one roof. The advantage is a single point of contact. The drawback is the risk that they can't offer deep expertise in every area.
- **Niche agencies specializing in B2B SaaS:** They work only with software and technology companies. They understand subscription metrics, trial/demo funnels and long sales cycles. They're usually more expensive, but the learning curve is short.
- **Performance marketing agencies:** They focus on paid channels such as Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads and retargeting. They're a good fit for rapid testing and measurable results.
- **Content and SEO agencies:** They drive long-term organic growth. For technical products, it's critical that they have writers who understand your industry.
- **Outbound and lead generation agencies:** They offer target account list building, cold email, LinkedIn prospecting and meeting booking. They're especially effective for SaaS companies with high contract values and a clear ICP.
- **Social media and LinkedIn-focused agencies:** They build trust and visibility by managing company pages and executive profiles.

For an early-stage SaaS company, the most efficient path is often to start with an agency that has deep expertise in a single channel and expand the scope as results come in. ## 7 critical qualities to look for in a B2B SaaS agency

### 1. A real understanding of the SaaS business model

In the first meeting, ask the agency how concepts like MRR, churn, CAC payback period and net revenue retention shape marketing strategy. An agency that can't answer this fluently most likely comes from the B2C or e-commerce world and will stay stuck on surface-level metrics like clicks and conversion rates.

### 2. A culture of working alongside the sales team

In B2B SaaS, marketing and sales can't be treated separately. A good agency wants to meet regularly with your sales team, get feedback on lead quality and update campaigns accordingly. Steer clear of any agency that says, "We don't need to talk to your sales team; we just deliver the leads."

### 3. CRM and attribution capabilities

If you can't track in your CRM where the agency's leads came from, what stage they're at and whether they turned into revenue, you can't measure the return on your marketing investment. Ask about the agency's experience with CRMs like HubSpot, Salesforce and Pipedrive, and how it sets up UTM structures and lead source fields. Keep in mind that measurement only means something when the data is clean. [Our article taking a strategic look at CRM data quality](https://saascorner.co/blog/b2b-saas-i-cin-crm-veri-kalitesi-stratejik-bir-bakis) can help you review your own setup before you start working with an agency.

### 4. The ability to produce technical content

SaaS products are often complex. The agency needs to understand your product and create content in your target audience's language. Ask for sample articles, case studies or ad copy. If possible, commission a small paid pilot (e.g., a single blog post or landing page copy) to test the quality firsthand.

### 5. Transparent reporting

Reports should show business results, not vanity metrics. A good monthly report answers these questions:

- How many qualified leads came in this month, and from which channels?
- How many of them were accepted by the sales team and turned into opportunities?
- What is the cost per lead and per opportunity by channel?
- What did we test, what did we learn and what will we change next month?

### 6. Team structure and continuity

The agency's founder or a senior strategist delivers the sales pitch, but the person managing your account may be someone else. Ask who will work on your project, what experience they have and how many clients they handle at the same time. An account manager juggling many clients means less attention for your account.

### 7. Proof of channel expertise

Every agency claims to be good at every channel. Ask for proof: have them walk you through the campaign structure they built for a similar B2B company, the hypotheses they tested and what they learned. For example, if organic growth matters to you, gauge how well the agency understands B2B search behavior in the Turkish market. ## How does the agency selection process work, step by step?

For most B2B SaaS companies, the following process can be completed in 4–6 weeks:

1. **Prepare a requirements document (Week 1):** Write a 2–3 page brief covering your goals, ICP, current channels, budget range, internal resources and expected start date.
2. **Build a long list (Week 1):** Ask SaaS founders in your network for referrals and research agencies on LinkedIn that focus on B2B SaaS. Start with a list of 8–10 candidates.
3. **Narrow it to a shortlist (Week 2):** Review their websites, published content, the industries they serve and their team structures to cut the list down to 3–4 agencies.
4. **Hold discovery calls (Weeks 2–3):** Schedule a 45–60 minute call with each agency. Use the question list below and record the answers in the same template.
5. **Request proposals and a strategy outline (Weeks 3–4):** Ask agencies for a proposal that includes not just pricing but also their approach for the first 90 days.
6. **Check references (Weeks 4–5):** Ask each agency for at least two references from current or former clients, and speak with those people yourself.
7. **Start with a pilot or short-term contract (Weeks 5–6):** If possible, start with a 3-month pilot period and set clear success criteria.

### Use a scoring table

To keep subjective impressions from driving the decision, create a simple scoring table. For example, you can rate each criterion from 1 to 5 and weight them:

- SaaS and B2B experience: 25%
- Strategy quality and 90-day plan: 20%
- Reporting and CRM capabilities: 20%
- Team and cultural fit: 15%
- Pricing and contract flexibility: 10%
- Strength of references: 10%

This approach ensures that the founder and the marketing and sales leaders involved in the decision are all speaking the same language.

## What questions should you ask in the agency meeting?

In discovery calls, these questions reveal an agency's true capabilities:

- "Have you worked with a B2B SaaS company with a similar sales cycle and contract value? What did you do in the first 3 months of that project?"
- "What metrics do you use to measure success? Number of leads, pipeline or revenue?"
- "Who will be on the team working for us, and how much time will they dedicate each week?"
- "What is your process when a campaign doesn't deliver the expected results?"
- "What kind of feedback do you need from our sales team, and how do you collect it?"
- "Who will own the ad accounts, content and data?"
- "What are the contract term and termination conditions?"
- "Can you tell us about a client you lost or parted ways with? What did you learn?"

The last question is especially valuable. An agency that can talk openly about its mistakes will also be honest with you when things aren't going well.

## How much does it cost? Pricing models

Agency fees vary widely depending on factors such as scope, agency size, depth of expertise and the market served. That's why giving a single number would be misleading. It's more useful to understand the common pricing models and when each one makes sense.

### Monthly fixed fee (retainer)

This is the most common model. The agency charges a fixed monthly fee for work within an agreed scope. It provides predictability, but if the scope isn't clearly defined, "that's not included in the package" disputes can start. Make sure monthly deliverables (e.g., 4 blog posts, 2 landing pages, weekly campaign optimization) are spelled out in the contract.

### Project-based fee

This suits work with a clear beginning and end, such as a website redesign, a brand positioning project or a marketing automation setup.

### Performance-based fee

The agency charges per lead, meeting or opportunity it delivers. It may sound attractive, but there are points to watch out for. If the definition of a "qualified lead" isn't spelled out very clearly in the contract, the agency may sacrifice quality to boost the numbers. This model generally works better for outbound meeting-booking services.

### Hybrid model

A lower fixed fee plus a performance bonus. It's often the most balanced model for aligning both parties' interests.

### Structuring the budget correctly: an example

Let's walk through a hypothetical example. Imagine a SaaS company with an average annual contract value of TRY 60,000 and a sales cycle of 2–3 months. The company allocates a total of TRY 120,000 per month to the agency and advertising. If this investment brings in 4 new customers a month, the marketing cost per customer is TRY 30,000, and each customer generates twice that cost in revenue within the first year. But if churn is high and customers leave after 8 months on average, the picture changes quickly.

This simple calculation shows that rather than judging an agency fee as "expensive" or "cheap" on its own, you should consider it alongside customer lifetime value and payback period. Also, in addition to the agency fee, add the advertising budget, tool licenses (marketing automation, analytics, design tools) and internal team time to your total cost.

## What red flags should you watch out for?

If you see several of the following signs at once, reconsider working with that agency:

- **Promises of guaranteed results:** Claims like "You'll rank first on Google in 3 months" or "100 demos a month, guaranteed" don't match the reality of B2B SaaS.
- **A focus on vanity metrics:** Constantly talking about impressions, followers and clicks in the pitch while never mentioning pipeline or revenue.
- **Keeping ownership of accounts and data:** Opening ad accounts in the agency's name, or not handing over content to you when the contract ends.
- **Long, inflexible contracts:** Demanding a 12-month commitment and heavy termination penalties for a first engagement.
- **The same package for everyone:** Offering a standard service package without listening to your brief.
- **Leaving the sales team out of the process:** An approach that "delivers" leads and doesn't care what happens next.
- **An unclear team:** No clarity on who will work on your account, or most of the work being outsourced without your knowledge.

## How should you manage the first 90 days of the partnership?

Choosing the right agency is only half the job. The other half is managing the partnership well. You can split the first 90 days into three phases.

### Days 1–30: Discovery and infrastructure

- Give the agency all relevant materials, including product demos, sales decks, customer call recordings and lost-deal analyses.
- Run a joint workshop with your sales team to review the ICP and messaging together.
- Set up lead source fields, the UTM structure and reporting dashboards in your CRM.
- Put the success criteria in writing: which metric, over what time frame and with what target.

### Days 31–60: Testing and learning

- Have the agency test 2–3 channel or messaging hypotheses with small budgets.
- Get feedback on lead quality from the sales team in short weekly sync meetings.
- Stop tests that aren't working quickly and shift budget to the promising ones.

### Days 61–90: Scaling and evaluation

- Scale the channels and messages that are working.
- Hold a thorough review meeting at the end of day 90: Where are we against our goals, what have we learned and what will we do differently next quarter?
- If the pilot period was successful, redefine the scope and contract term together.

The most common mistake during this period is evaluating results too early. For a SaaS company with a long sales cycle, it can take months for an opportunity sourced by the agency to close. That's why it's more realistic in the first 90 days to focus on leading indicators (number of qualified meetings, opportunity creation rate, the sales team's lead acceptance rate) rather than revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I choose a general digital agency or a SaaS-specialized agency for B2B SaaS?

If possible, choose an agency with B2B and SaaS experience. SaaS-specialized agencies already understand long sales cycles, subscription metrics and sales-marketing alignment, so the learning curve is shorter. If you work with a general agency, at least ask them to show concrete experience on a similar B2B project.

### How long does it take to see results from an agency?

It depends on the channel. With paid advertising and outbound, early signals usually appear within a few weeks, whereas meaningful results from SEO and content marketing often take several months. In the first 90 days, it makes more sense to track leading indicators such as the number of qualified meetings and opportunities rather than revenue.

### Do I need someone in-house when working with an agency?

Yes. Without an internal owner who manages the agency, gives approvals, shares product knowledge and acts as a bridge to the sales team, the partnership won't run efficiently. This person doesn't have to be a full-time marketer, but they need to be able to dedicate time to it every week.

### Is performance-based pricing always more advantageous?

Not always. The performance model only works well when the definition of a "qualified lead" or "valid meeting" is spelled out very clearly in the contract. Otherwise, the agency may compromise on quality to increase the numbers. For most B2B SaaS companies, a hybrid model made up of a lower fixed fee plus a performance bonus is a more balanced option.

## Conclusion

Choosing a marketing agency for B2B SaaS isn't about finding the one with the most impressive pitch. It's about finding a partner that understands your business goals, sits at the same table as your sales team and measures success in terms of pipeline and revenue. First clarify your goals, ICP and budget. Then compare agencies on SaaS experience, CRM and reporting capabilities, team structure and contract flexibility. If possible, start with a short pilot period, manage the first 90 days in a structured way and base your decision on data.

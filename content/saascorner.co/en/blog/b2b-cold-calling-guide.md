B2B cold calling means phoning the right decision-maker at a company that has never been in touch with you and booking a discovery meeting through a short, research-driven conversation. A successful cold call rests on four things: a clearly defined ideal customer profile, verified contact data, a flexible script focused on the prospect's problem, and a disciplined follow-up process that logs every call in your CRM. The goal is not to sell your product on the first call. The goal is to earn a second, 20-30 minute conversation.

At B2B SaaS companies, outbound teams usually lean heavily on email and LinkedIn and push the phone aside as "outdated" or "intrusive." Yet a well-prepared call can do in a few minutes what an email sequence that goes unanswered for weeks cannot: it starts a real-time dialogue, surfaces objections immediately and gives you instant feedback. In this guide, we walk through cold calling step by step, from preparation and scripting to objection handling and CRM metrics.

## Does B2B cold calling still work?

The short answer is yes, but not the way it did in 2010. Dialing a random list from top to bottom and reading everyone the same line genuinely doesn't work today. What does work is an approach we can call **"warm-cold" calling**. In this approach, the person you're calling doesn't know you, but you know them: you know what their company has been doing recently, which problems their role deals with and how your product addresses that problem.

The phone is especially powerful in B2B SaaS in the following situations:

- **High-ACV (high annual contract value) products:** When a single meeting is worth a lot, the time spent calling easily pays for itself.
- **Industries with low email response rates:** In sectors such as manufacturing, logistics, construction and healthcare, decision-makers do business over the phone far more than through their inboxes.
- **Short lists and ABM strategies:** If you have 200 target accounts, you need to reach each one through multiple channels. The phone is the most direct of those channels.
- **Market testing phases:** When entering a new segment, 50 conversations deliver richer insight than 5,000 emails.

## Step 1: Clarify who you're going to call

The quality of a cold call is determined before you ever pick up the phone. Calling the wrong person wastes even the best script.

### Put your ideal customer profile (ICP) in writing

Your ICP shouldn't be a vague definition like "mid-sized companies." Fill in the following questions with concrete answers:

- Which industries? (e.g., B2B software, 3PL logistics, pharmaceutical distribution)
- What employee count range? (e.g., 50-500)
- Which technologies do they use? (e.g., a specific CRM, a specific ERP)
- Which trigger events represent an opportunity for you? (a new funding round, a new sales director appointment, international expansion, a surge in job postings)
- Which companies should you never call? (existing customers, competitors' subsidiaries, very small businesses with no budget)

### Build your target account list

You need to turn your ICP into real company names. To start, a list of 100-300 accounts is large enough both to personalize your outreach and to get meaningful results. We explain in detail which criteria to filter the list by and how to segment it into tiers in our [guide to building a B2B target account list](/blog/how-to-build-b2b-target-account-list).

A practical tiering example:

- **Tier 1 (30 accounts):** Highest fit. In-depth research is done on each account, and 3-4 people are contacted.
- **Tier 2 (100 accounts):** Good fit. Moderate research is done, and 2 people are contacted.
- **Tier 3 (170 accounts):** Potential fit. Brief research is done, 1 person is contacted, and the script is more template-based.

## Step 2: Find the right person and the right number

The biggest time sink in B2B cold calling is waiting on switchboard lines and hearing "the person you're looking for isn't available right now." To avoid this, map three types of people at each account:

1. **Decision-maker:** The person who approves the budget (e.g., Sales Director, CRO, CFO).
2. **User / champion:** The person who will use the product daily and feels the problem most (e.g., Sales Operations Manager, Team Lead).
3. **Influencer:** The person who gives technical or procedural approval (e.g., IT Manager, Procurement).

When it comes to phone numbers, the order of priority is clear: **direct mobile number > direct desk line > switchboard.** You can use B2B data platforms to collect this data. However, no data source is 100% accurate. That's why you should make it a routine to flag wrong numbers during your first round of calls and clean up the list regularly.

## Step 3: Do 3 minutes of research before every call

The secret to warm-cold calling is short but focused research. The **"3x3 rule"** works well here: in 3 minutes, find 3 pieces of information about the person and company you're calling.

- **Company level:** Recent news, job postings (e.g., a "We're hiring 5 new SDRs" listing shows the sales team is growing), new product launches.
- **Person level:** Their recent LinkedIn posts, how long they've been in the role, their previous company.
- **Context:** A mutual connection, attendance at the same event, a shared industry development.

LinkedIn is the main source for this research. You can find ways to pull conversation openers from profiles and posts in our [guide to using LinkedIn for sales success](https://saascorner.co/blog/linkedin-ve-satis). Add the three pieces of information you find as a note on the contact record in your CRM. That way they're at hand during the call, and you can reuse them in your follow-up email.

## How do you write a cold calling script?

A good script isn't a text to be read word for word; it's the skeleton of the conversation. The following five-part structure is a solid starting point for B2B SaaS.

### 1. Opening (first 10 seconds)

Introduce yourself and be honest. The other person will realize it's a sales call within a few seconds anyway. Trying to hide it damages trust.

> "Hi Ayşe, this is Can calling from Company X. To be honest, this is actually a cold call. Would it be okay if I took 30 seconds to explain why I'm calling, and then you decide whether we keep talking?"

This **"permission-based opener"** gives the other person a sense of control and reduces the chance that they'll hang up immediately.

### 2. Reason for the call (research-based)

Use one piece of information from your 3x3 research here:

> "The reason I'm calling is this: I saw on LinkedIn that you've added three new SDRs to your sales team. When we talk to sales leaders who are scaling their teams quickly, we usually hear two things: new hires take a long time to generate pipeline, and it's unclear which leads to prioritize."

### 3. Problem question

Ask a question instead of pitching your product:

> "Does either of those sound familiar on your end, or is there a completely different priority?"

This question also leaves room for the other person to say "no, we don't have that problem." That's valuable information too, because you won't waste time following up with that account.

### 4. Brief value proposition

If the other person confirms a problem, give a one-sentence value proposition and a concrete example:

> "We offer a platform that solves exactly that. After automating lead prioritization, the sales team at a software company of similar size significantly reduced their reps' daily prep time."

If you have a real, shareable case study, use it. If not, keep it general and don't make up numbers. To create shareable case studies, take a look at our [guide to writing a B2B SaaS case study](/blog/how-to-write-b2b-saas-case-study).

### 5. A clear next step (CTA)

Instead of a vague "let me send you some information," suggest a specific time:

> "We could talk this through in a 20-minute call, tailored to your processes. Would Thursday at 2:00 PM or Friday at 10:30 AM work for you?"

Offering two options gets a higher acceptance rate than a "yes/no" question. Confirm the meeting with a calendar invite while you're still on the phone.

## How do you handle objections?

An objection isn't where the conversation ends; it's where it really begins. Here are the most common objections and response approaches that work:

- **"I'm not available right now."**
  "I understand, I'll keep it short. Is there a time this week when I could have 15 minutes of your time, or would next week be better?" Then make sure you lock in a specific time.

- **"Just send us an email."**
  This often means a polite rejection. "Of course, I'll send one. So I can send you something genuinely useful, may I ask just one question: how are you currently prioritizing leads?" This question can reopen the conversation.

- **"We already use a solution."**
  "Great, that shows this matters to you. I'm curious, what would you most like to see improved in your current solution?" Don't badmouth the competitor; let the prospect name the shortcomings.

- **"We don't have a budget."**
  "I understand. When does budget planning usually happen? Would it make sense to talk again closer to that time?" Log the answer as a reminder in your CRM.

- **"We're not interested."**
  "Understood, I respect that. One last question: is the reason you're not interested that you don't have this problem, or that you have other priorities right now?" Depending on the answer, either close the account or push it to a later date.

A suggested team exercise: List the 10 objections you hear most often, write 2 alternative responses for each and practice them in a 30-minute weekly role-play session.

## How many calls should you make per day?

There's no fixed "right number." The right number depends on your product, the quality of your list and the depth of your research. Still, to plan effectively, you need to measure each stage of the funnel with your own data. The following table is **an entirely hypothetical example scenario**; you should update it with your own rates:

- Daily call attempts: **60**
- Connect rate (someone picks up): **15%** → 9 conversations
- Conversation-to-meeting conversion: **20%** → approximately 2 meetings / day
- Weekly (5 days): approximately **9-10 meetings**

In this example, if your target is 15 meetings per week, you have two options: increase call volume or improve your connect and conversion rates. In practice, the second path is usually more sustainable. Better numbers raise your connect rate, while better research and scripting raise your conversion rate.

### How should you plan your calling blocks?

Cold calling becomes inefficient when it's done haphazardly. Here's the recommended structure:

- **Power hours:** Two uninterrupted 60-90 minute calling blocks per day. Email and Slack should be closed during this time.
- **Prep block:** 3x3 research for the next day's list, done the day before.
- **Batch processing:** Entering all notes and outcomes into the CRM within 15 minutes immediately after your calls.

Which hours are most productive varies by industry and role. Instead of memorizing a general rule, test different time slots during the first month and compare connect rates in your CRM.

## Should cold calling be used on its own?

No. The best results come from a **multichannel cadence** in which the phone works together with email and LinkedIn. The phone amplifies the impact of the other channels, and the other channels reduce the "coldness" of the phone.

An example 14-day cadence:

1. **Day 1:** LinkedIn profile visit and a connection request without a note.
2. **Day 2:** Cold call #1. If you can't reach them, leave a short voicemail.
3. **Day 2:** Personalized email #1 ("I just called; I'm writing about…").
4. **Day 4:** Cold call #2 (at a different time of day).
5. **Day 6:** A meaningful comment on one of their LinkedIn posts, plus a short message if the connection was accepted.
6. **Day 8:** Email #2 (sharing an insight or a relevant case study).
7. **Day 10:** Cold call #3.
8. **Day 14:** "Breakup" email ("Am I reaching the right person, or should I direct this to one of your colleagues?").

### Should you leave voicemails?

Yes, but keep them short. Don't go over 20-30 seconds. State your name, your company and your one-sentence reason for calling, and mention that you'll send an email. Instead of saying "please call me back," it's more effective to say "I'm sending you an email; my name will be in the subject line." That way, the voicemail becomes a bridge that increases the likelihood of your email being opened.

## How do you track cold calling results in your CRM?

A call that isn't logged is a call that wasn't made. Log the outcome of every call in your CRM with a standard "disposition" (outcome code):

- Not reached / voicemail left
- Wrong number
- Wrong person (referred to the right person)
- Spoke, not interested (with a note on the reason)
- Spoke, follow up later (with a reminder)
- Meeting booked

These codes let you track the following metrics weekly:

- **Connect rate:** Conversations / call attempts. If it's low, data quality or calling times are the problem.
- **Conversation-to-meeting rate:** Meetings / conversations. If it's low, the script, targeting or objection handling needs improvement.
- **Meeting show rate:** Meetings held / meetings booked. If it's low, your confirmation emails and reminder process are weak.
- **Meeting-to-opportunity rate:** Meetings that turn into sales opportunities. If it's low, you're booking meetings with the wrong people.

## What should happen after a meeting is booked?

If the SDR who makes the cold call and the AE (Account Executive) who runs the discovery call are different people, the handoff is a critical breaking point. As soon as a meeting is booked, the following should happen:

- The calendar invite is sent **the same day**, with an agenda and attendee names.
- The SDR enters the call notes (problem, current solution, budget cycle, decision process) into a standard template in the CRM.
- A short reminder email goes out 24 hours before the meeting.
- The AE reads the SDR's notes before the meeting and doesn't ask the prospect the same questions a second time.

## What should you watch out for legally?

When cold calling B2B prospects in Turkey, you need to keep two key regulations in mind: the Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK) and the legislation on commercial electronic messages. The general principles are as follows:

- Document the source from which, and the legal basis on which, you process personal data (first name, last name, mobile number).
- If the other person states during the call that they don't want to be contacted, log this in the CRM immediately and remove them from all cadences.
- The rules may differ for individual consumers versus buyers with merchant/tradesperson status. Get advice from your legal counsel specific to your situation.

## The most common cold calling mistakes

- **Reading the script like a robot:** The other person notices immediately. Memorize the script, then speak in your own words.
- **Pitching the product in the first 30 seconds:** The prospect doesn't yet know why they should listen. The problem should come first, then the solution.
- **Opening with "How are you?":** Asking this of someone you don't know often comes across as insincere and signals a sales call.
- **Giving up after one attempt:** Most connections require multiple attempts. Don't close an account before completing the cadence.
- **Not keeping records:** You can only know which script, which time slot and which segment works through data.
- **Sacrificing quality for volume:** 150 random calls a day usually generate fewer meetings than 50 researched calls.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a B2B cold call last?

An initial cold call should generally be kept to 2-5 minutes. The goal isn't to make a sale but to book a discovery meeting. If the conversation is running long and the prospect is interested, moving the details to a scheduled call shows respect for both your time and theirs.

### Which is more effective: cold calling or cold email?

They're not alternatives to each other; they're complementary. Email is scalable and leaves a trail, while the phone provides real-time dialogue and quick feedback. The best results usually come from multichannel cadences that combine both with LinkedIn.

### How many times is it reasonable to call one person?

Within a multichannel cadence, 3-4 call attempts spread over 2-3 weeks is considered reasonable. If the person clearly says they're not interested, stop calling immediately and record this in your CRM. You can revisit unresponsive accounts a few months later when a new trigger event occurs.

### Does it make more sense to build a cold calling team or outsource?

If you're still testing product-market fit, having founders or the people who know the product best make the first calls yields valuable insight. Once the process is clear and the need to scale arises, you should decide by comparing the cost and time of building an in-house team against the speed of working with an experienced external team.

## Conclusion

When done right, B2B cold calling is one of the fastest-feedback, most direct pipeline-generating channels for B2B SaaS companies. The formula for success is actually simple: a clear ICP, verified data, brief research before each call, a flexible problem-focused script, prepared objection handling and measuring every call in the CRM. Add a multichannel cadence and a seamless SDR-AE handoff, and cold calling turns from one-off attempts into a repeatable growth engine.

If you're choosing a B2B data tool in Turkey, here's the short answer: **If your outbound is email-led and you want data, sequences and CRM integration on a single platform, Apollo is usually the more sensible choice. If your sales team works primarily by phone and you want direct access to a small number of decision-makers, Lusha may be the better fit.** That said, both tools' data coverage in Turkey varies significantly by industry, company size and seniority level. The right decision therefore comes from a small pilot test with your own target list before you buy.

In this article, we won't compare the two tools feature by feature. Instead, we'll compare them through the everyday prospecting needs of a B2B SaaS company in Turkey. We'll walk step by step through data coverage, cost logic, KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law) and commercial electronic messaging rules, CRM integration, and a two-week testing plan.

## What do Apollo and Lusha actually do?

Both tools answer the same question: "Who is the right person at my target company, and how do I reach them?" But they approach that question from different angles.

### Apollo: A database and outbound platform in one

Apollo is a sales platform built on top of a large company and contact database. Its core components are:

- **Search and filtering:** Build target lists using filters such as industry, employee count, location, job title, department and technologies used
- **Contact data:** Business email addresses and phone numbers, unlocked through a credit system
- **Sequences:** Multi-step email, task and call workflows
- **Chrome extension:** Access contact details on LinkedIn profiles and company websites
- **Enrichment:** Fill in incomplete records in your CRM
- **Integrations:** Sync with HubSpot, Salesforce and similar CRMs

In other words, Apollo aims to bring everything from building a list to sending the first email into a single screen.

### Lusha: Fast, focused contact data

Lusha is positioned more around the need to "quickly reach the right person's direct contact details." Its standout features are:

- **Chrome extension:** One-click email and phone details while browsing LinkedIn
- **Direct phone numbers:** Known especially for its emphasis on mobile and direct-dial data
- **Prospecting search screen:** Build lists using filters
- **Enrichment and CRM integration:** Update existing records

Lusha has recently added engagement features as well, but it isn't positioned as an outbound platform as comprehensive as Apollo in terms of sequencing and automation depth. In short, you can think of Lusha as a "data tool" and Apollo as a "data + execution tool."

## Why is data coverage decisive in Turkey?

You'll find plenty of commentary on both tools' data quality in global reviews. However, most of these reviews are based on the US and Western European markets. The situation in Turkey is different, for several reasons:

1. **LinkedIn usage habits:** In Turkey, LinkedIn profiles are generally up to date, especially in technology, finance, consulting and large corporations. But in manufacturing, logistics, construction and family-owned businesses, many decision-makers have either never created a profile or haven't updated it in years. Since a large share of both tools' data relies on matching against LinkedIn and web sources, this directly affects coverage.
2. **Email formats:** Email formats in Turkish companies (firstname.lastname@, firstnamelastname@, firstinitiallastname@) vary from company to company. The conversion of Turkish characters (ş→s, ğ→g, ı→i) is sometimes applied inconsistently. This increases the bounce risk of guessed email addresses.
3. **Phone data:** Reaching decision-makers' mobile numbers through data tools is harder in Turkey than in the US. Switchboard numbers are easier to find, but getting through a switchboard to a general manager is a skill in its own right.
4. **SME segment:** Both tools' coverage drops noticeably for Turkish companies with 10–50 employees. Coverage improves as you move up to enterprise scale.

That's why generalizations like "Apollo's data is better" or "Lusha's phone numbers are more accurate" may not hold for your target audience. **The real question is which tool returns more accurate data for your ideal customer profile.**

## Which tool makes more sense in which scenario?

Let's think about the two tools not in the abstract, but through typical scenarios in Turkey.

### Scenario 1: Early-stage SaaS, one-person outbound

Picture an 8-person team in Istanbul building HR software. One of the co-founders spends a few hours a week on outbound and wants to reach HR directors at companies with 200–500 employees.

In this case, **Apollo** usually comes out ahead, because:

- You can start with a free or low-cost plan
- List building, email finding and sequence sending all happen in one place
- There's no need to pay for a separate email automation tool

### Scenario 2: Phone-led sales team

Picture a team of 4 SDRs selling ERP or field service management software to enterprise customers. The team usually makes first contact by phone and uses email as a supporting channel.

In this case, **Lusha** is worth considering, because:

- Its focus on direct phone numbers may better suit the needs of call-led teams
- Its fast-working LinkedIn extension fits well into SDRs' account-based research workflow
- When targeting a small number of high-value accounts, credit consumption can be kept under control

### Scenario 3: A Turkish SaaS company expanding abroad

Picture a company that has found product-market fit in Turkey and now wants to expand into Germany, the United Kingdom or the Gulf market. In this scenario, both tools' coverage is generally better than in Turkey. The choice again depends on your channel preference: Apollo for email and sequences at scale, Lusha for direct calling.

### Scenario 4: Account-based marketing (ABM)

If you're working a list of 50–100 target companies and trying to reach a 4–6 person buying committee at each, the two tools can also be used together. A common combination is using Apollo to map the org chart and build a broad contact list to put into sequences, while pulling phone data from Lusha for the 1–2 critical people. However, this approach doubles your budget, so it only makes sense when the potential revenue per account is high.

## How much does it cost?

Both tools work on a per-user monthly subscription and credit model. Since prices and credit allowances change frequently, always check the current figures on the official pricing pages. What matters here is less the price tag and more the **"cost per accurate contact"** calculation.

### Reading the credit model correctly

- **Apollo:** Offers limited email and phone credits on the free plan. On paid plans, email credits are relatively generous, while phone (mobile) credits are more limited. Sequencing and automation features unlock based on plan tier.
- **Lusha:** Provides a limited number of credits on the free plan. On paid plans, credits are used to unlock emails and phone numbers; unlocking a phone number usually consumes more credits than an email.

### Sample calculation: Cost per accurate contact

The figures below are an entirely **hypothetical** example; the goal is to illustrate the calculation method.

Let's say you pay $60 per user per month for a tool and unlock 500 contacts a month.

- An email was found for 350 of the 500 contacts
- After verification, 300 turned out to be valid
- Of those, 240 actually match your ideal customer profile (right title, right company size)

In this case, your cost per accurate contact is: 60 / 240 = **$0.25**.

Now consider the other tool: You pay $45 a month, but the same list yields only 150 accurate contacts. Cost per accurate contact: 45 / 150 = **$0.30**.

So the tool that looks cheaper may actually be more expensive for your target audience. Add the time SDRs waste on bad data, and the gap widens. If you're wondering how to factor this calculation into your overall lead costs, [our guide on how to calculate cost per lead (CPL) in B2B](/blog/b2b-cost-per-lead-cpl-calculation) is a good starting point.

### Watch out for hidden costs

- **Email verification:** Whichever tool you use, you may want to add an extra verification layer before sending.
- **Sending infrastructure:** Outbound requires separate domains, mailboxes and a warm-up period. This cost is independent of the data tool.
- **Annual commitment:** Annual billing is usually discounted, but committing to a year before you've seen the tool work in your market is risky.
- **Number of users:** As your team grows, per-user pricing multiplies quickly. Calculate in advance what a tool you started with 2 users will cost at 6 users.

## How should you test before buying?

The soundest way to decide is to test both tools side by side against your own ideal customer profile. The plan below can be completed in two weeks.

### 1. Prepare a test list

Create a sample of **100 people** who match your ideal customer profile. Distribute this list deliberately, not randomly:

- 40 people: Enterprise companies (500+ employees)
- 40 people: Mid-sized companies (50–500 employees)
- 20 people: SMEs (fewer than 50 employees)

In terms of job titles, include both your target decision-makers and influencers. If possible, already know the correct email address for some of these 100 people; this serves as a control group for measuring the tools' accuracy.

### 2. Search for the same list in both tools

Search for the same 100 people in the free or trial versions of Apollo and Lusha. For each person, record:

- Was an email found?
- Was a phone number found? (Switchboard, direct line or mobile?)
- Is the title and company information up to date?

### 3. Run verification

Run the emails you found through a verification tool. Compare them with the known emails in your control group. Call 10–15 randomly selected phone numbers to check their accuracy.

### 4. Run a small sending test

If you have a warmed-up sending infrastructure, send the same message to 30–50 verified contacts from each tool. Compare bounce rates, open rates and reply rates. This test shows not only whether the data is accurate, but whether it actually works in practice.

### 5. Score the results

A simple table like the one below will clarify your decision:

| Criterion | Weight | Apollo | Lusha |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email find rate | 25% | | |
| Email accuracy (bounce) | 25% | | |
| Phone coverage and accuracy | 20% | | |
| Ease of use | 10% | | |
| CRM integration | 10% | | |
| Cost per accurate contact | 10% | | |

Adjust the weights to fit your own sales model. For a phone-led team, it may make sense to raise the weight of phone coverage to 35%; for an email-led team, to lower it to 10%.

## How should you set up CRM integration and workflows?

A data tool doesn't create value on its own; value emerges from how the data flows into your CRM and sales process. A common mistake we see in Turkey is the data tool being used as an isolated island, with SDRs working out of Excel files.

### Steps for a healthy data flow

1. **Define a single source of truth:** The CRM should be the final home for contact and company records. The data tool should be used only as a feeder source.
2. **Map fields from the start:** Clarify the CRM equivalents of fields such as title, department, seniority, company size and industry before setting up the integration.
3. **Set deduplication rules:** The same person may come in from Apollo, from Lusha and from a web form. Define a merge rule based on email or domain.
4. **Add a source field:** Tag which tool each record came from. That's the only way to measure, six months later, which tool generated more opportunities.
5. **Connect it to scoring:** Firmographic data from the data tool (employee count, industry, technology usage) is a core input for lead scoring. We explain how to set this up in detail in our article on [building a B2B lead scoring model step by step](/blog/b2b-lead-scoring-model).

Apollo's advantage shows here too: because it can write sequence activity (sends, opens, replies) back to the CRM, sales managers can see the entire touchpoint history in one place. If you use Lusha, you may also need a separate engagement tool for sequences, along with its own CRM integration. ## What should you watch for regarding KVKK and commercial electronic messages?

When using data tools in Turkey, ignoring the legal side can make you pay dearly in the long run for the time you saved in the short run. The points below are a general framework; always consult your legal advisor about your specific situation.

- **Business email is personal data too:** An address like "ahmet.yilmaz@sirket.com.tr" identifies a specific individual, so it counts as personal data under KVKK. You need to document the legal basis on which you process this data.
- **Cross-border data transfer:** Apollo's and Lusha's servers are located outside Turkey. Uploading data from your CRM to these tools (for enrichment) may constitute a cross-border data transfer. Keep up with current legislation and Data Protection Board decisions on this matter.
- **Commercial electronic message rules:** In Turkey, the general rule for commercial electronic messages is prior consent. For messages sent to merchants and tradespeople, however, the legislation permits sending without prior consent under certain conditions, though the recipient always has the right to opt out. Clarify with your legal advisor which messages fall within this scope and what your İYS (Message Management System) obligations are.
- **Respond quickly to opt-out requests:** Offer a clear unsubscribe option in every email, and immediately remove anyone who opts out from all sequences and tools.
- **Data minimization:** Don't unlock or store data you don't need. This is both the right approach under KVKK and a way to protect your credit budget.

These rules don't directly change the choice between the two tools, but they do determine how you use them.

## What are the most common mistakes?

These are the recurring mistakes we see in how B2B SaaS teams in Turkey use data tools:

1. **Mistaking list size for success:** Pulling a 5,000-person list is easy. How many of them actually match your ideal customer profile is another question entirely. A well-segmented list of 300 people often brings in more meetings.
2. **Sending without verification:** Putting emails from a data tool straight into a sequence raises your bounce rate and damages your domain reputation.
3. **Relying on a single channel:** Using only email or only phone limits reply rates in Turkey. Multichannel approaches that combine email, phone and LinkedIn generally deliver better results. To see which channel works best for you, take a look at [our article comparing B2B lead generation channels](/blog/b2b-lead-generation-channels-compared).
4. **Neglecting Turkish-language personalization:** Translating the tools' ready-made English templates into Turkish and sending them makes the message sound robotic. Turkish decision-makers respond better to short, clear messages that speak the language of their own industry.
5. **Buying the tool without building the process:** Who will pull the lists, using which criteria, how many contacts will be unlocked, and who will manage which sequence? A tool bought without answering these questions ends each month with unused credits.

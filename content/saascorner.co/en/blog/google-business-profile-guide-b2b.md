Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is a **free** business profile that lets B2B companies manage their brand information, reviews and contact channels directly on Google Search and Google Maps. For B2B companies, its real value lies less in finding new customers and more in **building trust with buyers who search for your brand** and smoothing the verification step in the buying process. Every B2B company that has a physical office, meets customers face to face or serves a specific region should set up a profile and manage it regularly.

Many B2B SaaS companies assume this profile is only for local businesses like cafés or dental clinics, so they ignore it. Yet decision-makers on a buying committee search for a company's name on Google before a demo call or after receiving a proposal. If that search turns up an empty, unverified panel or one full of one-star reviews, months of sales effort can be undermined in seconds. In this guide, we take a B2B perspective and walk step by step through who the profile is right for, how to set it up, how to optimize it and how to measure its impact on your sales pipeline.

## Why does Google Business Profile matter for B2B companies?

In B2B, buying decisions aren't made with a single click. A CFO, an IT manager and an operations manager research the same vendor at different times and on different devices. What these searches usually have in common is that they're branded: queries like "company name + reviews", "company name + address" or "company name + contact".

At this point, Google Business Profile serves three critical functions:

- **It sends a trust signal:** A verified address, phone number, business hours and genuine customer reviews show that the company is real and active. For newly founded or growth-stage SaaS companies in particular, this signal is an indirect answer to an enterprise buyer's question: "Will this company still be around in two years?"
- **It takes up space in branded search:** The business panel, which appears in the right-hand panel on desktop and near the top on mobile, is one of the most eye-catching areas of the search results. If you don't manage this space, Google compiles information from other sources, and that information may be incomplete or inaccurate.
- **It provides visibility in local and regional searches:** Appearing in map results for searches like "Istanbul ERP consulting", "Ankara cybersecurity firm" or "Izmir industrial automation software" can generate direct demand, especially for B2B companies that offer installation, integration or field services.

You should think of the profile as part of your [B2B SaaS SEO strategy](https://saascorner.co/blog/b2b-saas-seo-rehberi-turkiye-adim-adim). While the content on your website grows organic traffic, your business profile ensures that traffic is met with accurate information at its first point of contact with your brand.

## Which B2B companies can create a profile?

The answer to this question is the most important point many SaaS companies overlook. According to Google's business profile guidelines, the profile is for businesses that **make in-person contact with customers during their stated business hours**. Businesses that operate only online, don't receive customers in person and don't visit customers are, in principle, not eligible.

On the B2B side, eligibility can be summarized as follows:

- **Eligible:** Companies with a genuine office where customer meetings take place and where staff are present during business hours.
- **Eligible (as a service-area business):** Companies that visit customers' offices to provide installation, training, consulting or technical support. In this case, the address can be hidden and only the provinces or regions served are shown.
- **Risky:** Virtual offices used only as mailing addresses. Coworking spaces are acceptable only if the business has permanent signage and staff present there during business hours.
- **Not eligible:** Fully remote SaaS companies with no office that never meet customers face to face.

If you're a fully remote team, creating a profile with a virtual office address may seem tempting. However, this significantly increases the risk of your profile being suspended. Reinstating a suspended profile takes time, and in the meantime you lose all control over your branded searches. Companies in this situation are better off focusing their energy on their website, LinkedIn company page and profiles on review platforms.

## How do you set up a profile? A step-by-step B2B setup process

Technically, setup takes 20–30 minutes; verification, however, can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few weeks depending on the method. The steps below provide the cleanest setup for a B2B company.

### 1. Start with the right account

Create the profile not with an employee's personal Gmail account, but with a corporate Google account tied to your company domain (e.g., marketing@yourcompany.com). This is critical to ensure ownership of the profile stays with the company when an employee leaves. Then add additional managers in the "People and access" section. Having at least two people with owner or manager permissions reduces the risk of losing access.

### 2. Enter your business name in its official form

Your business name should match the name you use on your signage, invoices and website. Keyword-stuffed names like "Acme Software | Best CRM and ERP Solutions Istanbul" violate the guidelines and can lead to your profile being suspended. The correct usage is simple and consistent: "Acme Software".

### 3. Set your address or service area

If you have an office where you receive customers, enter the address publicly. If you serve customers at their locations and don't receive customers at your office, hide your address and select provinces as your service area. For example, an Istanbul-based industrial IoT company could add Kocaeli, Bursa and Tekirdağ, where its installation team travels, as service areas.

### 4. Add your contact details

Use a phone number that actually gets answered and is routed to sales or the front desk. If a B2B buyer calls the number on your profile and gets lost in a phone tree, first impressions suffer. Don't forget to add UTM parameters to your website link (we cover this in detail in the measurement section).

### 5. Verify your profile

Google offers different verification methods depending on the type and location of the business: postcard, phone, email or video verification. Video verification has become more common recently. With this method, you're asked to show your business's location, signage, office interior and a document or piece of equipment proving the business is yours, all in a single uninterrupted video. Preparing a short script before filming reduces the chance of rejection: building entrance, floor or door number, company signage, office interior and a document belonging to the company.

### 6. Manage multiple locations

Companies with regional offices should create a separate profile for each location. Businesses with ten or more locations can apply for bulk verification. Giving each location its own phone number and, ideally, its own location page on your website helps distinguish the profiles from one another.

## How should you choose your category and description?

Category selection is one of the strongest signals determining which searches your profile appears in. You can select one primary category and up to nine additional categories.

### Primary category

Your primary category should be the single category that most accurately describes your company. For B2B SaaS companies, this is usually "Software company", or for service-heavy businesses, categories such as "Computer consultant", "Marketing agency" or "Business consultant". To see which categories your competitors use, you can search for your industry on Maps and check the categories of the top-ranking businesses.

### Additional categories

Additional categories should reflect services you actually offer. For example, for a company that both develops software and provides implementation consulting, the following combination could make sense:

- Primary: Software company
- Additional: Computer consultant, Website designer (if genuinely offered), Computer support and services

Adding categories for services you don't offer may bring in a few searches in the short term; however, visitors from irrelevant searches both eat up your sales team's time and damage your profile's credibility.

### Business description

You can write up to 750 characters in the description field. A B2B description should answer these four questions:

1. What do you do? (State the category and your core solution clearly.)
2. Who do you serve? (Industry, company size, role.)
3. What problem do you solve?
4. Which regions are you active in?

A sample structure: "Acme Software develops cloud-based maintenance management software for manufacturing companies with 50–500 employees. It provides implementation, integration and training services to reduce unplanned downtime, enable maintenance teams to manage work orders from mobile devices and track spare parts inventory. From our Istanbul office, we provide on-site support to customers in the Marmara and Aegean regions."

The description should not include discounts, URLs or exaggerated promises. A simple, informative text that speaks the buyer's language always performs better.

## How should B2B companies collect reviews?

Reviews are the most visible and most influential part of a business profile. Because B2B companies have far fewer customers than B2C companies, each review carries more weight. For a SaaS company with forty customers, fifteen quality reviews can make a significant difference in its industry.

### Choose the right moment to ask for a review

In B2B, the best moments to ask for a review are usually:

- After a successful onboarding process is completed,
- When the customer achieves their first tangible result (e.g., when the first monthly report comes back positive),
- Right after a contract renewal,
- After a support request is resolved quickly and satisfactorily.

Defining these moments in your CRM is far more effective than leaving review requests to personal initiative. For example, an automated task could be created as soon as a customer success manager marks the "onboarding completed" stage.

### Make the request easy

Add the short review link you get from your profile dashboard directly to your email or message. A good request message is short, personal and free of pressure:

"Hi Ayşe, thank you for your cooperation throughout the implementation process. If you'd like to share your experience with other manufacturing companies, it would mean a lot to us if you left a short review on our Google profile: [link]. Of course, it's entirely up to you."

### What not to do

- Offering discounts, gifts or perks in exchange for reviews violates the guidelines.
- Asking only customers you know are satisfied for reviews and filtering out the rest is also a practice that violates the guidelines.
- Having employees, partners or agencies write fake reviews leads to profile restrictions and reputational damage.

### How should you respond to reviews?

Respond to every review, positive or negative, within a few business days. For positive reviews, mention a specific detail from the review instead of using boilerplate phrases. For negative reviews, acknowledge the situation without getting defensive, offer a direct contact channel to resolve it and don't discuss the details in a public space. Remember: the person who really reads your response isn't the customer who wrote the review, but the potential buyer evaluating you.

## How should you use posts, products and services?

Your profile isn't just a static business card. A regularly updated profile shows both users and Google that the business is active.

### Posts

In the posts section, you can share updates, offers and events. Examples of posts that work for B2B companies:

- A brief announcement of a new product feature with a demo request link,
- An announcement of an upcoming webinar or industry event,
- A newly published guide, report or case study,
- A customer meetup or training day held at your office.

Posting once a week or every two weeks is a sufficient and sustainable rhythm for most B2B companies. Repurposing your blog, LinkedIn and newsletter content for your profile, rather than creating content from scratch, reduces the workload on your team. A content calendar built with [Social Media Corner's B2B LinkedIn management services](https://socialmediacorner.co) for regular, consistent LinkedIn content can easily feed into your business profile as well.

### Products and services

In the services section, add each of your core services separately with short descriptions, such as "ERP integration", "On-site user training", "Data migration" or "24/7 technical support". In the products section, you can list your plans or modules with visuals and short descriptions. If you don't share pricing publicly, you can leave the price field blank and direct people to "Request a quote".

### Photos

Use real photos of your office, team, events and product screens instead of stock images. For B2B buyers, seeing the team's faces reinforces the perception of "a company with real people behind it." Make sure your logo and cover image are the correct size and in line with your current brand identity.

## How do you measure performance and tie it to sales?

The performance screen in your profile dashboard shows how many people viewed your profile, which search terms they used to find you, website clicks, calls and direction requests. But for B2B, the real question is: how many of these interactions turn into qualified meetings and revenue?

### Add UTM parameters

Add a UTM with the following structure to your website link:

`?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`

This lets you separate visits from your business profile from general organic traffic in your analytics tool. Adding different UTMs to links in your posts on a per-campaign basis also shows you which content drives the most engagement.

### Prepare your form and CRM

When a visitor from your profile reaches your demo or quote form, the source information needs to flow correctly into your CRM. Capturing UTM data with hidden form fields and tagging it in your CRM as "Lead source: Google Business Profile" lets you see the channel's real contribution after a few months. It's also important that the form itself doesn't hurt conversions with unnecessary fields; [our guide on how many fields a B2B lead form should have](/blog/b2b-lead-form-field-count) is a good starting point on this topic.

### Track phone calls

In B2B, calls coming through the phone number on your profile are usually high-intent contacts. Having your front desk or sales team log these calls by asking "How did you hear about us?" is a simple but effective measurement method. It's also important to be clear about how quickly these contacts are handed over to the sales team; defining a [lead handoff SLA between marketing and sales](/blog/lead-handoff-sla-marketing-sales-alignment) keeps warm inquiries from your profile from going cold while they wait.

### Which metrics should you focus on?

- **Branded vs. non-branded search ratio:** Growing visibility in non-branded searches shows that your profile is generating new demand.
- **Website clicks / profile views ratio:** Measures how compelling your profile is.
- **Profile-sourced form submissions and calls:** Your direct demand contribution.
- **Profile-sourced opportunities and closed-won deals:** The real business outcome tracked in your CRM.

## What are the most common mistakes?

The most common mistakes B2B companies make when managing their business profile include:

1. **Setting up the profile and forgetting about it:** An old phone number, the address of an office you've moved out of or incorrect business hours erode trust at the buyer's first point of contact.
2. **Adding keywords to the business name:** Even if it brings visibility in the short term, it carries the risk of suspension.
3. **Using a virtual office address:** Addresses that don't comply with the guidelines cause verification problems and profile restrictions.
4. **Not responding to reviews:** Unanswered negative reviews in particular send potential buyers the message, "If I run into a problem, there'll be no one to talk to."
5. **Inconsistent information:** Differences in address, phone number and company name across your website, LinkedIn, industry directories and business profile confuse both users and search engines.
6. **Tying the profile to a single employee:** When that employee leaves, access is lost, and regaining ownership can take weeks.

## A 30-day implementation plan for B2B companies

To put the theory into practice, the four-week plan below offers a realistic starting point for most B2B companies:

**Week 1: Setup and verification**
- Create the profile with a corporate account or request ownership of an existing profile.
- Enter your business name, address or service area, phone number and website.
- Start the verification process and add a second manager.

**Week 2: Content and optimization**
- Define your primary and additional categories.
- Write a buyer-focused 750-character description.
- Fill out the services and products sections and upload at least 10 real photos.
- Add a UTM to your website link.

**Week 3: Review process**
- Compile a list of customers who completed onboarding or renewed their contracts in the last 6–12 months.
- Send personalized review request messages.
- Define the stages in your CRM that will trigger review requests.

**Week 4: Rhythm and measurement**
- Publish your first two posts and create a monthly posting calendar.
- Respond to all incoming reviews.
- Check that profile-sourced form submissions and calls are tagged correctly in your CRM.
- Prepare a monthly report template.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a remote SaaS company without an office create a Google Business Profile?

According to Google's guidelines, the profile is for businesses that make in-person contact with customers; companies that operate fully online and don't receive customers are not considered eligible. Profiles created with a virtual office address risk being suspended. Companies in this situation are safer focusing on their website, LinkedIn and review platforms.

### Does Google Business Profile generate leads directly for B2B companies?

For some B2B companies, especially those providing on-site services or seeing demand in regional searches, it can bring in direct form submissions and phone inquiries. However, for most B2B SaaS companies, its main contribution is building trust with buyers searching for the brand and strengthening the verification step in the sales process. UTM and CRM source tagging are essential to see this contribution.

### How many reviews do B2B companies need?

There's no fixed number; what matters is that your reviews are sufficient, recent and genuine compared to competitors in your industry. Reviews that arrive at regular intervals and describe concrete experiences are more reassuring than a large number of short reviews collected all at once. Responding to every review is just as important as the number of reviews.

### How can I prevent my profile from being suspended?

Use your business name in its official form, don't add keywords and only define a genuine address or service area that complies with the guidelines. Avoid fake or incentivized reviews, and keep your information consistent across all platforms. Take care not to make major changes to core information such as your address or name repeatedly within short periods.

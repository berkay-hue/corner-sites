Getting your first B2B sales job in Turkey comes down to three things: targeting the right entry-level roles (usually SDR, BDR or inside sales positions), proving you can communicate clearly in both Turkish and English, and running your job search the way a good salesperson runs an outbound campaign. You do not need a business degree or prior sales experience. You need evidence that you can research, reach out, handle rejection and follow up. Candidates who show that evidence in their applications and interviews get hired faster than those with better-looking CVs.

I have hired, trained and managed salespeople in Turkey for years, first as a rep myself and later as the founder of Corner Group. I have seen hundreds of applications from people trying to break into B2B sales. The ones who succeed follow a fairly predictable pattern, and the ones who struggle make the same handful of mistakes. This guide covers what works, step by step, with concrete actions you can take this month.

## What does an entry-level B2B sales job in Turkey actually look like?

Before you apply anywhere, understand what you are applying for. Most first B2B sales jobs in Turkey fall into one of four categories:

- **SDR / BDR (Sales or Business Development Representative):** You generate meetings for senior salespeople through cold calls, cold emails and LinkedIn outreach. This is the most common entry point, especially at SaaS companies and agencies.
- **Inside Sales Representative:** You handle the full sales cycle for smaller deals, mostly over phone and video. This is common in software, telecom, logistics and B2B services.
- **Junior Account Manager / Customer Success:** You manage existing customers, handle renewals and find upsell opportunities. It is less prospecting-heavy, but it still requires commercial thinking.
- **Export Sales Assistant / Foreign Trade Specialist (Dış Ticaret Uzmanı):** This role is common in Turkish manufacturing, textiles, food and machinery companies. You help the company sell to international buyers, often in English, German, Arabic or Russian.

There is also a growing fifth category: **remote roles for foreign companies**. Many European and US companies, along with agencies serving them, hire Turkish SDRs to sell into international markets. These roles typically require strong English and favor people who are comfortable with tools like CRMs, sequencing platforms and AI research assistants.

Geographically, Istanbul still has the highest concentration of B2B sales jobs, followed by Ankara and Izmir. Remote and hybrid roles have widened the map considerably, though. If you live in Eskişehir or Antalya, you are no longer locked out.

## Do you need a sales background or a specific degree?

No. This is the single most important thing to understand. B2B sales is one of the few well-paid career paths in Turkey where employers regularly hire people with no direct experience, as long as they show the right traits.

Some of the best reps I have worked with studied engineering, international relations, literature or tourism. My own background is in civil engineering. I wrote about that shift in [my journey from civil engineering to sales](/blog/from-civil-engineering-to-sales-a-journey-of-self-discovery). A technical degree can be an advantage when you sell to technical buyers, because you understand their problems.

What employers actually screen for:

1. **Communication:** clear, confident speaking and concise writing.
2. **English level:** for many B2B roles, especially in SaaS and export, working English is non-negotiable.
3. **Coachability:** do you take feedback and change your behavior quickly?
4. **Resilience:** can you hear "no" 40 times a day and still make call number 41 with energy?
5. **Curiosity:** do you ask good questions about the customer's business?
6. **Basic tool literacy:** spreadsheets, LinkedIn, a CRM, and increasingly AI tools.

If you have worked in retail, hospitality, call centers, student clubs or event organization, those experiences count. Frame them in sales language: targets you hit, people you persuaded, problems you solved.

## How should you prepare before applying?

Most candidates apply first and prepare later. Reverse that. Here is a 30-day plan I would give anyone starting from zero.

### Week 1: Learn the vocabulary and the process

Learn the basic B2B sales vocabulary: ICP (ideal customer profile), pipeline, discovery call, objection handling, MQL vs SQL, quota, conversion rate, sales cycle. Pick one structured methodology, such as BANT, MEDDIC or SPIN, and understand it well enough to explain it in two minutes.

Read real material from practitioners as well. Blogs written by people running sales teams will teach you more about day-to-day reality than generic career sites.

### Week 2: Get hands-on with tools

Create free accounts on a CRM (HubSpot's free tier works), a prospecting database, and an AI assistant. Practice the following:

- Building a list of 25 companies that fit a specific profile (for example, "Turkish e-commerce brands with 20 to 200 employees").
- Finding the right decision-maker at each one.
- Writing a short, personalized first-touch email for five of them.

You are not sending these emails. You are building a portfolio you can show in interviews.

### Week 3: Practice talking

Record yourself doing a 60-second cold call opener and a two-minute pitch for a product you know well. Listen back. Most people discover they speak too fast, use filler words ("yani", "şey", "actually") and never ask a question. Fix those three things and you will already be ahead of most applicants.

If you can, practice with a friend in sales. Ask them to raise common objections: "We're not interested," "Send me an email," "We already work with someone."

### Week 4: Rebuild your CV and LinkedIn

Rewrite your CV around outcomes, not duties. Compare:

- Weak: "Responsible for customer communication at a café."
- Strong: "Handled 150+ customers per shift; increased average order value by recommending add-ons."

Only use numbers you can defend in an interview. Then update your LinkedIn headline to something forward-looking, such as "Aspiring SDR | B2B SaaS & Outbound | Turkish & English." Post two or three short reflections on what you are learning. Hiring managers in Turkish B2B companies do check LinkedIn, and visible effort stands out. If you want to see what strong B2B LinkedIn presence looks like, study how agencies like [Social Media Corner manage LinkedIn for B2B brands](https://socialmediacorner.co). The same principles of consistency and relevance apply to personal profiles.

## Where do you find B2B sales jobs in Turkey?

Use several channels in parallel:

- **LinkedIn Jobs:** the main channel for SaaS, tech and international roles. Search terms: "SDR," "BDR," "Inside Sales," "Satış Temsilcisi," "İş Geliştirme Uzmanı."
- **Kariyer.net and Yenibiris:** stronger for traditional sectors like manufacturing, distribution, FMCG and export.
- **Startup ecosystems:** Turkish startup communities, accelerator portfolios and tech meetups often surface jobs before they are posted publicly.
- **Agencies and outsourced sales companies:** firms that run outbound for other companies hire junior reps regularly and train them fast. This is one of the best places to learn, because you see many industries in a short time.
- **Direct outreach:** the most underused channel, covered in the next section.

## How do you treat your job search like an outbound campaign?

Here is the approach that separates serious candidates from everyone else. Instead of waiting for job postings, prospect hiring managers the same way an SDR prospects buyers.

1. **Define your target list:** pick 40 to 60 companies that sell B2B products or services and are likely to grow their sales teams. Good signals include recent funding, new market launches, or multiple open sales roles.
2. **Find the right person:** usually the Head of Sales, Sales Manager or founder at smaller companies, not HR. Prospecting tools like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) let you find these people and their contact details, the same way working SDRs do.
3. **Research before writing:** spend five minutes per company. What do they sell, who do they sell to, and what changed recently?
4. **Send a short, specific message:** for example:

> "Hi Elif, I saw that [Company] recently started selling into Germany. I'm looking for my first SDR role and I've been practicing outbound for the logistics sector. I put together a list of 20 German mid-market companies that look like a fit for your product, with notes on why. Happy to send it over. Would a 15-minute chat next week make sense?"

5. **Follow up:** send two or three follow-ups over about two weeks, each adding something new. Most replies come after the first message, not from it.

Even if they do not hire you, many sales leaders will remember the candidate who prospected them well. I have personally hired people because their outreach to me was better than the outreach some of my existing reps were sending to prospects.

## What happens in a B2B sales interview?

Sales interviews in Turkey usually have three to four stages:

1. **HR or recruiter screen (15–30 minutes):** motivation, salary expectations, English check.
2. **Hiring manager interview (45–60 minutes):** your background, how you handle rejection, why sales, why this company.
3. **Role-play or case:** a mock cold call, an email-writing task, or a short pitch of the company's product.
4. **Final round:** often with a founder or senior leader, focused on culture fit and ambition.

The role-play is where most first-time candidates win or lose the job. Prepare by learning the company's product, its typical customer and two or three likely objections. In the role-play, ask questions before pitching. A candidate who asks "What does your current process look like?" almost always outperforms one who launches straight into features.

It also helps to see the process from the other side. My article on [the questions I ask in a sales rep interview](/blog/sales-rep-interview-questions) shows what hiring managers are really trying to find out. Prepare an honest, specific answer to each one.

At the end, ask your own strong questions: "How is ramp time structured?", "What does a successful rep look like here after six months?", "How is commission calculated and paid?"

## How much can you expect to earn?

Compensation for entry-level B2B sales in Turkey usually combines a **fixed base salary** and a **variable component** (commission or bonus tied to meetings booked, pipeline generated or revenue closed). Because of inflation, base salaries are often revised once or twice a year. Ask how and when raises happen.

Several factors shape your offer:

- **Sector:** SaaS, tech and international roles typically pay more than traditional distribution sales.
- **Currency:** remote roles for foreign companies sometimes pay in EUR or USD, which matters a great deal in the Turkish context.
- **Language:** fluent English, and especially a second foreign language like German, increases your value.
- **Variable structure:** a lower base with an uncapped, achievable commission can beat a higher fixed salary.

## What red flags should you watch for?

Not every "sales job" is a good first job. Be careful with:

- **Commission-only roles:** with no base salary, you carry all the risk while the company invests nothing in you.
- **Vague product descriptions:** if they cannot explain what you will sell and to whom, walk away.
- **No onboarding plan:** good companies can describe your first 30, 60 and 90 days.
- **Unrealistic promises:** "You'll earn a fortune in your first month" is a warning sign, not a perk.
- **High turnover:** check LinkedIn to see how long previous reps stayed.

## What should you focus on in your first 90 days?

Once you land the job, the goal is to ramp fast and build a reputation for reliability:

- **Days 1–30:** learn the product, the ICP and the playbook. Listen to as many recorded calls as you can.
- **Days 31–60:** hit your activity targets consistently. Ask for feedback weekly.
- **Days 61–90:** focus on quality: conversion rates, meeting show-up rates, pipeline value.

Track your own numbers from day one. When the conversation about promotion comes, data wins. My guide on [moving from SDR to Account Executive](/blog/move-from-sdr-to-account-executive) explains what that next step requires and how long it typically takes.

## FAQ

### Can I get a B2B sales job in Turkey without speaking English?

Yes, especially in domestic-facing sectors like distribution, manufacturing and local services, where Turkish is the main working language. However, many of the higher-paying roles in SaaS, export and remote work for foreign companies require at least working English. Improving your English is one of the highest-return investments you can make for a sales career in Turkey.

### How long does it take to land a first B2B sales job?

It varies, but candidates who prepare properly and use direct outreach alongside job boards often get offers within one to three months. Those who only apply to postings with a generic CV usually take longer. Treating the search as a structured outbound campaign shortens the timeline significantly.

### Is an SDR role a good start, or should I aim for account executive directly?

For most people with no sales experience, an SDR or BDR role is the better starting point. It teaches prospecting, objection handling and pipeline discipline, which every closer needs. Strong SDRs in Turkey commonly move into account executive roles within one to two years.

### Should I start at a startup or a large company?

Large companies usually offer structured training and stability, while startups offer faster learning, broader responsibility and quicker promotion paths. If you are coachable and comfortable with ambiguity, a well-run startup or outbound agency can accelerate your growth. Either way, prioritize a manager who will actually coach you over a famous brand name.

## Conclusion

Breaking into B2B sales in Turkey is very achievable, even with no experience and an unrelated degree. Target the right entry-level roles, build real skills with real tools, rewrite your CV around outcomes, and above all prospect your future employers the way a good SDR prospects buyers. The habits that get you hired are the same habits that will make you successful once you are in the seat.

**For most early-stage B2B companies, outsourced SDRs are the better first move when you need pipeline in the next 90 days and haven't yet proven which message and market work. In-house SDRs are the better choice once you have a repeatable playbook, a manager who can coach, and enough deal volume to justify building institutional knowledge inside the company.** The real answer is rarely "one forever." It is usually "outsource to learn, then in-house to scale," or a hybrid of both.

I'll be upfront about my bias. Through [SAAS Corner](https://saascorner.co) and [Sales Corner](https://salescorner.co), we run outbound for B2B companies, so I have a commercial interest in outsourcing. I have also built in-house teams, hired SDRs who became excellent, and watched outsourced engagements fail because the client wasn't ready for them. This piece is meant to help you choose well, even if that means choosing to hire your own people.

## What's the real difference between outsourced and in-house SDRs?

On paper, both do the same job: research accounts, reach out by email, phone, and LinkedIn, qualify interest, and book meetings for an account executive or founder. In practice, they differ in five ways that matter more than the job description.

- **Speed to first meeting.** An agency with existing infrastructure (domains, warmed inboxes, data tools, trained reps) can usually launch in two to four weeks. An in-house hire means a recruiting process, a notice period, onboarding, and a ramp period. Realistically, you are looking at three to five months before a new SDR is fully productive.
- **Who owns the learning.** When an in-house SDR discovers that CFOs respond better than CTOs, or that one objection keeps coming up, that insight stays in your building. With an agency, you only keep it if you deliberately extract it.
- **Management load.** In-house SDRs need daily coaching, call reviews, and 1:1s. An agency handles most of that, but you still need to manage the agency.
- **Fixed vs. variable cost.** Salaries are fixed and hard to reverse. Agency retainers can usually be paused or ended with notice.
- **Depth of product knowledge.** Your own SDR will eventually understand your product, customers, and edge cases far better than an external rep who splits attention across several clients.

Every other argument flows from these five differences.

## How much does each option actually cost?

Founders tend to compare an agency's monthly fee to an SDR's salary. That comparison is misleading. The fair comparison is **total cost per qualified meeting over the first six to twelve months**.

Here's an illustrative example. Plug in your own numbers, because salaries and agency fees vary widely by market.

**In-house SDR (first 6 months):**
- Gross salary plus employer costs: say 2,500 per month, so 15,000 over six months
- Recruiting time or fees: equivalent of roughly one month's cost, so 2,500
- Tools (data provider, sequencing tool, dialer, LinkedIn seat): around 300 per month, so 1,800
- Manager time for coaching (often the founder): easily 5–8 hours per week, which has a real opportunity cost
- Ramp: month one produces almost nothing, months two and three produce partially, so only about three productive months

**Outsourced SDR (first 6 months):**
- Retainer: say 3,000 per month, so 18,000
- Setup fee, if any: varies
- Your time: roughly 1–2 hours per week for feedback, call reviews, and approvals
- Ramp: often productive by week three or four, so roughly five productive months

Now say both produce around 10 qualified meetings in a productive month. The in-house SDR delivers about 30 meetings in six months at roughly 19,300 plus your coaching time. The agency delivers about 50 meetings at 18,000 plus less of your time. In this scenario, outsourcing wins in the first six months.

Run the same math for months 7–18 and the picture often flips. The in-house SDR is fully ramped, knows your product deeply, and may produce more meetings at a lower cost per meeting. The agency fee stays the same.

If you're hiring in Turkey specifically, salary expectations, SGK contributions, and benefits change the math considerably. I broke those down in [what a sales rep really costs in Turkey](/blog/what-does-a-sales-rep-cost-in-turkey).

## When does outsourcing make more sense?

Outsourcing tends to win in these situations:

1. **You haven't validated your outbound motion yet.** If you don't know which ICP segment, title, or message converts, you're running experiments. Agencies are good at running many experiments quickly because they've done it across many markets. Hiring a full-time employee to run experiments that might show outbound doesn't work for you is an expensive way to learn.
2. **You need pipeline quickly.** You may have a fundraise coming, a new market launch, or a board asking about pipeline coverage. A three-to-five-month hiring and ramp cycle is too slow.
3. **There's nobody to manage an SDR.** This is the most underrated factor. A junior SDR with no manager almost always underperforms, and then the founder concludes "outbound doesn't work for us." If the founder is still closing every deal and has no time to coach, an agency's built-in management is worth paying for.
4. **You're testing a new geography.** Entering Germany, the Gulf, or the UK from Turkey? An external team with local language skills and market experience lets you test demand before committing to a local hire and entity setup.
5. **Your volume is too low for a full-time role.** If your total addressable market is a few hundred accounts, a full-time SDR may run out of accounts within months. A part-time outsourced engagement fits better.

## When should you build in-house instead?

In-house tends to win in these situations:

1. **You have a proven playbook.** You know your ICP, your best-performing sequences, your reply rates, and your meeting-to-opportunity conversion. Now you need to execute at scale. If you haven't nailed your ICP yet, start with [defining your ideal customer profile for B2B](/blog/define-ideal-customer-profile-b2b) before you decide on either option.
2. **Your product is complex or highly technical.** If prospects ask detailed technical questions on the first call, an SDR who lives inside your company will handle them far better.
3. **You have a sales leader who can coach.** In-house SDRs thrive with a manager who runs weekly 1:1s, reviews calls, and builds a learning loop.
4. **You want a talent pipeline.** Many of the best account executives started as SDRs at the same company. In-house SDRs become your future closers, and that is very hard to replicate with an agency.
5. **Outbound is core to your go-to-market for years.** If outbound will be your main growth channel long-term, owning that capability is a strategic asset.

## What does a hybrid model look like?

Many of the companies I've seen succeed don't choose one or the other. They sequence the two or run them side by side. Three common patterns:

- **Outsource to learn, then hire.** Run an agency for 6–9 months. Use the data to define your ICP, best messages, and realistic conversion rates. Then hire in-house SDRs with a ready-made playbook so they ramp much faster.
- **In-house core, outsourced expansion.** Your internal SDRs cover your core market and top-tier accounts. An agency handles a new vertical, a new country, or lower-priority segments.
- **Split by task.** The agency handles list building, data enrichment, and top-of-funnel email. Your in-house SDR handles replies, phone follow-ups, and qualification. This keeps the conversation with interested prospects inside your company.

It's also worth asking how much of the SDR workload AI can now take on. Research, enrichment, and first drafts have changed significantly, but judgment and conversations still need people. I covered where the line sits in [can AI replace your SDRs](/blog/can-ai-replace-your-sdrs).

## How do you evaluate an outsourced SDR provider?

If you decide to outsource, the provider matters more than the model. Here's the process I'd follow.

1. **Ask how they define a "qualified meeting."** Get it in writing. A meeting with an intern who accepted out of politeness is not the same as a meeting with a decision-maker who has a relevant problem. Agree on criteria such as title, company size, confirmed pain, and attendance.
2. **Ask who will actually do the work.** Will you have a dedicated rep or a shared pool? How many other clients does that rep handle? What is their language level in your target market?
3. **Ask about infrastructure and deliverability.** Do they send from separate domains so your main domain's reputation is protected? How do they warm inboxes? Where does their contact data come from? Many teams build lists with tools like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) and then verify and enrich them. Ask what their verification step looks like.
4. **Ask for reporting you can audit.** You should see weekly activity, reply rates, positive replies, meetings booked, and meetings held. You should also have access to the actual conversations, not just a summary.
5. **Check contract flexibility.** A 12-month lock-in before they've proven anything is a red flag. Look for a defined pilot period with a clear exit.
6. **Ask what happens to the assets.** Who owns the lists, sequences, and learnings if you end the contract? You should.

## How should you run a 90-day test?

Whether you outsource or hire, treat the first 90 days as a structured experiment rather than an act of faith.

**Days 1–30: Set up and launch.**
- Agree on ICP, target accounts, personas, and qualification criteria
- Approve messaging and sequences
- Set baseline targets, for example a minimum number of accounts contacted and a target positive reply rate based on what's realistic for your segment

**Days 31–60: Iterate.**
- Review replies weekly, not just meetings booked
- Kill underperforming messages and double down on what's working
- Check meeting quality. Are your AEs or founders getting real opportunities from these meetings?

**Days 61–90: Decide.**
- Calculate cost per qualified meeting and cost per opportunity
- Compare meeting-to-opportunity conversion against your inbound or referral baseline
- Decide whether to scale, adjust, or stop

The metric that matters most is not meetings booked but **pipeline generated per unit of cost**. If you're not sure which numbers to watch, I wrote about [the sales metrics a founder should track](/blog/what-metrics-should-founder-track-sales).

## What mistakes do founders make with either option?

These are the mistakes I see most often:

- **Hiring a junior SDR with no manager.** This is the most common and most expensive mistake on the in-house side.
- **Outsourcing and then disappearing.** An agency without your feedback is guessing. Fifteen minutes a week reviewing replies and meeting notes changes results significantly.
- **Judging too early.** Outbound needs enough volume and iterations to produce a signal. Two weeks is not a test.
- **Paying per meeting without quality controls.** Pay-per-meeting models can incentivize volume over quality. They can still work, but only with strict qualification definitions.
- **Never transferring knowledge.** If you outsource, document every insight about which titles respond, which pains resonate, and which objections recur. That document becomes the onboarding manual for your future in-house team.

## FAQ

### Is outsourcing SDRs cheaper than hiring in-house?

In the first six months it often is, because an agency skips recruiting and ramp time and usually becomes productive within a few weeks. Over 12–18 months, a well-managed in-house SDR can deliver a lower cost per meeting. Compare total cost per qualified meeting, not monthly fee against salary.

### How long does it take an outsourced SDR team to produce results?

A good provider can typically launch within two to four weeks and start booking meetings within the first month or two. You need around 90 days to judge results fairly, because messaging and targeting usually need a few rounds of iteration.

### Can I switch from outsourced to in-house later?

Yes, and it's one of the most effective paths. Make sure your contract gives you ownership of lists, sequences, and learnings, and document what works during the engagement. Your first in-house SDR will ramp much faster with a proven playbook.

### Should a founder still be involved if SDRs are outsourced?

Yes. The founder or sales lead should review replies and meeting quality weekly and give clear feedback on fit. Outsourcing removes execution work from your plate, but it doesn't remove the need for your judgment about who is a good customer.

## Conclusion

The outsourced-vs-in-house question is really a question about where you are. If you're still figuring out who buys and why, need pipeline soon, or have nobody to coach a junior rep, outsourcing is usually the smarter, more reversible bet. If you have a proven playbook, a capable sales manager, and a long-term commitment to outbound, building in-house creates an asset that compounds over time. Many companies do best with both: outsource to learn quickly, then hire to scale.

Whichever route you choose, define what a qualified meeting means, measure pipeline rather than activity, and give it 90 days of honest iteration.

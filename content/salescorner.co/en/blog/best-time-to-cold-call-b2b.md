In B2B cold calling, the most productive windows are generally **Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, between 10:00–11:30 in the morning and 15:30–17:00 in the afternoon.** Monday mornings, lunchtime and Friday afternoons are the weakest slots for most teams. That pattern is only a starting point, though. Your best time depends on the roles and industries you target and on your own call data.

In this guide, we'll first explain why the general pattern works. Then we'll walk through what's different in Turkey, recommended times by role and industry, and how to find your own "golden hours" with data, step by step. The goal is to build a calling schedule that is measurable and repeatable rather than left to chance.

## The short answer: What's the best time and day?

The pattern sales teams have observed for years, and that is widely accepted in the industry, can be summarized as follows:

- **Best days:** Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. Midweek, decision-makers are on top of their agenda and open to new conversations.
- **Best morning window:** 10:00–11:30. The first meetings and the morning email rush are over, and lunch hasn't started yet.
- **Best afternoon window:** 15:30–17:00. Most of the day's work is done, gaps open up in the calendar and people start planning the next day.
- **Weak windows:** Monday 09:00–11:00 (weekly planning and meetings), 12:00–14:00 (lunch break), Friday after 14:00 (wrapping up the week).

This pattern involves two critical metrics, and you need to keep them separate:

1. **Connect rate:** The share of calls that get picked up.
2. **Conversation quality:** How open the person who answers is to listening, asking questions and agreeing to a meeting.

A general manager picking up the phone at 08:30 is a good result in terms of connect rate. But if their mind is full of the day's urgent tasks at that hour, conversation quality may be low. Good timing means optimizing both metrics together.

## Why does a decision-maker's daily rhythm matter so much?

The way to understand the best time is to picture the day of the person you're calling. A typical B2B decision-maker's day roughly unfolds like this:

- **08:30–10:00:** Emails, urgent requests, morning meetings. An unknown number calling at this hour usually falls into the "I'll deal with it later" category.
- **10:00–11:30:** The most focused and energetic part of the day. The morning fires have been put out, and there is mental space to hear a new idea.
- **12:00–14:00:** Lunch, external meetings, business lunches. Even if the phone is answered, the conversation is short and distracted.
- **14:00–15:30:** The afternoon meeting block. In many companies, internal meetings pile up in these hours.
- **15:30–17:00:** The day's wrap-up window. Meetings are over and the person is back at their desk. While planning the next day, they are more open to a new meeting request.
- **After 17:30:** Sometimes a surprisingly good window for senior executives. Assistants and team members have left, and the executive answers the phone personally.

Understanding the daily rhythm also lets you segment your call list in a meaningful way. Instead of calling everyone at the same time, the goal should be to call each role in its own optimal window.

## Why are cold calling times different in Turkey?

"Best time" recommendations from international sources don't map directly onto Turkey. Local business culture has a direct impact on the schedule.

### Lunch breaks are longer and more flexible

In many Turkish companies, lunch takes place between 12:00 and 13:30. For senior executives, however, business lunches can stretch to 14:00 or even 14:30. That's why starting afternoon calls before 14:30 often means wasted dials.

### Friday midday

Because of Friday prayers, 12:00–14:00 on Fridays is effectively a dead zone in many industries. Calling in this window is especially unproductive with companies in manufacturing, industry, construction and traditional trade. Friday morning, however, is usually still a usable window.

### Ramadan, the eves of religious holidays and bridge days

- **During Ramadan**, the working day effectively gets shorter. Calling close to iftar (especially in the last hour or two) is both unproductive and can sometimes come across as impolite. During this period, it makes more sense to focus on the morning window.
- **The eves of religious holidays and bridge days** are usually treated like "half days." Rather than scheduling large call blocks on these days, it's more productive to do list cleanup, data enrichment and sequence preparation.
- **Late July and August** are periods when reaching decision-makers gets harder, especially at large companies, due to the concentration of annual leave.

### The quarter-end and year-end effect

For finance, procurement and operations roles, month-end, quarter-end and December are dominated by closing work. Calling the same person in the first two weeks of the month usually works better than calling in the last week.

## Which role should you call at what time?

Even within the same company, different roles work to different rhythms. The recommendations below are based on general field observations. They should be treated as hypotheses to test, not hard rules.

### General managers, CEOs and business owners

- **Recommended times:** 08:00–09:00 and 17:30–18:30.
- **Why:** Senior executives usually arrive before their team and leave after them. During these hours there's no assistant filter, and they answer the phone themselves.
- **Watch out:** Keep the conversation short. If you can't say why you're calling and why you're calling this particular person within the first 20 seconds, you've lost your chance.

### SMB owners

- **Recommended times:** Tuesday–Thursday, 10:00–11:30.
- **Why:** SMB owners are both managers and the people running operations. The first hours of the morning go to operations, and the afternoon goes to customer work.

### Sales and marketing managers

- **Recommended times:** 15:30–17:00.
- **Why:** Their mornings are packed with team meetings, pipeline reviews and customer calls. In the afternoon, they have more time for their own work.

### IT and technology managers

- **Recommended times:** 10:00–11:30 and 14:30–16:00.
- **Why:** The first hours of the morning go to system checks and urgent requests. Midday is calmer.

### Finance and procurement

- **Recommended times:** In the first 2–3 weeks of the month, 10:00–12:00.
- **Why:** Month-end closing keeps these roles extremely busy. A call made during closing week rarely turns into a productive conversation.

### Production and operations managers

- **Recommended times:** 13:30–15:00.
- **Why:** In the morning they're busy with shift start-up and production planning. After lunch, they're more likely to be back at their desk from the floor.

## How does timing change by industry?

Alongside role, industry also shapes the rhythm. A few examples:

- **Restaurants, cafés and hospitality:** Calling during lunch and dinner service is nearly impossible. 10:00–11:30 and 15:00–17:00 are much more productive. For businesses that are busy on weekends, Tuesday and Wednesday stand out.
- **Retail stores:** The hours right after opening and close to closing are busy. Weekday mornings are a better window.
- **Agencies and consulting firms:** Mornings are packed with client deliverables and internal meetings. After 15:00 in the afternoon works better.
- **Healthcare organizations and clinics:** Outside of patient intake hours, calls very early in the morning or close to the end of the working day are generally more productive.
- **Logistics and distribution:** The first hours of the morning go to shipment planning. Afternoons are more suitable.
- **Software and SaaS companies:** Since flexible working hours are common, you can step outside the classic pattern. 16:00–18:00 often delivers good results.

## Which times should you absolutely avoid?

Some times aren't just unproductive; they can also damage how your brand is perceived:

- **Monday 09:00–10:30:** Weekly planning meetings and a backlog of emails. A sales call at this hour is often perceived as an annoyance.
- **Every day 12:30–14:00:** Lunch break. Even if the phone is answered, the person isn't at their desk.
- **Friday 12:00–14:00:** Effectively unusable, especially in traditional industries.
- **Friday after 15:00:** Wrapping up the week. Even a "yes, let's meet" at this hour can be forgotten by Monday.
- **Mobile phones outside business hours:** Calling a corporate number at 19:00 may be acceptable. Calling a personal mobile at the same hour, however, often means crossing a line. ## How do you find your own best calling time?

General patterns are enough to get started. But the real advantage lies in the answer that comes from your own data. Even if two teams target the same industry, their results will differ because the mix of roles in their lists is different. The steps below will help you find your own "golden hours" within 4–6 weeks.

### 1. Define the right metrics

Looking only at "how many calls we made" is misleading. Log every call with at least the following fields:

- **Call date, day and time** (split the day into 30-minute blocks)
- **Outcome:** No answer / gatekeeper / wrong person / conversation held / meeting booked
- **Target role and industry**
- **Call duration**

Three core ratios are calculated from this data:

- **Connect rate** = Calls resulting in a conversation / Total calls
- **Conversation-to-meeting conversion** = Meetings booked / Conversations held
- **Meetings per call** = Meetings booked / Total calls

We explained in detail how to track these ratios across your team in our [KPI guide to measuring sales team productivity](/blog/how-to-measure-sales-team-productivity-kpis).

### 2. Set up a controlled test plan

If you pile all your calls into the same time slot, you can't make comparisons. Instead, randomly split your call list and distribute it across different time blocks. A sample test schedule:

- **Block A:** 08:30–09:30
- **Block B:** 10:00–11:30
- **Block C:** 14:30–15:30
- **Block D:** 15:30–17:00
- **Block E:** 17:00–18:00

Aim for at least 50–100 calls per block each week. Comparisons based on fewer calls are skewed by random fluctuations.

### 3. Break down the results by role

Aggregate numbers can be misleading. For example, Block E's overall result may look average, but when you look only at calls to general managers, you may find it's the best block. Always review results broken down by role and industry.

### 4. Decide with a simple calculation

Let's consider a hypothetical example. You're comparing two time blocks:

- **Block B (10:00–11:30):** 200 calls, 30 conversations, 6 meetings. Connect rate 15%, conversation-to-meeting conversion 20%, meetings per call 3%.
- **Block E (17:00–18:00):** 200 calls, 20 conversations, 6 meetings. Connect rate 10%, conversation-to-meeting conversion 30%, meetings per call 3%.

At first glance, the two blocks look equal. But Block E produced the same number of meetings with fewer conversations. This shows that the people you reached at that time were more qualified, and that you were probably reaching decision-makers directly. With this insight, it makes sense to reserve Block E for senior executives and Block B for mid-level managers.

### 5. Retest regularly

As seasons, economic conditions and target audiences change, the best times shift too. Once a quarter, validate your assumptions with a short two-week test period.

## How do you build a weekly calling schedule?

Once you've gathered your data, you need to structure the team's week around it. The template below is a good starting point for many B2B teams:

- **Monday:** Morning list preparation, data cleanup and weekly planning. A light call block between 15:30 and 17:00 in the afternoon.
- **Tuesday:** Main call block from 10:00–11:30. Second call block from 15:30–17:00.
- **Wednesday:** Same structure as Tuesday. Can be planned as the busiest calling day of the week.
- **Thursday:** Call block from 10:00–11:30. Follow-up calls and meeting confirmations in the afternoon.
- **Friday:** A short call block from 09:30–11:30 in the morning. In the afternoon, CRM updates, note-taking and preparing next week's list.

The most important rule of this schedule is: **Call blocks must be protected.** Internal meetings, email writing or research should not be squeezed into calling time. A sales rep starting the day with "let me just do a bit of research first" ends up losing the most productive 90 minutes.

### Finish research before calling time

Researching the person and the company during the call breaks your rhythm. Instead, prepare the list the day before:

- The right person and their direct phone number
- The company's size, industry and recent developments
- A one-sentence personalization note explaining why you're calling

## How should you time cold calls with email and LinkedIn?

Cold calling isn't a channel that works on its own. The best results come from approaches where calls are coordinated with email and LinkedIn touchpoints within the same sequence.

A sequence that works in practice looks like this:

1. **Day 1:** LinkedIn profile visit and a short connection request.
2. **Day 2:** A personalized first email, sent between 08:00 and 09:00.
3. **Day 3:** First call, made between 10:00–11:30 or 15:30–17:00. Saying "I sent you a short email yesterday" during the call turns a cold call into a "warm" one.
4. **Day 5:** If you couldn't reach them by phone, send a short follow-up email.
5. **Days 7–8:** Make a second call in a different time slot. If the first call was in the morning, the second should be in the afternoon.

This last step is critical: **Don't call the same person at the same time every time.** If the person has a recurring meeting every Tuesday at 10:00, calling three times at that hour means failing three times. Spreading touchpoints across different days and times significantly improves your connect rate.

## How should you call markets in different time zones?

For teams selling from Turkey into international markets, timing requires separate planning:

- **Europe (Germany, the Netherlands, France, etc.):** Generally 1–2 hours behind Turkey. 11:30–13:00 in Turkey corresponds to Europe's 10:00–11:30 morning window. Dedicating Turkey's lunch break to European calls is a smart way to use the calendar efficiently.
- **United Kingdom:** 2–3 hours behind Turkey. 17:30–19:00 Turkey time corresponds to the UK's afternoon window.
- **Gulf countries:** In the same or a nearby time zone as Turkey. However, the weekend structure can differ. In some countries, Friday is not a working day. Working hours also change significantly during Ramadan.
- **US East Coast:** 7–8 hours behind Turkey. 17:00–19:00 Turkey time corresponds to the US 10:00–11:30 morning window.

For international calls, always store the **prospect's local time** as a separate field in your CRM. Leaving the call time to the rep's memory leads to both mistakes and a bad first impression. ## How does automation solve the timing problem?

Knowing the right time is one thing. Calling the right person, with the right information, at that time is another. In manual processes, reps spend a significant share of their time on tasks like list building, dialing numbers and updating the CRM. This shrinks the most productive time blocks.

The main areas where automation improves timing are:

- **Smart call queues:** Each lead is automatically assigned to the best call block based on their role and local time zone. When the rep sits down at 10:00, the list of people it makes the most sense to call at that hour is ready.
- **Trigger-based calls:** When a lead opens your email, clicks a link or visits your website, the relevant rep gets a "call now" notification. The first hours after a warm signal are usually the most productive calling window.
- **Automatic logging and analysis:** Each call's time, outcome and duration are automatically recorded in the CRM. This turns "best time" analysis into a continuously updated dashboard instead of weeks of manual work.
- **Follow-up reminders:** For someone who couldn't be reached, the next call is automatically scheduled for a different day and time slot.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the worst day of the week for cold calling?

Generally, Monday morning and Friday afternoon are the weakest windows. On Monday mornings, decision-makers are busy with weekly planning and backlogged work. On Friday afternoons, they're in week-wrap-up mode. In Turkey, Friday midday and the eves of religious holidays should be added to that list.

### Is it okay to cold call outside business hours?

For senior executives, 08:00–09:00 in the morning and 17:30–18:30 in the evening are often productive windows, because there's no assistant filter at those times. However, calling personal mobiles late outside business hours can be perceived as intrusive and can damage your brand's reputation. Preferring corporate numbers and generally treating 19:00 as the cutoff is good practice.

### How many times should I call the same person?

Getting a result from a single call is rare. Typically, several call attempts spread across different days and times are planned alongside email and LinkedIn touchpoints. What matters is not calling the same person at the same time every time, and offering new value or context with each touch.

### Is the best time the same for every industry?

No. Restaurants, clinics, manufacturing facilities and software companies operate on very different daily rhythms. General patterns can be used as a starting point. But the definitive answer comes from controlled tests you run on your own target audience over a few weeks.

## Conclusion

The short answer to the question of the best time and day for cold calling is clear: **Tuesday–Thursday, 10:00–11:30 and 15:30–17:00.** In Turkey, local factors such as longer lunch breaks, Friday midday, and the Ramadan and holiday periods need to be factored in. But the real competitive advantage lies not in memorizing general patterns, but in using your own data to figure out which role to call at what time.

To do this, log your calls systematically and test time blocks in a controlled way. Review results by role and industry, protect your call blocks and build a sequence coordinated with email and LinkedIn touchpoints. When you support this process with automation, your reps spend their most productive hours on real conversations instead of building lists.

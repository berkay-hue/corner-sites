The best alternative to LinkedIn Sales Navigator for Turkey isn't a single tool. The best results come from a setup that combines several sources. Databases like **Apollo.io, Lusha and Kaspr** work well for contact and people data. Combining them with **Turkey-specific local sources** such as Google Maps, trade show exhibitor lists and industry associations produces a more comprehensive list than Sales Navigator. In many cases, this setup also costs less. Sales Navigator is strong at finding enterprise decision-makers who are active on LinkedIn. But it doesn't provide email addresses or phone numbers, its dollar-based pricing is high, and it barely covers Turkish SMEs, which are poorly represented on LinkedIn.

In this article, we look at the alternatives within a realistic framework: what each tool is good for, where it's strong and weak on Turkish data, and which combination makes sense for your team size. At the end, we walk you step by step through building a prospecting workflow that doesn't depend on Sales Navigator. The goal isn't to hand you a "best tools" list. The goal is to help you build a data stack that fits your own ICP and budget.

## What does Sales Navigator actually do well, and what doesn't it do?

Before looking for alternatives, you need to be clear about what you're trying to replace. Sales Navigator has three core strengths:

- **Advanced filtering:** You can search LinkedIn members using filters such as job title, seniority, company size, industry, geography and time in current company.
- **Signal tracking:** Job changes, company hiring activity, posts and movements within your lead lists arrive as notifications.
- **InMail:** You can send a limited number of direct messages to people you're not connected with.

Its weak points, however, matter more for teams in Turkey:

- **It doesn't provide email addresses or phone numbers.** If most of your outbound runs through email or phone, you'll need an additional data tool anyway.
- **It can't find what isn't on LinkedIn.** In Turkey, in industries such as manufacturing, logistics, construction, retail and food, a significant share of decision-makers either have no LinkedIn profile or haven't updated it in years.
- **It's priced in dollars.** When you think in Turkish lira, the monthly cost per user becomes a serious line item, especially for small teams.
- **Exporting is limited.** Moving lists directly into your CRM or automation tools is cumbersome without higher-tier plans and integrations.

In short, Sales Navigator is a **discovery tool**, not a **contact data tool**. Keeping these two separate in your mind when evaluating alternatives helps you avoid the wrong purchasing decision.

## Why are alternatives more sought after in Turkey?

There are several concrete reasons why B2B teams selling into the Turkish market don't get what they expect from Sales Navigator.

The first is **audience mismatch**. If you sell to conglomerates, banks, tech companies or the Turkish offices of multinationals, LinkedIn data is quite rich. But if you want to reach the owner of a machinery manufacturer in Konya, the procurement manager of a textile company in Gaziantep or the plant manager of an automotive supplier in Bursa, the picture changes. You'll find some of these people on LinkedIn, but most of them you won't.

The second is **channel preference**. Phone and WhatsApp are still very strong channels in Turkish B2B sales. While InMail response rates vary by industry, many local decision-makers don't check their LinkedIn messages regularly. A tool that doesn't provide phone numbers only gets you halfway in this market.

The third is the **cost-efficiency balance**. For a three-person SDR team, annual Sales Navigator licenses can, in lira terms, amount to a significant portion of many companies' total marketing budget. Add a second tool for contact data on top, and the cost doubles.

## What criteria should you use to evaluate an alternative?

Before comparing tools, build your own evaluation checklist. Here are the six criteria we recommend:

1. **Turkey coverage:** How many records does the tool return for your target industry and company size? Test this not during a demo, but with a trial account using your own filters.
2. **Email accuracy:** What's the bounce rate on company emails in Turkey? Verify with a small sample, for example 100 addresses.
3. **Phone data:** Are mobile numbers available, or only switchboard numbers? Mobile number coverage for Turkey is usually limited in global tools, so know this from the start.
4. **Pricing model:** Per user or credit-based? Monthly or annual commitment? Do unused credits roll over?
5. **Integration:** Does it connect to your CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce, etc.) and your automation tools (n8n, Make, Zapier)? Which plan unlocks API access?
6. **Legal compliance:** Are the data source and processing method defensible under KVKK (Turkey's personal data protection law)?

Before weighing these criteria, you need to be clear about who you're selling to. If you haven't nailed that down yet, we recommend first working through [how to define your ideal customer profile (ICP)](/blog/how-to-define-ideal-customer-profile-b2b). With the wrong ICP, even the right tool produces an empty list.

## Leading Sales Navigator alternatives for Turkey

None of the tools below is an exact copy of Sales Navigator. Each one fills a different gap. Because prices change frequently, we describe pricing models and general positioning rather than figures. Always check current pricing on the tool's own website.

### Apollo.io: discovery and contact data in one place

Apollo is the closest "all-in-one" alternative to Sales Navigator. You can search using filters such as job title, industry, company size, location, technology usage and hiring signals, and see the email address and, in some cases, the phone number of the people you find. On top of that, it offers built-in email sequences.

**Where it's strong in Turkey:** Coverage is generally satisfactory for technology, SaaS, consulting, finance and enterprise companies. Its free plan makes small-scale testing easy, which is a big advantage for teams in Turkey.

**Where it's weak:** Coverage drops for local SMEs and traditional industries. Coverage of Turkish mobile numbers is limited. Also, some emails are in a "guessed" format, so you shouldn't send without verifying them.

To see the coverage for your own target audience, you can open a free account on [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) and enter your ICP filters. You'll get a good sense within a few minutes.

### Lusha: quickly finding contact details

Lusha stands out especially for its browser extension. When you open a LinkedIn profile or a company website, it shows the person's email and phone details. It works on credits and has a free starter plan.

**Use case:** Finding target contacts with LinkedIn's free search and pulling their contact details with Lusha. This combination greatly reduces the need for Sales Navigator in low-volume, high-value account-based (ABM) work.

**Watch out:** Credits run out fast. It's better suited to targeted lookups than bulk list building.

### Kaspr and Cognism: Europe-focused, phone-heavy

Kaspr is a Europe-focused extension that finds contact details via LinkedIn profiles. Cognism is positioned more toward the enterprise and is known for its phone-verified mobile numbers. Both are under the same corporate umbrella.

**From a Turkey perspective:** Being Europe-focused doesn't automatically mean their Turkey coverage is good. However, they're a strong option for Turkish teams that export to Europe or sell to companies in Europe. Because Cognism works with annual contracts and enterprise pricing, it can be a heavy burden for small teams.

### ZoomInfo: enterprise-grade, but often overkill for Turkey

ZoomInfo is one of the most comprehensive tools, particularly for North American data. It offers intent data, org charts and detailed company information.

**From a Turkey perspective:** It makes sense only if you sell exclusively to the US market and have an enterprise-level budget. For prospecting within Turkey, the cost-to-coverage balance is usually unfavorable. The annual commitment and high entry price put it out of realistic reach for small and mid-sized Turkish teams.

### Hunter and Snov.io: domain-based email finding

These tools are built not for people discovery but for **email finding and verification**. When you provide a company domain (e.g., firma.com.tr) and the person's name, they find and verify the likely email address. Snov.io also adds a simple email sequencing feature on top of this.

**Why they're valuable in Turkey:** Company lists from local sources such as Google Maps, trade show lists or industry associations usually contain only the company name and website. Hunter or Snov.io are among the most practical ways to turn these lists into person-level contact data.

### Clay: an enrichment layer that combines multiple sources

Clay isn't a single database. It's an enrichment and workflow platform that queries dozens of data providers in sequence. If an email isn't found with one provider, it automatically searches the next one (waterfall enrichment). It can also generate custom fields using website scraping and AI.

**Who it's for:** More technically mature teams working with high-volume, multi-source lists. Its learning curve is steeper than the other tools, but in markets like Turkey where a single source isn't enough, it can significantly increase coverage.

## Which Turkey-specific data sources can you use?

Where global tools fall short, local sources step in. In many industries, they're what really makes the difference:

- **Google Maps:** One of the broadest sources for businesses with a physical location. You can collect company name, phone, website and address by city, district and category. We covered the details and limitations of this method in our [guide to building B2B lead lists from Google Maps](/blog/google-maps-b2b-lead-list).
- **Trade show exhibitor lists:** Exhibitor lists published on industry trade show websites are a ready-made roster of active, growth-oriented companies. The periods before and after a trade show also provide natural context for outbound.
- **Industry associations and exporters' associations:** Member lists are valuable for finding companies of scale in a specific industry.
- **Organized industrial zone (OSB) company directories:** For those selling to manufacturing, they provide company lists filtered by zone.
- **Job posting platforms:** A company posting a job for a specific role is a strong buying signal. For example, a manufacturer looking for an "e-commerce specialist" is a warm target for someone selling e-commerce infrastructure.
- **Turkish Trade Registry Gazette:** Newly founded companies, capital increases and management changes can be tracked as growth signals.

The common problem with these sources is that they give you **companies**, not **people**. This is where enrichment comes in: you take the company list, add decision-maker names and contact details, and then verify them. We explained the process step by step in our [guide to B2B data enrichment for Turkey](/blog/b2b-data-enrichment-turkey-guide).

## How much does it cost?

Giving exact figures would be misleading, because tool prices, exchange rates and plan contents change frequently. But we can illustrate the comparison logic with an example.

**Scenario:** A team of 3 SDRs selling to mid-sized manufacturing companies in Turkey. Each SDR wants to reach about 300 new contacts per month.

**Option A – Sales Navigator-centric setup:**
- 3 Sales Navigator licenses (monthly dollar-based fee per user)
- Plus a second tool for contact data (because Sales Navigator doesn't provide email/phone)
- Result: The cost of two separate tools. Since LinkedIn coverage is low in manufacturing, a significant part of the target list still remains missing.

**Option B – Multi-source setup:**
- A team-shared or lower-tier license for a database tool (e.g., Apollo)
- Local company lists from Google Maps and OSB directories (low tool cost, but labor cost)
- A credit-based tool for email finding and verification
- LinkedIn's free version for contact verification and context
- Result: Generally lower total license costs and higher coverage in the target industry. In return, you need to invest more time in setup and process design.

When deciding, look not at the license price but at **cost per reachable contact**. The formula is simple: total monthly tool cost ÷ number of contacts added to your list with verified contact details during the month. For example, if your total monthly cost is 15,000 TL and you get 900 verified contacts, your cost per contact is about 16.7 TL. A setup that produces 400 contacts on the same budget is more expensive, even if it looks cheaper on paper.

## Which combination makes sense for which team?

There's no single right answer, but we can suggest starting points for common scenarios:

**Solo founder-led sales or a very small team:**
- LinkedIn free version + Lusha or Apollo free plan
- Targeted lists of 50-100 contacts per week
- Goal: Test message-market fit without spending budget

**A 2-5 person team selling to enterprises:**
- Apollo (discovery + email) + a single Sales Navigator license if needed (used by the team lead for signal tracking)
- Lusha or Kaspr for phone numbers on key accounts
- Goal: Give Sales Navigator not to everyone, but to the one person who needs it

**A team selling to SMEs and traditional industries:**
- Google Maps + OSB/association lists + enrichment with Hunter/Snov.io
- Apollo for enterprise targets
- Phone-heavy outbound
- Goal: Rely on local data, not LinkedIn

**A Turkish SaaS or export company selling abroad:**
- Apollo, or Cognism/ZoomInfo depending on the target region
- Multi-source enrichment with Clay
- Goal: Use the strongest data provider for the target market

## How do you build a prospecting workflow without Sales Navigator?

Once you've chosen your tools, the real work is building a repeatable workflow that connects them. The following seven steps form a framework that works for most teams:

1. **Turn your ICP into filters.** Instead of a general definition like "mid-sized manufacturing companies," write concrete filters: industry code or category, employee count range (e.g., 50-500), list of cities, target job titles (general manager, plant manager, procurement manager).
2. **Build your company list.** Use Apollo filters for enterprise targets and Google Maps and OSB lists for SME targets. Merge all sources into a single table (Google Sheets, Airtable or directly in your CRM) and deduplicate by domain.
3. **Add decision-makers.** Identify 1-3 target contacts for each company. Fill in those you can't find on Apollo using LinkedIn's free search or the "about us" page on the company website.
4. **Find and verify contact details.** Pull addresses with email finders, then always verify them. Sending to unverified addresses damages your domain reputation.
5. **Prioritize.** Score each record based on signals: a recent job posting, trade show participation, news of a new investment, an exact match with the target job title, and so on.
6. **Import into your CRM and launch your sequence.** Import records into your CRM along with their source information. That way, you can later measure which source generates more meetings.
7. **Measure by source.** Every month, look at the verified email rate, reply rate and meeting rate by source. Cut underperforming sources and scale the good ones.

Steps 2, 4 and 6 of this workflow are well suited to automation. ## What should you watch out for under KVKK?

Whichever tool you use, the moment you process data belonging to individuals in Turkey, you fall within the scope of KVKK. When working with alternative tools, pay particular attention to the following:

- **Record the data source.** Keep where each record came from (which tool, which list) as a field in your CRM.
- **Process only business-related data.** Stick to professional data such as corporate email and job title, and avoid personal emails.
- **Make opting out easy.** Clearly offer a way to end communication in every message, and process requests quickly.
- **Review commercial electronic message rules.** For email and SMS outreach, clarify with your legal counsel how İYS (Turkey's Message Management System) and related regulations apply to your scenario.
- **Set a retention period.** Delete or anonymize records that don't respond or progress in the process after a set period.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a free alternative to Sales Navigator in Turkey?

There's no complete free equivalent, but you can do effective small-volume prospecting by combining LinkedIn's free search with the free plans of Apollo or Lusha. Local sources such as Google Maps and trade show exhibitor lists are also free. These sources generally require effort and time.

### What's the best source for reaching SMEs in Turkey?

Global databases usually fall short for SMEs. Google Maps, OSB company directories, industry associations and trade show lists offer broader coverage. Enriching the company lists from these sources with tools like Hunter or Snov.io and supporting them with phone outreach is the most efficient approach.

### Which is better, Apollo or Sales Navigator?

It depends on your goal. Sales Navigator is stronger for signal tracking within LinkedIn and discovering enterprise decision-makers. Apollo, on the other hand, provides email and phone data alongside discovery, making it a more complete outbound tool on its own. For many teams, the sensible solution is to give Apollo to the whole team and Sales Navigator only to the person who needs it.

### Are Turkish emails in global data tools reliable?

It varies by industry and company size. Accuracy is generally better for enterprises and lower for small businesses. Whichever tool you use, running emails through a separate verification step before sending is essential to protecting your domain reputation.

When looking for a Sales Navigator alternative in Turkey, the right question isn't "which tool is better?" but "where are my target customers, and what data can I use to reach them?" You don't have to drop Sales Navigator entirely.

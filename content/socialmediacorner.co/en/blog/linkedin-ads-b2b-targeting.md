B2B targeting in LinkedIn ads works by defining your ideal customer profile (ICP) through **company attributes** (industry, employee count, location) and **member attributes** (job function, seniority, job title, skills), then layering these criteria together. The most effective approach is to draw a broad firmographic frame, add role filters that represent decision-makers inside it, and reinforce it with target account lists (ABM) and retargeting audiences. In short, good targeting isn't about reaching "everyone." It's about reaching the people who influence the buying decision, with the right message.

LinkedIn offers B2B marketers an advantage no other platform has: users declare on their own profiles where they work, what position they hold, and which skills they have. Used correctly, this data means your budget goes to the buying committee. Used poorly, LinkedIn's relatively high click costs evaporate on audiences that have nothing to do with your goals. In this guide, we cover the targeting options in LinkedIn Campaign Manager, how to balance audience size, ABM and retargeting setups, and a step-by-step campaign build, all with concrete examples.

## Why should you start LinkedIn targeting with an ICP definition?

Before you open Campaign Manager and start picking filters, you need to clarify in writing who you sell to. Most targeting mistakes come not from the platform but from a vague customer definition.

A good ICP definition answers at least these questions:

- **Companies in which industries** get the most value from your product?
- **What company size** are they? (For example, 50–500 employees)
- **In which geography** do they operate?
- **Who makes the buying decision**, who influences it, and who uses the product?
- What are these people's **biggest operational challenges**?

Then go one step further and map the buying committee. For example, if you sell HR software, the decision-maker might be the HR Director, the budget approver the CFO, and the technical evaluator the IT Manager. These three people should not see the same ad; each has different concerns. Building your targeting by persona also makes it possible to build your messaging by persona.

It's also important to align your ICP with your content. To create a consistent message that the personas you target in ads also encounter in their organic feed, take a look at our guide on [building a B2B content strategy step by step](/blog/how-to-build-b2b-content-strategy).

## What targeting options does LinkedIn offer?

In Campaign Manager, targeting criteria are grouped under a few main headings. Knowing the strengths and weaknesses of each is a prerequisite for building the right combination.

### Company (firmographic) targeting

- **Company name:** Lets you select specific companies one by one. This is the foundation of ABM campaigns.
- **Industry (Company Industry):** The industry declared on the company's LinkedIn page. Because some companies place themselves in generic categories, it can be misleading when used on its own.
- **Company size:** Ranges based on employee count (1–10, 11–50, 51–200, 201–500, 501–1000, etc.). One of the most reliable filters in B2B.
- **Company followers and connections:** Lets you target people who follow your page or your employees' connections.
- **Company growth rate:** Targeting fast-growing companies makes sense, especially for solutions focused on hiring or scaling.

### Work experience targeting

- **Job Function:** Broad department categories such as Marketing, Finance, Information Technology and Human Resources.
- **Seniority:** Levels such as Training, Entry, Senior, Manager, Director, VP, CXO, Owner and Partner.
- **Job Title:** LinkedIn's standardized list of job titles. The most precise but also the narrowest option.
- **Skills:** Skills that users list on their profiles. Very useful for technical products (e.g., "Salesforce," "Kubernetes").
- **Years of experience:** Filtering by total professional experience.

### Education and demographics

School, field of study and degree filters usually play a secondary role in B2B. They become relevant for employer branding, master's programs or campaigns aimed at alumni networks. We recommend not using age and gender filters in B2B campaigns; they mostly narrow your audience unnecessarily.

### Interests and traits

- **Member groups:** Targeting members of specific LinkedIn groups.
- **Interests:** Inferred targeting based on the content topics users engage with.
- **Member traits:** Behavioral signals such as "frequent travelers" or "job changers."

Interest targeting is weak on its own, but when used within a firmographic frame, it helps narrow your audience down to "people genuinely interested in the topic."

## Job title targeting, or job function + seniority?

This is one of the most frequently asked questions in LinkedIn advertising, and the answer is usually "**start with a job function + seniority combination**."

The problem with job title targeting is that the same role can be written in many different ways. "Pazarlama Müdürü," "Marketing Manager," "Head of Growth," "Digital Marketing Lead" and "Brand & Communications Manager" can all describe the same decision-maker. When you select titles one by one, you both miss some of them and shrink your audience quickly.

A job function + seniority combination works like this:

- Job function: **Marketing**
- Seniority: **Manager, Director, VP, CXO**

This combination captures decision-makers in the marketing department regardless of how their title is written. Later, you review the campaign data (using the demographic reports in Campaign Manager), see which titles actually engage, and, if needed, narrow down with a second, title-based campaign.

**When job title targeting makes sense:**

- If you're targeting a very specific, standardized role (e.g., "Director of Information Security," "CISO").
- If you want to reach specific people within a small company list in an ABM campaign.
- If the function category doesn't define the role well enough.

An important caveat: The "Owner" option in the seniority filter also includes freelancers and sole proprietors who describe themselves as business owners. If you sell an enterprise product, always use this option together with a company size filter.

## How large should your target audience be?

For a campaign to go live on LinkedIn, the target audience must consist of at least 300 members. But this is only the technical minimum; much larger audiences are recommended for performance. LinkedIn's general recommendation is an audience size of roughly **50,000 or more** for Sponsored Content campaigns and roughly **15,000 or more** for Message Ads.

In practice, B2B companies can use the following breakdown:

- **Very narrow audience (under 1,000):** Suitable for ABM and retargeting. Frequency rises quickly and ad fatigue sets in early. Creatives need to be refreshed often.
- **Mid-sized audience (10,000–80,000):** The natural range for most B2B campaigns in niche industries in the Turkish market. Precise enough, yet large enough for the algorithm to optimize.
- **Broad audience (over 150,000):** Suitable for awareness and top-of-funnel content, but the message has to stay more general.

In campaigns targeting a single country like Turkey, audiences shrink faster than you might expect. For example, the combination "Turkey + 201–1000 employees + Finance function + Director-level seniority and above" can drop to a few thousand people. In that case, you may need to loosen one of the filters (e.g., lower seniority to Manager level) or expand the geography.

Audience size is also directly linked to budget. Narrow audiences can result in higher click and impression costs. You'll find a more detailed look at the relationship between audience size and cost in our [LinkedIn advertising costs guide](/blog/linkedin-ads-cost-b2b-guide), where we cover budget planning in depth.

## AND vs. OR logic: How should you combine filters?

Filters in Campaign Manager work with two types of logic:

- **Selections within the same category are combined with "OR."** For example, if you select "IT Services" and "Software Development" as industries, people in either industry are targeted.
- **Categories added via "Narrow further" are combined with "AND."** For example, when you select industry + company size + job function, a person must meet all three conditions.

A common mistake is adding filters that already overlap with AND. For example, combining the "Marketing" job function with the "Digital Marketing" skill using AND leaves out many qualified decision-makers who haven't added that skill to their profiles. **As a rule, 3 layers, or 4 at most,** is enough for most B2B campaigns.

Another critical setting is the **Audience Expansion** and **LinkedIn Audience Network** options. Audience Expansion allows LinkedIn to show your ads to people who are "similar" to your target audience. Audience Network serves your ads on partner apps and sites outside LinkedIn. If you're running precise B2B targeting, we recommend turning both off during the initial test period. It's important to see baseline performance data before giving up control.

## How do you use Matched Audiences for account-based marketing (ABM)?

LinkedIn's most powerful targeting weapon in B2B is the **Matched Audiences** feature. It comes in three main types:

1. **Company list upload:** You upload a CSV file containing your target companies' names, websites or LinkedIn page URLs. LinkedIn matches these companies against its own database.
2. **Contact list upload:** By uploading a list of email addresses (preferably work emails) and names, you can show ads directly to these people.
3. **Retargeting audiences:** Engagement-based audiences such as website visitors, video viewers, people who opened a lead form, event attendees and company page visitors.

### An ABM setup with a company list

Let's walk through an example scenario. Imagine you're a logistics software company and your sales team has identified 400 target companies in Turkey.

1. Prepare a CSV containing the names and website domains of these 400 companies.
2. Upload the company list in Campaign Manager under Plan > Audiences. Check the match rate; adding website domains usually improves matching.
3. Select this list in your campaign and, using "Narrow further," add the **Operations, Supply Chain and IT** functions and **Manager and above** seniority.
4. Create a separate ad set for each persona: an efficiency message for the Operations Director, cost savings for the CFO, and ease of integration for the IT Manager.

With this setup, your audience may be only a few thousand people, but every one of them works at a company your sales team already wants to talk to. In ABM campaigns, success should be measured not by click-through rate but by **depth of engagement within target accounts** and **conversion to sales meetings**.

### Where do you find the right data for a contact list?

In contact list uploads, the match rate depends directly on data quality. Customer and opportunity records in your CRM are the most valuable source. Teams looking to expand their list can use B2B data platforms like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) or LinkedIn's own tool to filter decision-maker contact information. Our article comparing different options, [LinkedIn Sales Navigator pricing and alternatives](/blog/linkedin-sales-navigator-pricing-alternatives), makes it easier to decide which tool suits your team.

Always check compliance with KVKK (Turkey's personal data protection law) and related regulations when processing personal data; before uploading a list for advertising purposes, it should be clear how the data was collected and what consent it is based on.

## How do you build a funnel with retargeting?

B2B buying cycles are long. A decision-maker doesn't request a demo the moment they see your first ad. That's why you need to think of targeting not as a single campaign but as **a funnel**.

An example three-stage setup:

1. **Awareness:** A short video or thought leadership piece for a cold audience built with ICP filters. Goal: video views and engagement.
2. **Consideration:** A Lead Gen Form ad featuring a guide or case study, shown to people who watched at least 50% of the video and to company page visitors. Goal: lead capture.
3. **Decision:** A demo or free trial offer for people who visited your website's pricing or product pages in the last 30 days. Goal: sales meetings.

For retargeting, the **LinkedIn Insight Tag** must be installed on your website. This tag both builds retargeting audiences and enables conversion tracking. The Insight Tag also anonymously reports the company, industry and seniority breakdown of your website visitors; this data is extremely valuable for validating your ICP.

Since retargeting audiences are usually small, keep an eye on frequency. Showing the same ad to the same person many times a week can hurt brand perception. Refreshing creatives every 2–3 weeks is a good starting rhythm.

## Do Predictive Audiences and lookalike audiences still work?

LinkedIn gradually phased out its classic Lookalike Audiences feature and replaced it with AI-powered **Predictive Audiences**. This feature uses a source audience (for example, lead form submitters, converters or your contact list) to find new people with similar behaviors and attributes.

The key to getting results from Predictive Audiences is a high-quality source audience. Using only leads you've marked as "qualified" or your closed-won customers as the source usually produces more accurate results than using all form submitters. We recommend testing these audiences first in a test campaign separate from your main campaigns; that way, you can compare performance against manual targeting.

## Step by step: How do you set up a B2B-targeted campaign on LinkedIn?

The steps below are based on a hypothetical example of a B2B SaaS company that wants to reach finance leaders at mid-sized companies in Turkey.

1. **Set your objective.** Choose "Lead Generation" as the campaign objective. The aim is to collect form submissions from decision-makers who want to download a finance automation guide.
2. **Select the location.** Choosing only "Permanent location" instead of "Recent or permanent location" excludes people who are just briefly visiting Turkey.
3. **Build the firmographic frame.** Company size: 51–200, 201–500, 501–1000. Exclude industries you don't serve (e.g., government agencies).
4. **Add the role filter.** Job function: Finance, Accounting. Seniority: Manager, Director, VP, CXO.
5. **Apply exclusions.** Exclude your current customer list, your own employees and competitor companies.
6. **Turn off expansion settings.** Keep Audience Expansion and Audience Network off for the first 2–3 weeks.
7. **Check audience size.** If the estimated audience is in the 20,000–60,000 range, proceed. If it's too small, broaden the seniority or company size filter.
8. **Prepare the ad format and creatives.** Run at least 3–4 different ad variations in the same campaign: different headlines, visuals and value propositions.
9. **Set your budget and bidding strategy.** Start with automated bidding (Maximum Delivery) to collect data; once you have enough conversion data, consider switching to manual or cost cap bidding for cost control.
10. **Review the demographic reports after the first 7–14 days.** See which job titles, industries and companies are engaging, and separate out underperforming segments.

Planning your campaign content alongside your organic posts during this process ensures your target audience sees your brand at multiple touchpoints. ## Why are exclusions at least as important as targeting?

In many campaigns, a significant share of the budget is spent on people who will never become customers. Exclusion filters plug this leak.

Audiences you should always exclude:

- **Current customers:** Unless you're running an upsell campaign, showing them new customer acquisition ads is a waste of budget.
- **Your own employees:** Exclude your company name so your team doesn't skew the data by clicking your ads.
- **Competitors:** Employees of competing companies frequently engage with your ads but will never become customers.
- **Students and entry-level:** Exclude the "Training" and "Entry" seniority levels, unless you've deliberately chosen them.
- **People who submitted a form in the last 30–90 days:** Getting a lead from the same person for the same offer again creates duplicate records for the sales team.
- **Job seekers and recruiters:** In some industries, excluding the HR function or the "open to work" signal improves audience quality.

## What are the most common mistakes in B2B targeting?

- **Putting everything into one campaign:** Grouping different personas in the same campaign prevents you from seeing which segment works. Set up a separate campaign per persona or funnel stage.
- **Over-narrowing:** Shrinking your audience to a few hundred people with 6–7 layers of filters drives up costs and makes it harder for the algorithm to learn.
- **Relying too heavily on the industry filter:** Companies' declared industries aren't always accurate. Validate with company lists whenever possible.
- **Using "Owner" seniority unchecked:** It lets freelancers and sole proprietors inflate your audience.
- **Setting up targeting once and forgetting about it:** Campaigns that don't regularly review demographic reports and optimize the audience become less efficient over time.
- **Not matching the message to the targeting:** Reaching the right person with a generic message is as costly as reaching the wrong person. Talk to the CFO about financial impact, not a list of technical features.

## How do you measure whether your targeting is working?

Evaluating targeting quality solely by click-through rate (CTR) or cost per lead (CPL) can be misleading. If cheap leads come from people your sales team will never be able to close, a low CPL is not a success.

Metrics you should track:

- **Demographic breakdown:** The "Demographics" report in Campaign Manager shows the job title, seniority, industry and company breakdown of people engaging with your ads. This report is the most direct indicator of whether your targeting is actually reaching the intended people.
- **Lead quality rate:** What percentage of collected leads does the sales team accept as qualified (SQL)?
- **Target account engagement:** In ABM campaigns, at how many of the companies on your list did at least one person engage with your ads?
- **Conversion to opportunities and sales:** With CRM integration, track the contribution of LinkedIn leads to pipeline and revenue.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum audience size for LinkedIn ads?

For a campaign to go live on LinkedIn, the target audience must consist of at least 300 members. However, for healthy performance, an audience of roughly 50,000 for Sponsored Content campaigns and roughly 15,000 or more for Message Ads is recommended. ABM and retargeting campaigns can fall below these recommendations, but in that case frequency and creative fatigue should be monitored more closely.

### Is job title targeting or job function and seniority targeting better?

For most B2B campaigns, a combination of job function and seniority is a better starting point because it also covers different spellings of the same role's title. Job title targeting is more suitable when you want to reach very specific, standardized roles or when targeting specific people within a small ABM list. The ideal approach is to start with function and seniority, then narrow down by job title based on demographic reports.

### Can I show ads to specific companies on LinkedIn?

Yes. With the Matched Audiences feature, you can upload a list of your target companies as a CSV or select company names one by one to show ads only to employees at those companies. By combining this list with job function and seniority filters, you can build ABM campaigns that reach decision-makers at your target accounts directly.

### Should I keep Audience Expansion turned on?

If you're running precise B2B targeting, it's recommended to keep Audience Expansion off, at least during the initial test period. This feature expands your audience to people LinkedIn considers similar and can reduce your control. Once you've seen baseline performance data, you can turn it on in a separate campaign and compare the results.

## Conclusion

B2B targeting in LinkedIn ads is about much more than selecting the right filters. When you build a structure that starts with a clear ICP and buying committee map, combines a firmographic frame with role filters, goes deeper with ABM lists and retargeting audiences, and stops budget leaks with exclusions, LinkedIn's seemingly high costs start turning into qualified opportunities. Remember: targeting isn't a set-and-forget setting; it's a process you continuously refine with demographic reports and sales data.

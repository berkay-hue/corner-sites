For a B2B company, an Instagram hashtag strategy isn't about tacking on random popular tags. It's about building a small, layered and continuously tested hashtag library made up of the **industry, solution, event and brand tags** your target customers actually search for and follow. In practice, that means 3–5 carefully chosen hashtags per post, ready-made sets organized by content pillar, and regular revisions based on at least three months of measurement.

In this article, we walk step by step through what hashtags actually do for a B2B Instagram account today, how to research and categorize them, and how to measure their performance. By the end, you'll have a framework you can adapt to your own industry, along with a sample hashtag set.

## Do hashtags still work in B2B?

The short answer: Yes, but you need to set the right expectations. Instagram's algorithm has changed over the years, and platform representatives have said many times that hashtags don't dramatically boost reach on their own; rather, they help the platform **understand what the content is about**. Today, keywords in captions, on-screen text, audio and account names carry at least as much weight in Instagram search as hashtags do.

For B2B, this is actually an advantage. In B2B, the goal isn't to reach millions of people, but a few thousand of the right decision-makers and influencers. Well-chosen hashtags do four jobs:

- **Classification:** They tell the algorithm which industry and topic your content belongs to. This increases the chances of your content appearing in the Explore feeds of users with similar interests.
- **Discoverability:** Attendees browsing the relevant tag during a trade show, conference or industry meetup can find you.
- **Campaign tracking:** A branded or campaign-specific hashtag gathers user-generated content and employee posts in one place.
- **Brand consistency:** Using the same tags with discipline helps your account become associated with a specific area of expertise.

Your hashtag strategy should be treated as part of your overall Instagram plan. If your account positioning and content pillars aren't clearly defined yet, we recommend first reading our article on [how to build an Instagram strategy for B2B companies](/blog/b2b-instagram-strategy).

## How do you build a B2B hashtag strategy step by step?

The seven steps below offer a roadmap that works both for a B2B account starting from scratch and for one that has been using hashtags inconsistently for years.

### 1. Define your target audience and content pillars

Before you start researching hashtags, answer two questions:

1. **Who do we want to reach?** For logistics software, for example, this audience might include logistics managers, supply chain specialists, e-commerce operations managers and students in the field.
2. **What topics do we create content about?** A typical B2B account has 4–5 content pillars: industry insights and education, product and solution showcases, customer success stories (which can be told without naming the client), company culture and employer branding, and events and announcements.

Each content pillar needs a different hashtag set. The tags you use in employer branding content shouldn't be the same as the ones you use in a technical product video. When setting up your content pillars, our guide on [how to create a B2B content strategy step by step](/blog/how-to-build-b2b-content-strategy) is a good starting point.

### 2. Define your hashtag layers

An effective B2B hashtag library usually consists of five layers:

- **Brand hashtag:** Your company name or a short slogan. Example: #CompanyName, #CompanyNameTeam. It stays constant across every post or all relevant posts.
- **Industry hashtags:** Broad tags that describe your field of activity. Example: #lojistik (logistics), #tedarikzinciri (supply chain), #endüstri40 (Industry 4.0).
- **Niche and solution hashtags:** Narrower tags that describe the specific problem your product solves. Example: #depoyönetimi (warehouse management), #rotaoptimizasyonu (route optimization), #kestirimcibakım (predictive maintenance).
- **Event and campaign hashtags:** Temporary tags for trade shows, webinars and product launches. Example: the event organizer's official hashtag or #CompanyNameWebinar.
- **Location and community hashtags:** Tags aimed at a specific city, region or professional community. Example: #istanbulteknoloji (Istanbul tech), #kadınmühendisler (women engineers).

This layered structure lets you send both a broad classification signal and a narrow, low-competition discovery opportunity with every post.

### 3. How do you research hashtags?

The most common mistake is using only the tags that "come to mind." Combine the following methods for your research:

- **Instagram search bar:** Type in a keyword and look at the "Tags" tab. The suggested related tags and their post counts give you a sense of volume.
- **Competitor and industry leader analysis:** Pick 8–10 active accounts in your industry, both domestic and international. List in a spreadsheet which tags they use repeatedly in their last 20–30 posts.
- **The language of customers and prospects:** Look at the terms used in posts by your target companies and the decision-makers there. The jargon your marketing team uses and the language your customers use are often different.
- **Event calendar:** List in advance the official hashtags of the trade shows and conferences you'll attend or follow during the year.
- **Sales and customer service teams:** Find out the questions customers ask most often and the phrases they use. These are pure gold, both as hashtags and as caption keywords.

By the end of this stage, you should have a raw list of 60–100 candidates.

### 4. Strike a balance between volume and competition

Split your raw list into three groups based on post count. The ranges below aren't hard rules, but a practical starting benchmark for B2B accounts in the Turkish-language market:

- **High volume (several hundred thousand posts or more):** Tags like #teknoloji (technology) and #pazarlama (marketing). Competition is extremely high, and your content gets lost in the feed within minutes. Use them sparingly.
- **Medium volume (from a few thousand to a few hundred thousand):** Tags like #tedarikzinciri and #endüstri40. This group offers the best balance between classification and discovery.
- **Low volume (up to a few thousand):** Niche tags like #kestirimcibakım and #depootomasyonu (warehouse automation). Their reach potential is limited, but anyone browsing these tags is very likely to be exactly your target audience.

Also, always check what's actually inside each tag. Sometimes a professional-sounding hashtag is full of irrelevant or spammy content. Using such a tag can lead to your account being associated with the wrong audience.

### 5. Build a hashtag library

The output of your research should be a **hashtag library** that the whole team shares. A simple spreadsheet is enough. Suggested columns:

- Hashtag
- Layer (brand, industry, niche, event, location)
- Related content pillar
- Language (Turkish / English)
- Approximate volume group
- Last checked date
- Performance notes

In addition to this spreadsheet, define 2–3 ready-made **hashtag sets** for each content pillar. That way, content creators don't have to start from scratch with every post, and consistency is maintained across the brand. It's also good practice to make hashtag sets a checkpoint in your content approval workflow. To do this, we recommend adding the item "Is the hashtag set consistent with the library?" to the checklist in our article on [how to set up a social media content approval process in B2B](/blog/b2b-social-media-content-approval-process).

### 6. Set placement and usage rules

Once your library is ready, put a few simple rules in writing:

- **Place hashtags at the end of the caption.** Tags scattered through the middle of the text hurt readability and undermine a professional look.
- **Choose the caption over the first comment.** For years, the advice to "put hashtags in the first comment" made the rounds. However, tags in the caption stay both tidier and directly tied to the content.
- **Don't copy the same set onto every post.** Using an identical block of tags on every post may be seen by the platform as low-quality behavior. Rotate your sets and swap out 1–2 tags depending on the content.
- **Use capitalization for readability.** #EndustriyelOtomasyon is easier to read than #endustriyelotomasyon, and it's also more accessible for people using screen readers.
- **Standardize your decision on Turkish characters.** A hashtag like #tedarikzinciri can be split across different spellings (e.g., ı/i, ş/s). Check during your research which spelling is more common and set a single standard in your library.

### 7. Test, measure and revise

A hashtag strategy isn't something you set up once and forget. Evaluate performance in cycles of at least **three months**. A simple testing method:

1. Split 8–10 posts from the same content pillar and in a similar format into two groups.
2. Use Set A on the first group and Set B on the second.
3. Record each post's reach, non-follower reach rate, saves, shares and profile visits.
4. At the end of the period, compare the averages for each set.

The performance of a single post is affected by many variables, such as posting time, visuals and topic. So when making decisions, look at averages rather than individual results.

## How many hashtags should you use per post?

For many years, Instagram allowed up to 30 hashtags per post. Recently, however, the platform has been openly encouraging the use of fewer, more relevant hashtags and has taken steps to lower the limit. Our recommendation for B2B accounts is clear: **3–5 hashtags per post.**

There are three reasons for this:

- **The relevance signal gets stronger.** Five well-targeted tags send a clearer classification signal than thirty scattered ones.
- **A professional look is maintained.** Long blocks of hashtags can come across to decision-makers as "follower hunting."
- **Measurement becomes easier.** With fewer tags, it's much easier to understand which set is working.

A practical distribution example:

- 1 brand hashtag
- 1 industry hashtag (medium volume)
- 1–2 niche/solution hashtags (low volume)
- 1 event or campaign hashtag, if applicable

## Should you use Turkish or English hashtags?

The answer depends on your target market.

- **If you only sell to the Turkish market:** The emphasis should be on Turkish tags. That said, in some industries English terms are more common than their Turkish equivalents (such as #SaaS, #ERP and #CyberSecurity). Validate these terms through research and use a mix.
- **If you export or serve global customers:** The language of your tags should match the market the content is aimed at. Adding only English hashtags to a post with a Turkish caption sends an inconsistent signal.
- **For employer branding content:** In posts aimed at candidates in Türkiye, Turkish tags (such as #kariyer, #stajfırsatı and #mühendislik) reach a more accurate audience.

## Example: A hashtag set for an industrial automation company

To make this concrete, let's consider a fictional scenario: a mid-sized company in Türkiye that provides predictive maintenance and sensor solutions to manufacturing facilities. The target audience is factory managers, maintenance engineers and production directors.

**Content pillar: Educational content (carousel)**
Topic: "5 early signs of unplanned downtime"
Set: #CompanyName #endüstri40 #kestirimcibakım #bakımmühendisliği #üretimverimliliği

**Content pillar: Product showcase (Reels)**
Topic: A 30-second installation video for the new vibration sensor
Set: #CompanyName #endüstriyelotomasyon #IIoT #sensörteknolojisi

**Content pillar: Customer success story**
Topic: Reducing downtime at an automotive supplier facility (without naming the client)
Set: #CompanyName #otomotivyansanayi #kestirimcibakım #dijitaldönüşüm

**Content pillar: Event**
Topic: Booth announcement for an industry trade show
Set: #CompanyName #TradeShowOfficialHashtag #endüstri40 #CompanyNameAtTheShow

**Content pillar: Employer branding**
Topic: A day in the life of field engineers
Set: #CompanyNameTeam #mühendislik #kariyer #kadınmühendisler

Notice that the brand hashtag appears in every set, while the remaining tags change according to the topic of the content. This way, the algorithm understands that the account is generally positioned in the "industrial automation" space, while each post covers a different subtopic within that space.

## How do you create a branded hashtag, and when does it work?

In B2B, branded hashtags are often created with the wrong expectations. Nobody searches for #CompanyName. However, a branded hashtag does create real value in these scenarios:

- **Events and trade shows:** To bring together posts from visitors and your team.
- **Employee posts:** A shared tag your team uses when posting company culture content on their own accounts.
- **Campaigns and launches:** A temporary tag for a specific product launch or webinar series.
- **Partner and dealer network:** For dealers or solution partners to use in co-branded content.

A good branded hashtag has the following qualities:

1. **It's short and easy to type.** Ideally, it shouldn't exceed 15–20 characters.
2. **It's unique.** Search Instagram before creating it to make sure it isn't being used with a different meaning.
3. **It doesn't cause Turkish character issues.** Avoid letters that are prone to typos whenever possible.
4. **It's promoted in visible places.** Such as your bio, trade show booth, email signature and the final slide of presentations.

## Is hashtag use different for Reels, carousels and Stories?

The core logic is the same, but there are some differences by format:

- **Reels:** The format with the highest potential to stand out in Explore and the Reels feed. Here, what matters more than hashtags are the caption keywords, on-screen text and the first three seconds of the video. 3–4 well-targeted hashtags are enough.
- **Carousel:** The most-saved format for educational B2B content. Niche and solution-focused hashtags deliver the best results here, because people searching for this content are usually professionals looking for information.
- **Single image:** Used for announcements and event content. The event hashtag is critical here.
- **Story:** You can use the hashtag sticker, but in B2B the real strength of Stories lies in engaging with existing followers. Adding the official hashtag sticker during an event helps you become visible to attendees browsing that tag.

## What are the most common hashtag mistakes B2B accounts make?

These are the mistakes we encounter most often in practice:

- **Using popular B2C tags:** Tags like #instagood, #photooftheday and #follow bring no qualified audience to a B2B account and send the wrong signal on top of that.
- **Relying only on very broad tags:** With tags like #teknoloji, #iş and #başarı, your content disappears within seconds.
- **Mistaking hashtags for strategy:** Weak content won't perform even with a perfect hashtag set. Content quality comes first.
- **Not updating the library:** Industry terms change and event tags go stale. A set built six months ago may no longer work today.
- **Lack of team standards:** When different people post using different spellings (#Endüstri4.0, #endustri40, #industry40), the impact gets diluted.
- **Making decisions without measuring:** Hashtags chosen based on the feeling that "this tag looks good" often fail to deliver the expected results.

## How do you measure hashtag performance?

Instagram's native analytics are more limited than before when it comes to providing detailed data at the hashtag level. That's why hashtag performance needs to be evaluated through indirect metrics:

- **Non-follower reach rate:** One of the most important indicators for understanding the impact of hashtags and discovery. If this rate is noticeably higher on posts using a particular set, that set is doing its job.
- **Saves and shares:** The strongest signals that you've reached the right audience. Content that reaches the wrong audience gets reach but isn't saved.
- **Profile visits and follows:** These show whether people who discover you find your account interesting.
- **Website clicks:** Traffic to the link in your bio or to Story links is the metric closest to business results.
- **Follower profile:** Periodically sample who your new followers are. Are they professionals from your target industries, or irrelevant accounts?

To see in detail how to interpret these metrics, check out our article on [how to read Instagram analytics from a B2B metrics perspective](/blog/how-to-read-instagram-analytics-b2b).

Set up a simple reporting routine: At the end of each month, log the average reach, non-follower reach rate, saves and profile visits by content pillar and hashtag set in a spreadsheet. At the end of three months, replace 1–2 tags in the weakest-performing set and repeat the cycle.

## How does a hashtag strategy connect to lead generation?

Hashtags don't drive sales directly. However, they play an important role at the very top of the sales funnel, in the **awareness and discovery** stage. The chain usually works like this:

1. A maintenance manager browses the #kestirimcibakım tag or sees your carousel in their Explore feed.
2. They save the content, visit your profile and follow you.
3. Over the following weeks, they keep seeing your educational content.
4. They download a guide or submit a demo request via the link in your bio.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you need to add hashtags to every post on a B2B Instagram account?

It's not mandatory, but it's recommended. Adding at least your brand hashtag plus 2–3 industry and niche tags that describe the topic of the content to every post helps the algorithm classify it correctly. For posts with no discovery goal, such as internal announcements, you can reduce the number of tags even further.

### Should hashtags go in the caption or the first comment?

Placing them at the end of the caption is the better choice. This keeps the tags directly tied to the content and eliminates the risk of them getting lost when the post is edited. Putting them in the first comment is an old habit, and there's no strong evidence that it offers any notable advantage today.

### Is it okay to use competitors' branded hashtags?

No, it's not recommended. Using competitors' brand tags can be seen as ethically questionable, damages your professional image and leaves a negative impression on your competitor's audience. Instead, analyze the industry and niche tags your competitors use and choose the ones that fit your own strategy.

### How often should the hashtag library be updated?

As a general rule, a performance review every three months and a comprehensive library revision every six months are sufficient. Event and campaign tags, on the other hand, should be added ahead of each event according to the calendar and removed from the active list once the event is over.

## Conclusion

A B2B hashtag strategy on Instagram requires working with fewer but better tags. Define your target audience and content pillars, build a library made up of brand, industry, niche, event and location layers, establish a consistent discipline of 3–5 tags per post, and refine your sets by measuring every three months. Hashtags won't work miracles on their own. But combined with strong content, they provide a solid foundation that makes it easier for the right decision-makers to discover you.

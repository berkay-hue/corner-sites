In a healthy B2B social media content mix, **roughly 60-70% of posts should be educational, value-driven content**, **20-25% should be trust-building content** (customer stories, team, company culture), and **no more than 10-15% should be direct sales or promotional content**. These ratios are not a fixed formula but a starting point. You need to adjust them based on the platform (LinkedIn or Instagram), the length of your sales cycle, and where your target audience sits in the buying process.

Many B2B companies drift to one of two extremes on social media. Either every post turns into a product pitch, a demo invitation or a campaign announcement, or the account fills up with nothing but holiday greetings and trade show photos. Either way, the result is the same: follower numbers stall, engagement drops, and social media remains a "shop window" that contributes nothing to sales. In this article, we walk you step by step through how to set your content mix ratios, how they change by platform and funnel stage, and how to turn them into a workable content calendar.

## Why does content mix matter so much?

Content mix is the balance that determines which types of content you publish and how often. This balance directly affects three things:

- **Algorithmic reach:** LinkedIn and Instagram algorithms show content that earns engagement in the first few minutes to wider audiences. Constant sales-focused posts usually get low engagement and, over time, drag down the account's overall reach.
- **Building trust:** B2B purchasing decisions take a long time and require sign-off from multiple people. Decision-makers follow you for months before a buying need even arises. The content they see during this period determines whether they position you as an "expert solution partner" or as "a vendor that's always trying to sell."
- **Team efficiency:** A clear mix ratio eliminates the "What should we post this week?" question. The content team knows in advance what to produce and how much of it, and approval processes speed up.

In short, the right ratio is the backbone of a social media presence that speaks to both the algorithm and people. Before building that backbone, you need an overall content strategy in place. ## Do the well-known content ratio rules work in B2B?

There are a few classic rules that come up again and again in social media marketing. They all rest on the same idea: give value first, then ask.

### The 80/20 rule

80% of your content should inform, entertain or benefit your audience, and only 20% should directly promote your brand or product. It's a fairly solid starting point for B2B. However, the "80% value" category is very broad, and teams often fill it with generic industry news.

### The 4-1-1 rule

For every six posts, four are third-party or educational content, one is your own original content (blog, report, etc.), and one is directly sales-focused. This rule is better suited to accounts that curate content. Because sharing other people's content on LinkedIn company pages typically gets low reach, it's hard to apply this rule as-is in B2B.

### The 5-3-2 rule

For every ten posts, five are curated from others, three are your own content, and two are personal, people-focused posts about company culture. Its emphasis on people-focused content is a valuable reminder for B2B.

### Where these rules fall short in B2B

The classic rules split content into two buckets: "value" and "sales." In B2B, however, there's a critical layer in between: **trust and proof content.** Customer case studies, snapshots from project work, and posts that showcase your team's expertise are neither pure education nor pure sales. Yet these are usually the pieces that influence buying decisions the most. That's why we recommend a more detailed content mix model for B2B.

## What should the recommended B2B content mix look like?

The model below provides starting ratios for a B2B company that works primarily on LinkedIn and has a mid-length sales cycle:

1. **Educational and solution-focused content: 35-40%**
   Practical solutions to industry problems, how-to guides, checklists, common mistakes. Example: a "5 mistakes in warehouse inventory counts" carousel for a logistics software company.

2. **Thought leadership and opinion content: 20-25%**
   Commentary on industry trends, opinion pieces that set out the company's perspective, predictions. The goal here is less about informing and more about saying "this is how we see it." Example: "Why digital transformation in manufacturing should usually start with processes, not software."

3. **Social proof content: 15-20%**
   Customer success stories, before/after project comparisons, customer testimonials, certifications, awards, partnerships. If you can't use the customer's name, anonymized descriptions such as "a manufacturer in the food industry" work too.

4. **Company culture and people content: 10-15%**
   Team introductions, office/field snapshots, new hires, event highlights. This content shows the "face" of the company and also strengthens your employer brand.

5. **Product, service and offer content: 10-15%**
   New feature announcements, demo invitations, webinar registrations, campaigns, lead magnet promotions.

Balance the ranges against each other so the total doesn't exceed 100%. For example, if your social proof is strong, go with 20% and bring educational content down to 35%.

Notice that while the share of direct sales content looks low, content with an "indirect sales" effect (social proof + thought leadership) adds up to nearly half the total. In B2B, sales usually come not from sales content but from trust content.

## Should the ratios be different on LinkedIn and Instagram?

Yes, absolutely. The two platforms differ in purpose, user expectations and the way content is consumed.

### Recommended mix for LinkedIn

Users on LinkedIn are in a professional mindset. They're there to learn, follow their industry and build their network. That's why educational and opinion content takes center stage:

- Educational content: **40%**
- Thought leadership: **25%**
- Social proof: **15%**
- Company culture: **10%**
- Product/offer: **10%**

On LinkedIn, the document (PDF carousel) format generally works well for educational content, while short text + image tends to work well for opinion content. Posting days and times can also change the impact of this mix. It makes sense to place educational content on your highest-engagement days of the week and culture content on quieter days.

### Recommended mix for Instagram

On Instagram, a B2B audience expects more visual, more human and more "behind-the-scenes" content. Users here have stepped a little further away from their professional identities:

- Educational content (quick tips, carousels): **30%**
- Company culture and behind the scenes: **25%**
- Social proof (project visuals, shots from the field): **25%**
- Thought leadership (founder/expert videos): **10%**
- Product/offer: **10%**

On Instagram, company culture and project visuals play a much bigger role than on LinkedIn. Especially in industries with visual output, such as manufacturing, architecture, construction and industrial design, the share of social proof can go as high as 30%. If you'd like a more detailed look at a platform-specific approach, check out our article on [Instagram strategy for B2B companies](/blog/b2b-instagram-strategy).

### Using the same content on both platforms

You can use the same idea on both platforms, but not in the same format. An idea you share on LinkedIn as a 1,200-character opinion post should become a 30-second Reel or a 6-slide carousel on Instagram. This way, you can maintain each platform's ratios without increasing your content production workload.

## How do the ratios change across sales funnel stages?

Content mix should be considered not only by "content type" but also by funnel stage. For a general B2B social media account, the following balance is a good starting point:

- **Awareness (top of funnel): 50-60%** – Content that defines the problem, comments on the industry and speaks to a broad audience.
- **Consideration (middle of funnel): 25-35%** – Content that compares solution approaches, walks through case studies and shows your methodology.
- **Decision (bottom of funnel): 10-15%** – Content featuring demos, offers, your pricing approach and calls to get in touch.

### When should you change the ratios?

These ratios can be deliberately shifted depending on the company's situation:

- **New companies or companies entering a new market:** If nobody knows you yet, decision-stage content will have little impact. Raise the awareness share to 65-70%.
- **Companies with a large following but few conversions:** Increase consideration-stage content. Case studies and "how we work" content can go up to 35-40%.
- **Launch or campaign periods:** You can temporarily raise decision-stage content to 25-30% for a 3-4 week period. Just don't forget to return to your normal balance once the campaign ends.

An important note: sales content on social media won't fill your pipeline on its own. Organic content builds trust, but a fast, scalable lead flow usually needs to be supported by targeted outbound efforts. ## Does format mix matter too?

The mix of content formats affects results just as much as the mix of content types. Using the same format all the time both tires your audience and causes you to miss people with different consumption habits.

A sample format mix for a LinkedIn company page:

- **Document/carousel: 30%** – Ideal for educational content; save and share rates are usually high.
- **Single image + text: 30%** – Opinion content, announcements, social proof.
- **Short video: 20%** – Expert explainers, team introductions, event recaps.
- **Text-only or poll: 10%** – For quick engagement and gathering audience opinions.
- **Link posts (blog, report): 10%** – Should be kept low, since external links can limit reach.

For Instagram, a structure built mainly around Reels, carousels and Stories works better. Whether Reels are truly necessary for B2B depends on your industry and resources.

## How do you turn the ratios into a content calendar?

Setting the ratios is easy; the real challenge is applying them consistently every week. The following steps make the process concrete.

### 1. Set your monthly posting volume

First, measure your capacity realistically. For example, if you plan to post 4 times a week on your LinkedIn company page, you'll need to produce about **16 posts** a month.

### 2. Convert the ratios into post counts

Applying the LinkedIn mix to 16 posts:

- Educational content (40%): **6-7 posts**
- Thought leadership (25%): **4 posts**
- Social proof (15%): **2-3 posts**
- Company culture (10%): **1-2 posts**
- Product/offer (10%): **1-2 posts**

Where you get fractions, round based on that month's priorities. For example, if there's a webinar that month, round offer content up to 2.

### 3. Build a weekly rhythm

Instead of scattering content randomly across the month, establish a weekly rhythm. Example:

- **Monday:** Thought leadership (opening the week with a point of view)
- **Tuesday:** Educational carousel
- **Wednesday:** Social proof or educational content (alternating)
- **Thursday:** Educational content or company culture / product content (alternating)

This rhythm eliminates the risk of posting two sales pieces back to back, and your audience gets a similar experience every week. To turn this structure into a calendar template, you can use our [guide to creating a LinkedIn content calendar](/blog/b2b-linkedin-content-calendar).

### 4. Cross-reference with content pillars

Content type ratios determine "what kind" of content you share; content pillars determine "what topics" you share about. For example, an industrial automation company's three content pillars might be "energy efficiency," "maintenance costs" and "workplace safety." Distributing 6 educational posts across these three pillars, two each, over the month ensures balance in both type and topic.

### 5. Check for deviations at the end of the month

There's usually a gap between plan and reality. Last-minute trade show announcements, unexpected events or "let's post this too" requests from management throw the ratios off. At the end of each month, count your posts by category, and if the deviation exceeds 10%, rebalance the next month accordingly.

## What ratios should employee and founder accounts use?

In B2B social media, the company page alone isn't enough. The personal accounts of founders, executives and the sales team often generate more reach and trust than the company page. However, the ratios for these accounts are different.

Recommended mix for personal accounts:

- **Personal experiences and lessons learned: 35-40%** – For example, "Something I noticed in a customer meeting this week..." or "3 lessons from 10 years in the field."
- **Industry opinions and commentary: 25-30%**
- **Educational content: 15-20%**
- **Sharing or commenting on company content: 10-15%**
- **Direct sales or offers: under 5%**

On personal accounts, the share of sales content should be even lower than on the company page. The power of personal accounts comes from the fact that they don't seem to be selling. A founder candidly describing how they solved a customer's problem can be more effective than dozens of promotional posts on the company page. ## How do you measure whether your ratios are working?

The recommended ratios are a starting point; only your own data can show you the right ratio. To get there, tag every post by content type and compare the following metrics by category each month:

- **Engagement rate:** Which content type gets the most reactions, comments and shares per impression?
- **Saves and shares:** These show whether your educational content in particular is genuinely seen as valuable.
- **Profile visits and follower growth:** Which content draws new people to your account?
- **Click-through rate:** Shows the conversion potential of offer and product content.
- **Follower demographics:** Is your content reaching people with the right job titles, industries and company sizes?

### A sample analysis scenario

Say three months of data show that your social proof content consistently gets noticeably above-average engagement, while your product content consistently performs the worst. The answer here isn't to drop product content altogether, but to combine the two. Instead of promoting the product directly, you can switch to a case-study format that shows how the product solved a customer's problem. That way, you increase your share of social proof and deliver your product message more effectively at the same time.

To interpret these metrics correctly, you need to know LinkedIn's built-in analytics screens well. Our article on [how to read LinkedIn page analytics](/blog/how-to-read-linkedin-page-analytics) will guide you through this.

### How often should you update the ratios?

- **Monthly:** Check the deviation between plan and reality.
- **Quarterly:** Review performance by category and adjust ratios by 5-10%.
- **Annually:** If company goals, the product roadmap or the target audience have changed, revisit the mix model from scratch.

Don't make major changes based on a single month's data. Algorithm fluctuations, holiday periods or a single viral post can make short-term data misleading.

## What are the most common content mix mistakes?

These are the mistakes we encounter most often at B2B companies in our consulting work:

- **Filling the "value" category with generic news:** Sharing industry news without commentary doesn't count as educational content. Unless you add your own perspective to the news, you're not creating value.
- **Treating special days as a separate category:** Holiday, commemoration and special-day posts can add up to 3-4 a month. They quietly throw off your ratios. Count special-day posts within your company culture share and keep them limited.
- **Stacking sales content back to back:** Putting all of the month's offer content in the same week makes the account come across as a sales account that week.
- **Using the same ratio on every platform:** Assuming that a mix that works well on LinkedIn will also work on Instagram turns your Instagram account into a corporate brochure.
- **Letting the approval process dictate the ratios:** Educational and opinion content gets delayed because it goes through more rounds of approval, while quickly approved announcements fill the gap. In the end, approval speed, not the plan, determines the mix. Differentiating the approval workflow by content type helps solve this problem.
- **Not measuring the ratio:** Many teams think they're working with a specific ratio, but when they count their posts retrospectively, they find the actual mix is very different.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the maximum share of sales content on B2B social media?

For company pages, sales and promotional content should not exceed 10-15% of total posts. During launch or campaign periods, this share can rise to 25-30% for a few weeks, but you should return to your normal balance after the campaign. On personal accounts, direct sales content should stay below 5%.

### Is the 80/20 rule enough for B2B companies?

The 80/20 rule is a good starting point, but it's too general for B2B. In B2B, there's a trust-focused layer between value and sales content, including customer stories, case studies and content that demonstrates expertise. Planning this layer as a separate category is a more results-oriented approach when it comes to influencing buying decisions.

### Can I use the same content ratios on LinkedIn and Instagram?

It's better not to. On LinkedIn, educational and thought leadership content should dominate, while on Instagram, company culture, behind-the-scenes and visual social proof content should make up a larger share. By sharing the same idea in different formats on each platform, you can maintain both mixes without increasing your production workload.

### How often should I update my content ratios?

It's recommended to check your planned versus actual mix every month and to review your ratios every three months based on performance by category. Making major changes based on a single month's data can be misleading. When company goals or the target audience change, the mix model should be reconsidered from scratch.

## Conclusion

The right content mix on B2B social media is a balance that puts the emphasis on educational, value-driven content, makes regular room for trust-building social proof and culture content, and deliberately limits direct sales content. As a starting point, a ratio of 60-70% value, 20-25% trust and 10-15% sales provides a solid framework. But real success comes from adapting these ratios to the platform, funnel stage and your company's growth phase, turning them into a weekly rhythm and updating them regularly based on data.

Setting ratios on paper is easy; the hard part is applying them consistently every week and measuring the results.

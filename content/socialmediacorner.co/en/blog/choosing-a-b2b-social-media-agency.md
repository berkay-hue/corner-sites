To choose the right B2B social media agency, you need to look at **whether the agency understands the B2B sales cycle, how much strategic depth it has on LinkedIn and Instagram, how it approaches measurement, and how transparent its way of working is**. Put another way, a good B2B agency doesn't report "how many likes we got"; it reports "how many qualified conversations we contributed to." For a B2B company, an agency that grows your follower count but never brings your sales team a single meaningful conversation is nothing more than expensive decoration.

In this article, we walk step by step through the criteria you should clarify before sitting down with an agency, the questions you can ask at the proposal stage, the red flags to watch for, and a simple scoring model that makes it easier to compare proposals. The goal is to help you choose **the agency that will contribute the most to your business**, rather than "the most creative agency."

## Why isn't a generalist social media agency enough for B2B?

The difference between B2C and B2B social media isn't just a difference in tone. The business model itself is different, and that difference affects everything you expect from an agency.

- **Purchasing decisions are made by a committee, not one person.** When a company buys software, industrial equipment or consulting services, the technical team, finance, procurement and senior management are usually all involved in the same decision. Content needs to deliver different messages to these different people.
- **The sales cycle is long.** A B2C product can be bought in minutes, while in B2B, months can pass between first contact and signature. The job of social media isn't to close a sale with a single click; it's to keep the brand top of mind and trusted throughout that long process.
- **The target audience is small but valuable.** In Turkey, the number of decision-makers in a given industry may be limited to a few thousand people. Going viral is often not the goal; consistently reaching the right 500 people is far more valuable.
- **Content requires expertise.** Explaining the integration advantages of a logistics software product or the certification process for a chemical product requires much more industry knowledge than shooting a Reel to a trending song.

What happens when a generalist agency doesn't understand these dynamics? Typically, content is produced for LinkedIn with an Instagram mindset, success is measured in likes, and six months later management can't answer the question, "What has social media actually done for us?"

## Does the agency truly understand the B2B sales cycle?

In the first meeting, pay attention to how hard the agency tries to understand your business. A good B2B agency asks questions before pitching its service packages:

1. How long is your average sales cycle?
2. Which industries and company sizes make up your ideal customer profile (ICP)?
3. Which job titles play a role in the purchasing decision?
4. Through which channels does your sales team currently engage with customers?
5. Roughly what is a customer's lifetime value?

If these questions aren't asked, the agency is most likely getting ready to adapt a ready-made template to you. By contrast, an agency that asks, "Can we hold a joint meeting with your marketing and sales teams?" shows that it sees social media as part of the sales funnel.

**A concrete test:** Ask the agency to describe what a decision-maker in your industry, for example, a procurement manager at a manufacturing company, encounters on LinkedIn during the day and what kind of content might catch their attention. The depth of the answer will tell you a lot.

## Which platforms is it truly an expert in?

"We manage all platforms" sounds good, but for B2B it is often unnecessary. For most B2B companies, two platforms are decisive: **LinkedIn** and **Instagram**.

### How can you tell if an agency has LinkedIn expertise?

LinkedIn is the center of B2B social media. The agency needs to be competent in the following areas:

- Understanding the different roles of the company page and personal profiles
- Using document (carousel) posts, polls, video and text posts according to the objective
- Establishing a consistent publishing cadence and running it according to plan
- Supporting executives' personal brands (thought leadership)
- Reading LinkedIn analytics and reflecting the insights in content decisions

Ask the agency for a sample monthly plan. The plan shouldn't be just a list of formats like "Monday: image, Wednesday: video"; it should show which audience and which objective each piece of content is created for. If you want a good point of reference, our [guide to building a LinkedIn content calendar for B2B](/blog/b2b-linkedin-content-calendar) will help you evaluate the plan the agency presents.

### How should Instagram be used in B2B?

In B2B, Instagram is generally valuable for employer branding, company culture, showing the product in use in the field, and trade show/event visibility. The agency shouldn't run Instagram as a copy of LinkedIn, but it shouldn't position it like a B2C brand either. The right question is: "Which business goal are you tying Instagram to for us?" If the answer is something as general as "brand awareness," ask them to go deeper: Among which audience? With which message? Measured how?

## Does it deliver strategy, or just produce content?

Many agencies are essentially content production studios: they design visuals, write copy and post it. This model has its own value, but most B2B companies need more than that.

A strategic agency should be able to deliver the following outputs:

- **Target audience and persona definitions:** The main pain points, objections and interests of each persona
- **Messaging architecture:** The brand's 3–5 core messages and how they map to content categories
- **Content pillars:** For example, 40% industry insight and education, 25% customer success and use cases, 20% company culture, 15% product and campaigns
- **Channel roles:** The job of LinkedIn, Instagram and any other channels
- **Measurement framework:** Which metric corresponds to which goal

This structure is a lasting asset that stays with your company even if you change agencies. At the proposal stage, be sure to ask whether the agency will deliver a strategy document within the first 30 days. If you want to know in advance how a good strategy is built, our [step-by-step guide to creating a B2B content strategy](/blog/how-to-build-b2b-content-strategy) provides a solid reference for comparing the agency's approach.

## How does it measure and report on success?

This is perhaps the most decisive criterion when choosing an agency, because work that can't be measured can't be defended at budget time.

### The right metrics hierarchy

In B2B, it helps to think about metrics in three layers:

1. **Visibility metrics:** Impressions, reach, follower growth. Important, but meaningless on their own.
2. **Engagement and interest metrics:** Engagement rate, click-through rate, video watch time, profile visits, comments from the target audience.
3. **Business impact metrics:** Qualified traffic to the website, form submissions, demo requests, sales conversations sourced from social media, influenced sales opportunities.

A weak agency stops its reporting at the first layer. A good agency will ask you for CRM access, a UTM structure or a regular feedback loop with the sales team in order to reach the third layer.

### Ask for a sample report

Ask the agency for a real monthly report sample with client details redacted. Look at the following in the report:

- Does it contain only numbers, or also commentary and recommendations?
- Does it compare results against the previous month and against targets?
- Is there a "Here's what we learned this month, here's what we'll change next month" section?

Knowing in advance how to calculate the return on your social media investment strengthens your hand in negotiations. Our article on [how to measure B2B social media ROI](/blog/measure-b2b-social-media-roi) clarifies which data you should request from the agency.

## How will content production and approval work?

In B2B companies, the biggest bottleneck in social media is not content production but **the approval process**. When legal, the technical team, the product manager and senior management comment on the same post at different times, planned content can sit waiting for weeks.

Ask the agency these questions:

- How far in advance do you submit content for approval? (Ideally at least 1–2 weeks ahead)
- Which tool or format do you use for approvals?
- How many rounds of revisions are included in the price?
- For technical content, who do you need to talk to for information, and how often?
- How does the process work during urgent news or a crisis?

An agency that doesn't propose a clear approval workflow will wear out your team too by the end of the first month. ## How competent is it in ad management?

Organic content builds trust and continuity, but in B2B, paid advertising is often necessary to reach specific decision-makers quickly. LinkedIn ads are a powerful tool for B2B because they allow targeting by job title, industry, company size and even specific company lists; however, since cost per click can be higher than on other platforms, a poorly structured campaign can burn through the budget quickly.

To gauge the agency's advertising competence, ask the following:

- How do you set up targeting? Is it based on job title alone, or on combinations of company lists and industries?
- Do you prefer Lead Gen Forms or driving traffic to the website, and why?
- How do you structure retargeting?
- How do you plan A/B tests, and when do you stop a variation?
- In whose name is the ad account opened, and who owns the data?

The last question is especially important. **The ad account and all historical data should belong to your company.** You shouldn't lose months of accumulated data when you switch agencies.

## Does it contribute to social selling and employee advocacy?

In B2B, people engage with the people who work at a company more than with company pages. A personal post from a general manager, sales director or technical expert can attract far more attention than the same content on the company page.

So check whether the agency provides support in the following areas:

- Personal brand content for executives (including ghostwriting)
- A ready-made content pool that employees can share
- LinkedIn profile optimization and engagement training for the sales team
- Tracking the performance of employee posts

To get ideas on how this area can be structured, take a look at [our article on setting up an employee advocacy program on LinkedIn for B2B companies](/blog/b2b-employee-advocacy-linkedin).

It's worth making a distinction here: a social media agency strengthens the **content and visibility** side of social selling. Systematic outbound sales, prospect list building and appointment setting, however, are a separate area of expertise. ## Who will be on the team?

The proposal presentation is usually given by the agency's most experienced people. But you need to know in advance who will manage your account once the contract is signed.

Points you should clarify:

- **Who will the account manager be, and how many clients are they handling at the same time?** An account manager running many clients simultaneously means less time devoted to your brand.
- **Does the content writer have B2B experience?** A writer who understands technical topics and can explain them in plain language is a B2B agency's most critical asset.
- **Are the design, video and advertising specialists part of the agency's in-house team, or outsourced?**
- **How is the process managed if the team changes?** Is there documentation for knowledge transfer?

If possible, ask for a short introductory meeting with the people who will work on your account during the proposal stage.

## How much does it cost?

B2B social media agency fees vary across a very wide range depending on scope, number of platforms, content volume, video production, ad management and the depth of strategic consulting. That's why it's more useful to understand the pricing model than to look for an "average price."

### Common pricing models

- **Monthly retainer:** The most common model. Covers a set amount of content, reporting and management services.
- **Project-based fee:** For one-off work such as a strategy document, page setup or a launch campaign.
- **Ad management fee:** Charged either as a flat fee or as a percentage of the ad budget.
- **Hybrid model:** Fixed fee + performance-based bonus.

### A simple calculation for comparing proposals

Let's consider two proposals (the figures are only meant to illustrate the calculation logic):

- **Agency A:** TRY 40,000 per month, 12 LinkedIn + 8 Instagram posts per month, monthly report.
- **Agency B:** TRY 55,000 per month, 12 LinkedIn + 8 Instagram posts per month, strategy document, personal brand content for two executives, biweekly performance meeting, ad management included.

On a per-post basis, Agency A looks like TRY 2,000 and Agency B like TRY 2,750. But if with Agency A you have to pay a separate specialist or a second agency for ad management and produce executive content with your own team, the total cost picture may flip. **Looking only at the monthly fee without a line-by-line comparison is the most common mistake in choosing a B2B agency.**

### Ask about hidden costs

- Are additional revisions charged?
- Are stock images, music licenses and video shoots billed separately?
- Who covers the cost of reporting tools?
- What are the minimum contract term and termination conditions?

## 10 critical questions to ask at the proposal stage

Keep this list handy during your meetings:

1. Have you worked with companies in our industry or with a similar sales cycle? What results did you achieve?
2. What will you deliver in the first 30, 60 and 90 days?
3. Which metrics do you use to define success, and will we set them together?
4. How does your content approval process work?
5. How much time will you need from our team when you need technical information?
6. Who will own the ad accounts and all content files?
7. Who will manage our account, and how many clients are they working with at the same time?
8. How do you show business impact in your reports?
9. What action plan do you follow if performance falls short of expectations?
10. How does the handover process work at the end of the contract?

Pay attention to how concrete the answers are. Vague answers such as "We'll adjust everything to your needs" may indicate that the agency doesn't have a clear system in place for that area.

## What counts as a red flag?

Be cautious if you see several of the following signs together:

- **Follower count guarantees:** Promises like "10,000 followers in three months" are meaningless for B2B and often point to low-quality growth tactics.
- **Proposing a ready-made package without getting to know you:** Agencies that hand you a price list in the first meeting but don't ask about your business model.
- **Reports that only include likes and reach:** Reporting that doesn't connect to business impact.
- **Ambiguity about account ownership:** Wanting to open the ad account in the agency's own name.
- **Long, inflexible contracts:** Not offering a trial period or reasonable termination terms.
- **Insisting that you be on every platform:** Steering you toward channels where your target audience isn't present, which spreads your resources thin.
- **Being unable to share references:** Confidentiality is understandable, but being unable to offer any concrete examples, case summaries or reference calls is a cause for concern.

## A simple scoring model for comparing proposals

When you meet with more than one agency, you can use a weighted scorecard to make the decision easier. Score each criterion from 1 to 5 and multiply by its weight:

- **B2B and industry understanding:** 20%
- **Strategy and planning depth:** 20%
- **Measurement and reporting:** 20%
- **LinkedIn and Instagram expertise:** 15%
- **Team and communication quality:** 10%
- **Ad management competence:** 10%
- **Price / scope balance:** 5%

The low weight on price is a deliberate choice. The cost of six months with the wrong agency is usually far higher than the fee difference between two proposals: lost time, starting over and the effort your team has spent all need to be factored in.

Example: If an agency scores 5 on B2B understanding, 4 on strategy, 4 on measurement, 5 on platform expertise, 4 on team, 3 on advertising and 3 on price, its weighted score is calculated as follows:
(5×0.20) + (4×0.20) + (4×0.20) + (5×0.15) + (4×0.10) + (3×0.10) + (3×0.05) = 1.00 + 0.80 + 0.80 + 0.75 + 0.40 + 0.30 + 0.15 = **4.20**

When you run this calculation for all candidates, your decision rests on data rather than gut feeling.

## What should you expect in the first 90 days after starting with an agency?

Choosing the right agency is half the job; the other half is managing expectations correctly in the first few months.

**Days 1–30 – Discovery and setup:**
- Stakeholder interviews, meeting with the sales team
- Analysis of existing accounts and competitors
- Strategy document, personas and messaging architecture
- Measurement infrastructure: UTM structure, defining goals

**Days 31–60 – Publishing and testing:**
- Regular content publishing
- Testing different formats and messages
- If needed, launching the first ad campaigns with a small budget

**Days 61–90 – Optimization:**
- Analysis of data from the initial period
- Scaling the content types that work and dropping those that don't
- Updating messaging based on feedback from the sales team

In B2B, how soon meaningful business results appear depends on the length of your sales cycle. What you should expect in the first 90 days isn't a sales explosion; it's signs that **the right audience is being reached, the quality of engagement is improving and a clear learning loop has been established**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should B2B companies manage social media with an agency or an in-house team?

It depends on your content volume, your in-house team's expertise and your budget. For many B2B companies, the most efficient model is hybrid: strategy, content production and ad management sit with the agency, while industry knowledge, approvals and executive visibility stay in-house. This structure balances specialist expertise with brand knowledge.

### How long does it take to see results from a B2B social media agency?

Initial improvements in reach and engagement can usually be seen within a few weeks. However, business results such as qualified leads and sales opportunities can take several months, depending on the length of your sales cycle. Planning for an evaluation period of at least six months is more realistic.

### What should I watch out for when signing a contract with an agency?

Make sure the scope (number of posts, platforms, revision allowance, reporting frequency) is clearly defined in writing. State in the contract that the ad accounts, content files and data belong to your company. A reasonable notice period for termination and how the handover process will work at the end of the contract should also be included.

### Does B2B need both LinkedIn and Instagram?

For most B2B companies, LinkedIn is the main channel, while Instagram plays a complementary role for employer branding, company culture and visual product storytelling. If your resources are limited, it's wiser to first build a strong foundation on LinkedIn and then add Instagram by tying it to a specific objective.

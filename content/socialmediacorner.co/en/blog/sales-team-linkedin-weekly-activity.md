For most teams, a realistic and sustainable target for a B2B sales team member is **3-5 hours of LinkedIn activity per week**. In practice, that works out to a focused routine of **30-45 minutes a day** and covers the following each week: **2-3 original posts**, **15-25 meaningful comments**, **20-40 targeted connection requests** and **10-20 personalized messages**. What matters most is not the total time but whether the activity is consistent, focused on your target audience and measurable.

In this article, we'll answer the "how much per week" question role by role. We'll walk step by step through how to build a daily routine, when the numbers start to work against you and how to embed this discipline across the team. If you're not yet comfortable with the fundamentals of social selling, we recommend first reading our [step-by-step social selling process for B2B teams](/blog/what-is-social-selling-b2b-guide). This article focuses on the "dosage" of that process.

## Weekly activity framework: The short answer

Telling your sales team to "be active on LinkedIn" isn't enough. If it's unclear what should be done and how much, the team will either do nothing at all or fill time by liking posts at random. The framework below is a good starting point for most B2B sales teams:

- **Total time:** 3-5 hours per week (30-45 minutes a day, 5 working days a week)
- **Original posts:** 2-3 posts per week
- **Comments:** 15-25 meaningful comments per week (at least 2-3 sentences that add value)
- **Connection requests:** 20-40 targeted requests per week (to people who match your ideal customer profile)
- **Personalized messages:** 10-20 messages per week (to new connections and warm prospects)
- **Profile visits and research:** 1-1.5 hours per week tracking target accounts

These numbers aren't hard rules. They should be adjusted based on team size, sales cycle length, the size of your target market and each person's role. Still, without a reference point, activity can be neither measured nor improved.

## Why the real question isn't "how much" but "how much, and on what?"

Many sales managers evaluate LinkedIn activity by volume: how many connection requests were sent, how many posts were published. But in social selling, volume only delivers results when it's paired with the right targeting.

Let's illustrate with an example. Sales rep A sends 100 connection requests a week but doesn't target them. Most of the people who accept fall outside the ideal customer profile. Sales rep B sends 30 requests a week, but every one of them goes to a decision-maker on the target account list whose content B has already engaged with. A few months later, B's network will be smaller, but its potential to turn into sales opportunities will be far higher.

That's why weekly activity should be thought of in three layers:

1. **Visibility:** Showing up in your target audience's feed through posts and comments
2. **Relationship:** Becoming recognizable through connection requests, messages and two-way engagement
3. **Opportunity:** Moving warm prospects into meetings, demos or discovery calls

The time devoted to each layer should vary according to the person's role.

## How does weekly activity vary by role on a sales team?

Expecting the same LinkedIn performance from everyone on the same team is a common mistake. As the responsibilities of a role change, so does its focus on LinkedIn.

### SDR / BDR (business development representatives)

The core job of SDRs is to create new opportunities. That's why connections and messaging carry the most weight in their LinkedIn activity.

- **Weekly time:** 5-7 hours (as part of their prospecting time)
- **Posts:** 1-2 per week (mostly observations, industry commentary, short insights)
- **Comments:** 20-30 per week, especially on posts by people at target accounts
- **Connection requests:** 40-80 targeted requests per week (keeping LinkedIn's weekly invitation limits in mind)
- **Messages:** 20-40 personalized messages per week

For SDRs, LinkedIn is part of a multichannel prospecting process that works alongside email and phone. LinkedIn alone isn't enough. Supporting LinkedIn touchpoints with B2B data tools like [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya) to access prospects' work emails and company information makes it easier to build a consistent touch sequence across channels.

### AE (sales representatives / account executives)

AEs manage active opportunities and move them toward close. On LinkedIn, their real job is to build trust and develop relationships with different members of the buying committee.

- **Weekly time:** 3-4 hours
- **Posts:** 2-3 per week (insights into customer problems, process walkthroughs, industry analysis)
- **Comments:** 15-20 per week, mainly on posts by people in open opportunities and industry leaders
- **Connection requests:** 15-25 per week (especially secondary decision-makers in active opportunities)
- **Messages:** 10-15 per week (follow-ups, content sharing, post-meeting touchpoints)

### Sales manager / sales director

Managers support both their own network and the team's visibility.

- **Weekly time:** 2-3 hours
- **Posts:** 1-2 per week (leadership perspective, market assessment, team wins)
- **Comments:** 10-15 per week, including on their own team's posts
- **Connection requests:** 10-20 per week (senior decision-makers, strategic accounts)
- **Messages:** 5-10 per week (senior-level relationship management)

When a manager comments on team members' posts, it boosts engagement in the first few hours and helps the post reach more people. It also signals to the team that the process matters.

### Founder / CEO (especially at small and mid-sized B2B companies)

At many B2B companies, the founder is the strongest voice in the sales process. The founder's presence on LinkedIn directly shapes brand perception.

- **Weekly time:** 2-3 hours (additional time can be set aside for content preparation)
- **Posts:** 2-3 per week (vision, lessons learned, industry views)
- **Comments:** 10-15 per week
- **Messages:** Selective; mostly responding to inbound requests and strategic relationships

## How many posts should you publish per week?

The ideal posting frequency for a sales team is **2-3 original posts per week**. This frequency helps the algorithm recognize you as a consistent content creator without wearing out your followers.

Posting 5 or more times a week could, in theory, increase visibility. But for a sales team, it creates two problems: First, it becomes harder to maintain content quality. Second, the time spent preparing posts is taken directly away from selling activities.

An effective weekly content mix for a sales rep might look like this:

- **1 insight post:** A common problem that came up in customer conversations and your perspective on it (without naming the customer)
- **1 educational post:** A practical tip, checklist or approach that's useful to your target audience
- **1 personal/company post (optional):** Event notes, a moment from the team, a lesson learned

For example, an AE at a B2B software company might share: "Most of the finance directors we spoke with this month said the same thing: The real time drain in reporting isn't collecting the data, it's checking whether the data is accurate. I've put together three approaches teams are trying to solve this…" A post like this doesn't sell a product, but it gets the right person to think, "this person understands my problem."

The impact of your posts also depends on how strong your profile is. When someone who sees your content clicks through to your profile, they should find a clear value proposition. ## How do you build a 30-45 minute daily routine?

The best way for a sales team to stay active on LinkedIn sustainably is to turn the activity into a routine tied to the calendar. LinkedIn work done "whenever there's time" usually doesn't get done at all.

The routine below takes 30-45 minutes a day and can be split into two blocks:

### Morning block (15-20 minutes)

1. **Check notifications and messages (5 minutes):** Respond to incoming messages, accepted connections and comments on your posts.
2. **Scan your target list's feed (5-10 minutes):** Look at recent posts from people at your target accounts. Leave meaningful comments on 3-5 of them.
3. **If it's a posting day, publish your post (5 minutes):** Publish your pre-prepared content and make a note to respond to comments within the first hour.

### Afternoon block (15-25 minutes)

1. **Send new connection requests (5-10 minutes):** 5-8 targeted requests a day, ideally with a short, context-based note.
2. **Send personalized messages (10 minutes):** 2-4 messages a day to newly accepted connections or warm prospects.
3. **Log notes in your CRM (5 minutes):** Record important interactions, meeting requests and warm signals.

It also helps to add a weekly **60-minute planning block**. For example, Friday afternoon can be used to draft the next week's 2-3 posts and update the target account list. Once you factor in this block, the total weekly time lands in the 3-5 hour range.

## How many connection requests and messages should you send?

This is one of the questions sales teams ask most often, because the wrong dosage can lead to account restrictions and damage to brand perception.

### Connection requests

LinkedIn limits the number of invitations you can send per week. This limit can vary depending on the account's history and the acceptance rate of its invitations. Rather than relying on an exact figure, sending **5-10 targeted requests a day** is both a safe and manageable level for most sales professionals.

The metric you really need to watch is the **acceptance rate**. If your acceptance rate is low, sending more requests isn't the answer. You need to revisit your targeting, your profile and your invitation note. Our [article on increasing your LinkedIn connection request acceptance rate](/blog/linkedin-connection-acceptance-rate) offers concrete tactics on this.

A practical approach:

- Visit the person's profile before sending a request
- If possible, comment on one of their recent posts
- Send the connection request 1-2 days later
- In your note, reference a shared context (a post, the industry, an event); don't make a sales pitch

### Messages

Sending a sales message immediately after a connection is accepted is the most common mistake in social selling. A healthier sequence looks like this:

1. **After acceptance (within 1-2 days):** A short thank-you and a sentence explaining why you connected. Asking a question is optional.
2. **1-2 weeks later:** Share a piece of content, an insight or a resource the person might find interesting.
3. **If there's engagement:** Suggest a brief call or an exchange of ideas.

10-20 messages a week is enough to run this sequence for people at different stages. If you need to reach out directly to people you've never interacted with before, our [examples of LinkedIn cold sales messages](/blog/linkedin-cold-sales-message-examples) will be a good starting point.

## Can being too active hurt you?

Yes, it can. Being "too active" on LinkedIn carries three potential risks:

- **Account restrictions:** Sending a large number of connection requests in a short time, especially with a low acceptance rate, can lead to temporary account restrictions. Aggressive use of automation tools may also conflict with LinkedIn's terms of service.
- **Perception problems:** If your target sees you in their feed every day and every post you share is about your product, instead of becoming interested they may start blocking or unfollowing you.
- **Eroded selling time:** An AE who spends 2-3 hours a day on LinkedIn can't devote enough time to following up on active opportunities and attending meetings.

To find the right balance, ask yourself: **"Does this activity increase the likelihood that someone in my target audience will meet with me?"** If the answer is no, cut back on that activity.

## How do you measure weekly activity?

Activity that isn't measured eventually either declines or drifts from its purpose. When evaluating a sales team's LinkedIn performance, you need to track two types of metrics together.

### Input metrics (activity)

- Number of posts per week
- Number of meaningful comments per week
- Number of connection requests sent
- Number of personalized messages sent

### Output metrics (results)

- Connection request acceptance rate
- Message reply rate
- Share of profile views coming from the target audience
- Number of meetings sourced from LinkedIn
- Sales opportunities and pipeline value sourced from LinkedIn

Input metrics show the team's discipline, while output metrics show whether the strategy is working. If a rep is hitting every activity target but not booking any meetings, the problem isn't time; it's targeting or messaging.

To clarify which metrics to report on, our [guide to B2B social media KPIs](/blog/b2b-social-media-kpis) will be helpful.

A practical tip: Set aside 5-10 minutes for LinkedIn in your weekly sales meeting. Have each rep briefly answer these three questions:

1. How many warm conversations came from LinkedIn this week?
2. Which post or message got the best response?
3. Which 5-10 target accounts will I focus on next week?

## How do you roll it out across the team?

A phased transition plan works well for turning LinkedIn activity from an individual preference into a team discipline. The 90-day approach below can be adapted to most B2B sales teams.

### First 30 days: Building the foundation

- Every team member's profile is reviewed and updated
- A shared ideal customer profile and target account list are defined for the team
- Weekly targets are kept low: 1 post, 10 comments, 15-20 connection requests
- A daily 20-30 minute routine is added to the calendar

### Days 31-60: Building rhythm

- The posting target is raised to 2 per week
- The comment target is raised to 15-20 and connection requests to 25-40
- A shared document is created where the team collects post ideas
- In collaboration with the marketing team, content drafts are prepared for reps to use

### Days 61-90: Optimizing

- Input and output metrics are compared
- The best-performing post types and messaging approaches are shared with the team
- Role-based targets (SDR, AE, manager) are finalized
- LinkedIn activity is integrated into a multichannel sales sequence alongside email and phone touchpoints

## What are the most common mistakes?

The most common mistakes sales teams make with LinkedIn activity are:

- **Only liking posts:** A like is the lowest-effort form of engagement, and no one remembers you because of a like. A 2-3 sentence comment is far more effective.
- **Inconsistent activity:** Two hours a day one week, then nothing for the next three. What makes an impact on LinkedIn isn't intensity but consistency.
- **Pitching your product in every post:** Most of your posts should deliver value to your target audience; content that talks directly about your product should be kept to a minimum.
- **Sending a sales message the moment a connection is accepted:** A pitch made before trust is established usually goes unanswered.
- **Spending time on people outside your target audience:** What matters isn't the size of your network but whether it's made up of the right people.
- **Not measuring activity:** A process that isn't measured can't be improved, and managers won't take ownership of it.
- **Simply resharing company content:** Resharing a company post without comment has very little impact. Adding your own perspective when you share delivers much better results.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much time should a sales rep spend on LinkedIn each day?

For most sales reps, 30-45 minutes a day is enough and sustainable. This time can be split into two blocks, one in the morning and one in the afternoon. For roles focused on creating new opportunities, such as SDRs, it can go up to 60-80 minutes a day.

### How many LinkedIn posts should a sales team publish per week?

2-3 original posts per week is the ideal frequency for sales reps. It maintains visibility without lowering content quality or consuming too much selling time. Teams that are just getting started can begin with 1 post a week and increase gradually.

### How many connection requests is it safe to send on LinkedIn per week?

LinkedIn limits the number of weekly invitations, and this limit can vary by account. 5-10 targeted requests a day, or roughly 25-50 a week, is both a safe and manageable range for most users. Keeping your acceptance rate high matters more than increasing the number.

### How do you measure the impact of LinkedIn activity on sales?

Alongside activity metrics (posts, comments, requests, messages), you should also track outcome metrics: acceptance rate, message reply rate, number of meetings sourced from LinkedIn and pipeline value. Recording this data in your CRM with source information makes LinkedIn's contribution to sales visible.

## Conclusion

The short answer to how active a sales team should be on LinkedIn each week is **3-5 hours**. But what really makes the difference is how that time is used. When tied to a consistent routine, 2-3 valuable posts, 15-25 meaningful comments, 20-40 targeted connection requests and 10-20 personalized messages a week turn LinkedIn into a powerful sales channel.

Setting role-based targets, tying activity to the calendar and regularly tracking both input and output metrics form the foundation of this process. Remember: The team that wins on LinkedIn isn't the one that posts the most, but the one that builds the most consistent relationships with the right people.

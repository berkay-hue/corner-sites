To set up Meta Business Manager, go to business.facebook.com, sign in with your personal Facebook account and create a business portfolio using your corporate email address. Then connect your Facebook Page, Instagram account, ad account and Meta Pixel to that portfolio. Setup is complete once you've assigned role-based access to team members and completed domain verification and business verification. Technically, the process takes an hour or two. For B2B companies, the time-consuming part is figuring out who currently controls each asset and designing the right permission structure.

At many B2B companies, the Instagram account is tied to an intern's personal profile. The ad account was opened by a former agency, and nobody knows who has the Pixel. This disorder becomes a serious problem once ad campaigns launch: lost access, measurement gaps and security risks. In this guide, we explain step by step how to set up Meta Business Manager from scratch to meet a B2B company's needs, the order in which to proceed and how to avoid the most common mistakes.

## What is Meta Business Manager and why does it matter for B2B companies?

Meta Business Manager is the management tool that brings all your Meta assets, including Facebook Pages, Instagram accounts, ad accounts, Pixels, catalogs and team access, together under a single corporate umbrella. Meta has recently started using the term **"Business Portfolio"** instead of "Business Manager" in its interface. Management is now largely handled through **Meta Business Suite** and the **Business Settings** screens. In short, the name may have changed, but the function is the same: think of it as the title deed to your company's digital assets on Meta.

For B2B companies, this structure matters in three key ways:

- **Ownership control:** Assets belong to the company, not to individuals. When an employee leaves, the Instagram account doesn't leave with them.
- **Secure collaboration:** You can give agencies, freelancers or sister companies limited, revocable access without sharing passwords.
- **Accurate measurement:** When the Pixel, Conversions API and domain verification are managed in one place, you can reliably track the performance of your lead campaigns.

Because B2B buying cycles are long, ad data needs to be collected accurately and without interruption for months on end. Setting up Business Manager correctly from the start prevents data loss down the line.

## What should you have ready before you start?

Before you begin, set aside 15–20 minutes for the preparation below. It will keep you from getting stuck halfway through the process.

1. **A personal Facebook account:** Meta requires a real personal account to create a business portfolio. Fake profiles or ones "opened on behalf of the company" can quickly get restricted. So the person who will manage the portfolio should use their own real account.
2. **A corporate email address:** Use an address on your company domain (e.g. marketing@yourcompany.com) instead of Gmail or Hotmail. This detail matters for business verification.
3. **An asset inventory:** List your existing Facebook Page, Instagram account, ad accounts and Pixels. Note who currently controls each one.
4. **Official company details:** Legal company name, address, phone number, tax ID and website. This information must match your trade registry records exactly.
5. **Website DNS access:** For domain verification, you'll need an IT contact who can access DNS records, or your hosting panel credentials.
6. **A corporate credit card or payment method:** A payment method issued in the company's name, to be linked to the ad account.

**Practical tip:** Keep your asset inventory in a simple table. Include columns for asset name, asset type, current owner, people with access and action. Especially at companies that have worked with multiple agencies, this table clears up the "whose ad account is this?" question.

## How do you set up Meta Business Manager step by step?

### 1. Create your business portfolio

Go to business.facebook.com and sign in with your personal Facebook account. Click "Create account" or "Create a business portfolio." The screen that opens asks for three pieces of information:

- **Business name:** Your public-facing brand name, consistent with your legal company name. For example, "ABC Industrial Automation."
- **Your first and last name:** This must match the name on your personal account.
- **Business email address:** Your corporate address.

After you submit the form, a confirmation link will be sent to your email address. Some actions remain restricted until you confirm this link, so verify your email first.

### 2. Fill in your business details completely

Go to Business Settings > Business Info. Fill in the fields for your legal company name, address, phone number, website and tax ID. This information forms the basis of the business verification you'll complete later. An address or company name that doesn't exactly match the details in the Trade Registry Gazette can lead to your verification being rejected.

On this screen, you'll also see your **Business ID** number. Save this long numeric code somewhere. You'll need it when setting up partnerships with agencies and when corresponding with Meta support.

### 3. Add your Facebook Page

From the Business Settings > Accounts > Pages menu, click the "Add" button. You'll see three options:

- **Add a Page:** Use this option if the Page is already under your control. The Page is linked directly to your portfolio.
- **Request access to a Page:** If the Page belongs to another business (e.g. one of your clients' Pages), use this option to request access. Ownership stays with them.
- **Create a new Page:** If you don't have a Page yet, you can create one here.

The most common situation at B2B companies is that the Page is still sitting in a former agency's portfolio. In that case, ask the agency to remove the Page from their portfolio, then add it to your own portfolio "as the owner."

### 4. Connect your Instagram account

Go to Business Settings > Accounts > Instagram Accounts, click "Add" and log in with your Instagram account. The account must be a **professional account** (business or creator). If it's a personal account, switch it to a professional account in the Instagram app first.

Next, link the Instagram account to your Facebook Page as well. This way, your ads run with a consistent identity on both platforms, and messages and comments are gathered in one place in the Meta Business Suite inbox. If your Instagram bio, highlights and contact buttons haven't yet been tailored to a B2B audience, our guide to [optimizing your Instagram profile for B2B](/blog/instagram-profile-optimization-b2b) will come in handy at this stage.

### 5. Create or add your ad account

The Business Settings > Accounts > Ad Accounts menu again offers three options: add an existing ad account, request access or create a new ad account.

When creating a new account, there are three critical settings to watch:

- **Time zone:** If you operate in Turkey, select (GMT+03:00) Istanbul. The time zone can't easily be changed later. The wrong choice will throw off your daily reports and ad scheduling.
- **Currency:** You can choose TRY, USD or EUR. This setting can't be changed later either; to change it, you'll need to open a new account. Agree with your finance team in advance on which currency you'll use for budget planning and invoicing.
- **Account name:** Give it a clear name such as "Company Name – Main Account" or "Company Name – TR Lead Gen." If you'll be opening separate accounts for multiple product lines or markets, establish a naming convention from the start.

Newly created portfolios are limited in the number of ad accounts they can open. This limit increases over time based on spending history and account trustworthiness, so avoid opening unnecessary accounts.

### 6. Set up your payment method

From within the ad account, go to Billing & Payments and add a credit card issued in the company's name or another supported payment method. Make sure the company name and tax ID are entered correctly in the billing details. Otherwise, e-invoicing and expense recording can cause problems on the accounting side.

If you haven't yet planned how to split your budget across Meta, LinkedIn and other channels, take a look at our article on [how to allocate your B2B social media ad budget](/blog/b2b-social-media-ad-budget-allocation).

### 7. Set up the Meta Pixel and Conversions API

Create a new dataset from Business Settings > Data Sources > Datasets (formerly called Pixels) and connect it to your website. There are three common ways to set it up:

1. **Via Google Tag Manager:** The most flexible method, reducing the marketing team's dependence on developers.
2. **CMS integration:** Ready-made plugins are available on platforms such as WordPress and Shopify.
3. **Adding the code manually:** The Pixel code is added to the `<head>` section of every page on the site.

The core events B2B companies should track are:

- **Lead:** Contact or demo form submission.
- **CompleteRegistration:** Webinar or event registration.
- **ViewContent:** Viewing a product, solution or pricing page.
- **Contact:** Clicking a phone number or WhatsApp button.
- **Custom events:** Such as whitepaper downloads or case study views.

Because of browser restrictions and ad blockers, relying on the Pixel alone leads to data loss. That's why you should also enable server-side data sending with the **Conversions API (CAPI)**. To prevent events from being double-counted, deduplication between the Pixel and CAPI should be configured using event_id.

You'll need similar infrastructure on the LinkedIn side as well. We cover this topic separately in our article on [setting up the LinkedIn Insight Tag](/blog/linkedin-insight-tag-installation-guide).

### 8. Verify your domain

Add your website's domain under Business Settings > Brand Safety > Domains. Meta offers three verification methods:

- **DNS TXT record:** The most permanent method; your IT team simply adds a TXT record in the DNS panel.
- **HTML file upload:** The file provided by Meta is uploaded to the site's root directory.
- **Meta tag:** A tag is added to the `<head>` section of the homepage.

Domain verification gives you control over link previews and provides confidence when configuring conversion events. It also makes it harder for others to use your brand's domain without permission. DNS changes can take a few hours to propagate. If verification fails on the first attempt, wait a while and try again.

### 9. Add team members and assign roles

Invite team members by email from Business Settings > Users > People. In the current interface, Meta offers two main access levels:

- **Full control:** Can manage everything in the portfolio, add and remove people, and delete assets.
- **Partial access:** Can only work on the assets assigned to them, with the permissions assigned to them.

After adding a person, you also need to select which assets they can access and with which permissions. For example, a content specialist might be given permission to publish content on the Facebook Page and Instagram account, with no access to the ad account at all.

### 10. Complete your security settings

In the Security Center, make **two-factor authentication (2FA)** mandatory for all users. Takeover attempts targeting B2B companies' Meta accounts are common, and 2FA significantly reduces this risk.

On this screen, also make sure there are **at least two people with full control**. If a sole admin's account gets locked or the admin leaves the company, you could lose access to the entire portfolio.

### 11. Start business verification

Start the business verification process using the "Start verification" option in the Security Center. To prove your company is real, Meta may ask for documents such as a Trade Registry Gazette, tax certificate or an invoice issued in the company's name. Confirmation is then completed with a code sent to the company phone number or your corporate email.

Verification times vary. It usually concludes within a few days, but can sometimes take a few weeks. A verified business portfolio can access higher ad account limits and additional features such as the WhatsApp Business API, and it is in a stronger position when appealing restrictions.

## How should the role and permission structure be designed at B2B companies?

Just as important as the technical setup is the question of who gets access to what. For a mid-sized B2B company, a sample permission structure might look like this:

- **Marketing director:** Full control. The primary owner of the portfolio.
- **IT manager or second admin:** Full control. Serves as the backup admin for emergencies.
- **Performance marketing specialist:** Campaign management permissions on the ad account, plus view and edit permissions on the dataset.
- **Content specialist:** Permission to create and publish content and reply to messages on the Facebook Page and Instagram.
- **Sales team representative:** View-only access to reports, or access to lead data.
- **Finance:** View-only billing permissions.

The key principle here is **least privilege**: everyone should have only the access they need to do their job. If you also want to clarify who needs to approve your content publishing workflow, our guide on [how to set up a social media content approval process in B2B](/blog/b2b-social-media-content-approval-process) can be designed in parallel with your Business Manager permission structure.

**Run a permissions audit every quarter.** Remove employees who have left the company, freelancers whose projects have ended and agencies you no longer work with from the portfolio. This simple routine closes most security gaps.

## How should you grant access when working with an agency?

Never give your agency your personal login credentials or the password to your company's Facebook account. The right method is **partner access**:

1. Ask the agency for their Business ID number.
2. Go to Business Settings > Users > Partners, click the "Add" button and select "Give a partner access to your assets."
3. Enter the agency's Business ID and choose which assets (Page, ad account, dataset) they can access and with which permissions.
4. The agency then assigns these assets to its own team members within its portfolio.

With this method, ownership of the assets always stays with you. When the partnership with the agency ends, you can revoke access with a single click. Campaign history, Pixel data and audiences remain with your company.

**A common critical mistake:** The agency opening the ad account in its own portfolio. In that case, when the partnership ends, all campaign history and optimization data stays with the agency. Make it a contractual requirement that the ad account be created in your portfolio and that the agency be given partner access.

## How much does setup cost?

Setting up and using Meta Business Manager is **completely free**. You don't pay Meta anything to create a business portfolio, add Pages and accounts, invite team members, set up a Pixel or complete business verification. You only pay once you start running ads, and only for the ad budget you spend.

Indirect costs may include:

- **Developer or agency support:** The server-side setup of the Conversions API in particular may require technical expertise.
- **Internal team time:** Building the asset inventory, recovering assets from former agencies and preparing verification documents can take several business days.
- **Tool costs:** Some third-party integration tools for CAPI charge a monthly fee.

## What are the most common mistakes B2B companies make?

We've compiled the mistakes we encounter most often in the field, along with their solutions:

- **Leaving a single admin:** If the admin leaves or their account gets locked, access to the portfolio can be lost entirely. Always assign at least two people with full control.
- **Opening multiple portfolios:** Different departments opening their own portfolios scatters your data. Aim for a single main portfolio. If needed for different brands, open separate ad accounts.
- **Wrong time zone or currency:** These settings can't be changed later, forcing you to open a new account and splitting all your historical data.
- **Setting up the Pixel without testing it:** Use the "Test Events" tool in Events Manager to confirm that form submissions actually fire. A Pixel that's installed but not working is more dangerous than no Pixel at all, because it gives a false sense of security.
- **Skipping 2FA:** Account takeovers usually start with the weakest link, meaning a single user who doesn't use 2FA.
- **Mixing up personal account details with business details:** Using a personal email address or mobile number for business verification can cause problems.

## What should you check in the first 30 days after setup?

Once setup is complete, use the following checklist to make sure your infrastructure is solid:

1. **Week 1:** Check that Pixel and CAPI events appear correctly in Events Manager and that the event match quality score is at a reasonable level.
2. **Week 1:** Add UTM parameters to all ad links. To create a standard naming structure, you can use our guide to [measuring social media traffic with UTM parameters](/blog/utm-parameters-social-media-traffic).
3. **Week 2:** Build your core custom audiences, such as website visitors, people who started but didn't complete a form, and existing customer lists.
4. **Week 2:** Review the messaging competitors are using in their Meta ads. [Competitor analysis with the Meta Ad Library](/blog/meta-ad-library-competitor-analysis) will help you set your creative direction at this stage.

6. **Week 4:** Confirm that lead data from Meta is being transferred correctly to your CRM and that the sales team can follow up on these leads.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Meta Business Manager and Meta Business Suite the same thing?

Not exactly, but they're closely linked. Business Manager (now called a business portfolio) is the overarching structure where you manage asset ownership and access. Meta Business Suite is the interface where you handle day-to-day tasks such as publishing content, messaging and viewing basic insights. You access both with the same account.

### Can you set up Business Manager without a personal Facebook account?

No, you need a real personal Facebook account to create a business portfolio. However, your personal posts on that account aren't linked to your business assets, and your team members can't see your personal profile. Meta is gradually rolling out options that also allow you to log in with a separate Accounts Center profile.

### Is business verification mandatory?

It isn't always required to start running ads, but it's strongly recommended for B2B companies. Verified portfolios get higher ad account limits, access to the WhatsApp Business API and faster resolution when restrictions occur. All you need for verification is to have official documents such as your trade registry record and tax information ready.

### Can we move the ad account from our former agency into our own portfolio?

If the ad account was created in the agency's portfolio, transferring ownership may not always be possible. In that case, you'll usually need to open a new ad account and bring the Pixel and audiences into your own portfolio. That's why, when you start working with a new agency, you should make it a requirement that the ad account be opened in your own portfolio.

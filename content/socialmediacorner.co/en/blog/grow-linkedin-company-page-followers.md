**The fastest way to grow your LinkedIn company page followers is to activate your employees, share regular and valuable content aimed at your target audience, use your monthly invitation credits strategically and, when needed, support these efforts with follower ads.** Organic growth rests on a fully optimized page and content published at least 3-4 times a week that speaks to your ideal customer profile. Shortcuts such as buying followers or "follow-for-follow" exchanges inflate the number but kill engagement and sales potential.

In the B2B world, company page followers are more than a vanity metric. This audience includes the decision-makers your sales team will talk to, the talent you want to hire and the business partners who will recommend you. In this guide, we walk through how a B2B company starting from scratch or from a plateau can grow its LinkedIn page step by step, with concrete tactics, a sample 90-day plan and the most common mistakes.

## Why does follower count matter for B2B companies?

Your follower count sets the baseline for your organic reach on LinkedIn. Every post you publish is first shown to a portion of your followers. If it gets engagement, the algorithm carries it to a wider audience. In other words, the same quality post from a page with 300 followers and a page with 5,000 followers starts from very different levels of reach.

Follower count also works as a **trust signal**. When a purchasing manager researches a supplier they've never heard of, they look at its LinkedIn page. A page with 120 followers whose last post was eight months ago gives the impression that the company is small or inactive. A page that publishes regularly and is followed by hundreds or thousands of people from its industry sends the message: "This company is serious."

But there is a critical distinction here: **What matters isn't the number of followers, but the number of the right followers.** If your target market is purchasing managers at manufacturing companies in Turkey, 10,000 followers made up of irrelevant profiles from other countries will get you nothing. On the contrary, they lower your engagement rate, which leads the algorithm to show your posts less often.

## Is your page ready before you start gathering followers?

Driving traffic to a page that can't convert visitors into followers is like filling a leaky bucket. Before launching any campaigns, complete this checklist:

1. **Logo and cover image:** Your logo should be sharp and square. Your cover image shouldn't be mere decoration; it should be a space that conveys your value proposition in a single sentence. For example: "Energy efficiency solutions for industrial facilities | 15 years of field experience".
2. **Tagline:** Say what you do and who you do it for. Instead of generic phrases like "Building the future together," use a clear sentence such as "Fleet management software for logistics companies."
3. **About section:** The first two sentences appear in search results and previews. Write them with keywords that cover your services, target industries and what sets you apart. Three to four short paragraphs in total are enough.
4. **Industry, company size, location and website:** These fields are used as filters in LinkedIn search. Every field you leave blank reduces your discoverability.
5. **Custom button (CTA):** Choose the button that fits your goal, such as "Visit website," "Contact us" or "Register."
6. **Featured posts:** Pin 2-3 posts that performed best or that best represent your company.
7. **Employee association:** Make sure your company page is correctly selected on your employees' profiles. Company names typed as plain text, without the logo showing, cost you an important source of traffic to your page.

A complete page is recommended more often by LinkedIn, and the rate at which visitors follow it rises noticeably. This is a one-time task, but it determines the return on everything you build on top of it.

## Which followers should you target?

The first question in a follower growth strategy shouldn't be "how" but "who." To answer it, define your ideal follower profile in 3-4 segments:

- **Prospective customers:** Decision-makers and influencers in your target industries (e.g., general managers, purchasing managers, IT directors).
- **Existing customers:** To keep the relationship alive and spot cross-selling opportunities.
- **Talent pool:** Professionals in the roles you want to hire for.
- **Ecosystem:** Business partners, distributors, industry associations, journalists.

The importance of each segment varies from company to company. For a fast-growing tech company, the talent pool may be just as critical as customers, while for an industrial equipment manufacturer, the distributor network may take priority. This definition directly shapes both your content topics and your invitation and ad targeting.

## How effective are employees in growing followers?

The resource B2B companies underestimate most is their own employees. Let's do a simple calculation: imagine a company with 40 employees. If each employee has an average of 500 connections, the total reachable network is around 20,000 people. Most of this network consists of professionals working in the same industry, which means they are exactly your target audience. While the company page has 800 followers, the audience reachable through employees is many times larger.

Follow these steps to get employees involved:

1. **First, make sure employees themselves follow the page.** Surprisingly, at many companies a significant share of employees don't follow their own company page.
2. **Fix the experience section on their profiles.** An experience entry linked to the correct company page opens a door to your company page for everyone who visits their profile.
3. **Send a weekly "sharing suggestion."** Through your internal communication channel, send the most important piece of content published on the company page, along with ready-made copy suggestions employees can share with their own commentary.
4. **Start with leadership.** When the general manager and department heads post regularly, it sets a natural example for other employees.
5. **Don't force it; make it easy.** Rather than making sharing mandatory, create content that employees will be proud to share.

## How should you use LinkedIn invitation credits?

LinkedIn allows company page admins to invite their own connections to follow the page. Each page is allocated a certain number of invitation credits per month (this number may be updated from time to time, so check the current limit in your page's admin panel). When an invitation is accepted, the credit used is generally refunded. This means well-targeted invitations effectively increase the total number of invitations you can send.

To use these credits efficiently:

- **Don't send mass, random invitations.** Instead of inviting everyone in your network, use filters to select by target industry, location and job title.
- **Start with warm connections.** People you've recently interacted with, met in meetings or got to know at events have a much higher acceptance rate.
- **Have more than one page admin.** Invitations are sent through the connections of the admin sending them. Adding admins from sales, HR and marketing expands the network you can invite.
- **Match timing with content.** Send invitations right after a strong piece of content is published on your page. When the invitee looks at the page, they should find an active, valuable feed.

A sample scenario: Three page admins use their monthly credits exclusively on connections in target industries. If the acceptance rate holds up, hundreds of qualified followers can be added every month through this method alone. And each of these followers is someone who already knows you.

## What kind of content wins followers?

People follow because they see value. If your page consists only of product announcements, holiday greetings and trade show photos, nobody has a reason to follow you. Content that wins followers gives readers the feeling that "if I follow this page, I'll learn things that are useful for my work."

### Define your content pillars

For most B2B companies, these four pillars are a good starting point:

- **Educational content (around 40%):** Guides, checklists and common mistakes explaining how to solve a problem in your industry.
- **Opinion and trend content (around 25%):** Your company's perspective on industry changes, regulations and technological developments.
- **Proof content (around 20%):** Customer case studies (with permission), project results, stories from the field.
- **Culture and team content (around 15%):** Employee stories, team introductions, company values.

These ratios aren't strict rules, but they are a reminder that sales-focused content shouldn't dominate the feed.

### Choose the right formats

- **Document (PDF carousel) posts:** Turning a guide into a swipeable 8-10 page document typically drives high swipe-through and save rates in B2B. For example, a carousel titled "7 steps to improve warehouse efficiency."
- **Short videos:** 30-90 second subtitled videos focused on a single message. An engineer explaining how they solved a problem in the field can generate far more engagement than a corporate promotional film.
- **LinkedIn newsletter:** Company pages can publish newsletters. When a newsletter is launched, page followers are notified, and subscribers are alerted to every new issue. A monthly industry newsletter creates a strong reason to follow the page.
- **Polls:** A well-designed poll targeting your industry both drives engagement and provides data for your future content.
- **Events and live streams:** Webinars organized through LinkedIn Events introduce attendees directly to your page.

### Tie your content to a calendar

Random posting makes follower growth random, too. Planning in advance which pillar and which format will be published each week both reduces the team's workload and ensures consistency. ## How often should you post?

As a general rule, **3-5 posts per week** is a healthy range for a B2B company page aiming to grow. Posting once a week is usually not enough for the algorithm to consider your page "active." Posting more than once a day, on the other hand, strains resources and can cause posts to cannibalize each other's reach.

More important than frequency is **consistency**. Posting every day for two weeks and then going silent for a month delivers worse results than posting three times a week at a steady rhythm. If your resources are limited, start with three quality posts a week and increase as the process settles in.

There is no exact formula for posting times. Test within your target audience's working hours, especially weekday mornings and lunchtime, and decide based on your own analytics data.

## How do you attract followers from channels outside the page?

Promoting your LinkedIn page only within LinkedIn means using just part of its potential. Put the touchpoints you already own to work:

- **Website:** Add the LinkedIn icon to the footer and the "About Us" page. At the end of blog posts, add a page link with a note like "We regularly share content like this on LinkedIn."
- **Email signatures:** Adding the company page link to every employee's email signature is a zero-cost, always-on channel. For a company sending hundreds of external emails a day, this means significant visibility.
- **Email newsletters and customer communications:** Add a call to follow your LinkedIn page to the newsletters you send customers.
- **Trade shows and events:** Put a QR code linking to your page on your booth design and brochures. Give visitors a concrete reason, such as "We'll share a summary of our presentation on our LinkedIn page."
- **Webinars:** At the end of the webinar, announce that you'll share the recording and additional resources on your page.
- **Proposals and presentation decks:** Add your LinkedIn page details to the last slide of corporate presentations.

## Do follower ads work?

LinkedIn offers ad formats aimed directly at gaining followers. The best known is personalized **Follower Ads**, which display the user's profile photo side by side with your company logo. In addition, sponsored content with brand awareness or engagement objectives also brings in followers indirectly.

Follower ads make sense when:

- Your page is new and doesn't yet have the initial audience needed for organic growth.
- You're entering a new market or industry and need to become visible to that audience quickly.
- You want to grow your audience ahead of a major launch, rebrand or event.

The key to success with ads is targeting. By targeting by job title, seniority, industry, company size and even specific company lists, you can reach only your ideal follower profile. For a detailed look at targeting options, see our article on [how to do B2B targeting in LinkedIn ads](/blog/linkedin-ads-b2b-targeting).

An important caveat: cost per follower varies significantly depending on audience size, competition and industry. Run a 2-3 week test with a small budget, measure your cost per follower and the profile quality of incoming followers, and then scale. Followers gained through ads quickly go passive if they don't find valuable content on the page. That's why ads should complement your content strategy, not replace it.

## When should you use Showcase pages?

If you have multiple product lines or business units serving different target audiences, Showcase pages can be useful. For example, a machinery manufacturer serving both the agriculture and automotive industries can offer more focused content by creating separate Showcase pages for each audience.

However, a Showcase page can split your main page's followers and adds to your content workload. If your main page can't yet produce content regularly, opening a Showcase page means having two weak pages. Establish your main page first, then consider a Showcase page if there's a clearly distinct audience.

## Does engaging as a page bring in followers?

Yes, it does, and most companies never use this feature. LinkedIn lets you comment on other posts on behalf of your company page. Writing comments that **add value** on behalf of your page on posts from industry leaders, customers, business partners and association pages extends your visibility beyond your own followers.

The key phrase here is "add value." Comments like "Great post!" don't work. Instead, add a data point, a different perspective or a practical suggestion to the discussion. Ten to fifteen quality comments a week can noticeably increase your page's recognition in your industry within a few months.

Responding quickly and thoughtfully to comments on your own posts is just as important. Posts where comments receive replies generally stay in the feed longer.

## Which metrics should you track?

To manage follower growth, monitor these metrics monthly:

- **Net follower growth:** Followers gained minus followers lost.
- **Follower demographics:** The breakdown of your followers by job title, seniority, industry, company size and location. This data shows whether you're attracting the right audience.
- **Organic vs. paid follower ratio:** How much of your growth comes from ads.
- **Page visitor-to-follower conversion:** If visitor numbers are high but follower growth is low, review your page optimization.
- **Engagement rate per post:** If your engagement rate is falling as followers grow, you may be attracting the wrong audience.
- **Follower-generating posts:** Note which content was published in the weeks when follower growth accelerated.

## A sample 90-day follower growth plan

The plan below is an example designed for a B2B company with around 500 followers and 30-50 employees. The numbers are targets and should be adapted to your industry.

### Days 1-30: Foundation

1. Fully complete the page optimization checklist.
2. Make sure all employees follow the page and that their profiles are linked to the correct page.
3. Define your ideal follower profile and 4 content pillars.
4. Prepare the first month's content calendar: 3 posts a week, 12 posts in total.
5. Add 2-3 page admins and start using invitation credits on target connections.
6. Add the page link to email signatures and the website.

### Days 31-60: Momentum

1. Increase posting frequency to 4 times a week; include at least 2 document carousels and 1 short video.
2. Start sending employees a weekly sharing suggestion; aim for at least 3 leaders to post regularly.
3. Write 10 quality comments a week on behalf of the page.
4. Launch a monthly LinkedIn newsletter.
5. Run a small-budget follower ad test and measure cost per follower.

### Days 61-90: Optimization

1. Review analytics from the first 60 days: identify the 5 posts that brought in the most followers and engagement.
2. Do more of the formats and topics that worked, and cut back on the underperformers.
3. Check follower demographics; if you're attracting an off-target audience, adjust your content and targeting.
4. Host a webinar or LinkedIn Event.
5. If the ad test delivered positive results, increase the budget in a controlled way.

The goal is that a company applying this plan consistently will have, after 90 days, not just a higher follower count but also a working content system and data showing which tactics work for it.

## What are the most common mistakes when growing followers?

- **Buying followers:** Fake or irrelevant accounts lower your engagement rate, the algorithm penalizes your page and your credibility takes a hit.
- **Joining "follow-for-follow" groups:** The number rises in the short term, but this audience isn't your customer.
- **Only talking about yourself:** Letting product-focused posts dominate the feed leads to follower loss.
- **Irregular posting:** Going silent for weeks resets the momentum you've built.
- **Not involving employees:** This means leaving your biggest source of organic reach untapped.
- **Not measuring:** Without knowing which tactic works, every effort amounts to nothing more than trial and error.
- **Not replying to comments:** You miss engagement opportunities and make the page look "unmanned."

## How do you turn followers into sales?

For B2B companies, the ultimate goal of growing followers is business results. To turn your followers into sales opportunities:

- **Track the people who engage with your posts.** If a decision-maker matching your target profile regularly likes or comments on your content, that's a warm signal for your sales team.
- **Use soft calls to action in your content.** At the end of each educational post, point readers to a related guide, webinar or assessment call.
- **Align marketing and sales.** Your sales team should know what content is being published on the page and be able to use it in customer conversations.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to grow LinkedIn company page followers?

It depends on your starting point, number of employees and posting consistency. With regular content, employee participation and active use of invitation credits, noticeable momentum usually appears within the first 2-3 months. Lasting, high-quality growth, however, is the result of 6-12 months of consistent work.

### Does it make sense to buy followers on LinkedIn?

No. Purchased followers are usually fake accounts or accounts irrelevant to your target audience; they don't engage with your posts and they reduce your organic reach by lowering your engagement rate. In addition, behavior that violates LinkedIn's terms of service can put your page at risk.

### Which gets more reach: a company page or a personal profile?

On LinkedIn, personal profiles generally achieve higher organic reach than company pages. That's why the most effective strategy is to use both together: the company page serves as a credible hub and content source, while employees and leaders multiply its reach by carrying that content into their own networks.

### How much budget should I allocate to follower ads?

A testing approach is recommended rather than a fixed figure. Run a 2-3 week test campaign with a small budget, and measure your cost per follower and the profile quality of incoming followers. If the results match your target audience, increase the budget gradually.

## Conclusion

There is no single magic way to grow your LinkedIn company page followers. Lasting results come from several mutually reinforcing elements coming together consistently: a fully optimized page, a clearly defined target audience, valuable content published regularly, active employee participation, smartly used invitation credits and, when needed, well-targeted ads. When you move forward by measuring all of this and improving based on the data, your follower count stops being just a number and becomes a real asset for sales, recruitment and brand awareness.

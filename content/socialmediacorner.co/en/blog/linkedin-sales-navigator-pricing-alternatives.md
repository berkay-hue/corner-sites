The Advanced plan, designed for teams, runs approximately $140–180 per user per month. Advanced Plus, which offers CRM integration, is priced by custom quote.** For B2B teams whose budget doesn't stretch that far or whose needs are different, LinkedIn Premium Business, data and prospecting tools such as Apollo.io, Lusha and Cognism, or well-structured free LinkedIn searches are strong alternatives.

> **Note:** LinkedIn regularly updates its pricing based on region, currency, promotions and billing period. If you purchase from Turkey, you may see different amounts on screen. The figures below are approximate. Check the current price on LinkedIn's own page before you buy.

## What is LinkedIn Sales Navigator and who is it designed for?

Sales Navigator is a paid prospecting tool (for finding potential customers) that LinkedIn developed for sales teams. A standard LinkedIn account lets you find people. Sales Navigator, on the other hand, helps you **filter the right people, track them and reach out at the right time**.

It is primarily designed for:

- **B2B teams running account-based marketing (ABM):** Those who want to map decision-makers at specific companies.
- **Industries with long sales cycles:** In fields such as software, consulting, industrial equipment and financial services, a single sale can take months. Tracking changes among decision-makers during that time becomes critical.
- **Sales reps practicing social selling:** Professionals who build trust through content, comments and relationships rather than cold messages.
- **Founders and senior executives:** Time-strapped people who drive business development through their own networks.

The product's core function is to run far more fine-tuned searches on LinkedIn's own data and turn those searches into a sales workflow.

## How much does Sales Navigator cost?

LinkedIn offers Sales Navigator in three main plans. The plan names have changed over time: the former "Professional" plan is now called **Core**, and the former "Team" plan is now **Advanced**.

### Core plan

Designed for individual sales reps, founders and one-person business development roles.

- **Approximate price:** Around $99 per month when billed monthly, or around $80 per month when billed annually.
- **Key features:** Advanced lead and company filters, saving leads and accounts, alerts such as job changes and posts, monthly InMail credits, an extended version of "Who's Viewed My Profile" data, and personalized lead recommendations.
- **Who it's for:** Anyone who spends at least a few hours a week prospecting on LinkedIn.

### Advanced plan

Designed for teams with more than one sales rep.

- **Approximate price:** Around $140–180 per user per month. The per-unit cost drops with annual billing.
- **In addition to Core:** The **TeamLink** feature (seeing warm introduction opportunities through your teammates' connections), lead sharing within the team, Smart Links (seeing who viewed the content you share and for how long), bulk export, and centralized administration/billing.
- **Who it's for:** Sales teams of 3 or more people and teams working from a shared target account list.

### Advanced Plus plan

Designed for enterprise-scale sales organizations.

- **Price:** There is no public list price. Custom quotes are provided based on the number of seats and contract length. In practice, the annual cost per user is significantly higher than the Advanced plan.
- **In addition to Advanced:** Deep synchronization with CRMs such as Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics, automatic updating of CRM data, advanced reporting and enterprise security options.
- **Who it's for:** Large organizations where the CRM sits at the center and that have a revenue operations (RevOps) team.

## What factors affect the true cost?

The list price is only the starting point. When budgeting, factor in these items as well:

1. **Billing period:** Annual billing typically saves around 15–20% compared to monthly. However, if you're not sure you'll actually use the tool, start with monthly billing.
2. **Number of seats:** When a 5-person team moves to the Advanced plan, the annual cost can easily reach several thousand dollars. Ask whether each rep will genuinely be an active user.
3. **Regional pricing and taxes:** For purchases made from Turkey, local currency, VAT and exchange rate differences can change the total amount.
4. **Free trial:** LinkedIn usually offers a one-month free trial of the Core plan. If you don't cancel before the trial ends, automatic billing begins. Set a reminder in your calendar.
5. **Cost of complementary tools:** Sales Navigator does not give you email addresses or phone numbers. If you need this data, you also need to account for the cost of an additional data tool.
6. **Time cost:** The most expensive item is often not the license but the hours your reps spend. A poorly structured Sales Navigator setup can end up costing a lot, even if it looks cheap on paper.

## Is Sales Navigator worth the money?

The answer depends entirely on your sales economics. You can get clarity with a simple return calculation.

**A hypothetical example:** Say a B2B software company has an average annual contract value of $6,000. The Core plan costs roughly $960 a year. In that case, a single new customer covers more than six times the annual license cost.

Here's how you can run the calculation for your own company:

1. **Determine your average customer value** (annual contract or initial order amount).
2. **Derive the conversion rate of meetings sourced from LinkedIn** from your historical data.
3. **Write down how many additional meetings per month** you're targeting.
4. **Add up license + complementary tool + time costs.**
5. If additional meetings × conversion rate × customer value exceeds the total cost, the investment makes sense.

For example, if you're targeting 4 additional meetings a month and 10% of meetings convert into sales, you'll win roughly 4–5 new customers a year. If your average customer value is a few hundred dollars, Sales Navigator is most likely an unnecessary expense. If it's a few thousand dollars, it's a very strong investment.

If you want to extend this kind of calculation across all your social media activities, the metrics framework in our [guide to measuring B2B social media ROI](/blog/measure-b2b-social-media-roi) will make the job easier.

### When Sales Navigator usually makes sense

- Your average customer value is high and your sales cycle is long.
- Your target audience is active on LinkedIn (executives, decision-makers in technology, finance, HR and marketing).
- You do account-based selling and need to track developments at specific companies.
- Your team has reps who regularly create content and engage on LinkedIn.

### When Sales Navigator is usually unnecessary

- Your target audience is passive on LinkedIn (e.g., small local businesses, industries dominated by field workers).
- Your product is low-priced and sold on volume.
- No one on the team will use the tool on a weekly basis.
- What you really need is bulk email and phone data, not relationships on LinkedIn.

## What features set Sales Navigator apart from free LinkedIn?

When making an investment decision, it's important to know exactly what you're buying with your money. In practice, the features that make the biggest difference are:

- **Advanced filters:** More than 50 filters, including company size, seniority level, function, years at the company, changed jobs in the past 90 days and posted in the past 30 days. Most of these aren't available on free LinkedIn.
- **Breadth of search results:** Free accounts hit the commercial use limit and only see a limited portion of search results. Sales Navigator lets you work with much larger result sets.
- **Saved searches and alerts:** You get notified when new people matching your criteria are added. This keeps you up to date without constantly scanning the market.
- **Lead and account lists:** You can organize target accounts into lists and keep notes on each one.
- **InMail credits:** The ability to message people you aren't connected with directly.
- **Account mapping:** You can visually organize decision-maker, influencer and user roles at a company (Advanced and above).

The value of these features emerges when you tie them to a process. Sales Navigator bought purely for "better search" is usually canceled within a few months.

## What are the best LinkedIn Sales Navigator alternatives?

To evaluate alternatives properly, first clarify your need. **Do you want to build relationships within LinkedIn, or are you looking for data to reach people outside LinkedIn (email, phone)?** These two needs call for different tools.

### 1. LinkedIn Premium Business

The closest and cheapest alternative to Sales Navigator.

- **Approximate price:** Around $50–60 per month.
- **What it offers:** Unlimited profile view history, a limited number of InMails per month, company insights and broader search capabilities.
- **What it lacks:** The advanced lead filters, saved search alerts and lead lists found in Sales Navigator.
- **Who it's for:** Founders and consultants who use LinkedIn for occasional prospecting and reach a limited number of people each month.

### 2. Free LinkedIn and Boolean search

Surprisingly powerful when used correctly.

- **Price:** Free.
- **How to use it:** You can narrow down results by using Boolean expressions in the search bar, such as `"purchasing manager" AND (automotive OR "spare parts") NOT intern`. Location, current company and industry filters are also available on free accounts.
- **What it lacks:** There's a monthly commercial use limit and a limited number of results, and there's no alert system or list management.
- **Who it's for:** Early-stage companies testing the market and identifying their first 50–100 target accounts.

### 3. Apollo.io

One of the platforms most often chosen by teams that need to reach people outside LinkedIn.

- **Price:** It has a limited free plan. Paid plans start at a few tens of dollars per user per month.
- **What it offers:** A large B2B contact and company database, verified work emails and phone numbers, email sequences, pulling data from LinkedIn profiles via a Chrome extension, and CRM integrations.
- **What it lacks:** Its LinkedIn engagement data (signals such as who's posting or changing jobs) isn't as current or rich as Sales Navigator's. In markets like Turkey, you need to test the data coverage for your specific industry.
- **Who it's for:** Teams that combine outbound email with LinkedIn and need scalable lead lists. To try it with the free plan, you can sign up through [Apollo.io](https://get.apollo.io/679irkzm35ya).

### 4. Lusha, Kaspr and similar contact data tools

These tools focus on finding a person's contact details while you view their LinkedIn profile.

- **Price:** They work on a credit basis. Free tiers and low-to-mid budget monthly plans are available.
- **What they offer:** Fast email and phone lookup, a Chrome extension and basic CRM integrations.
- **What they lack:** They don't offer prospecting strategy or filtering. You need to have already found your target person.
- **Who they're for:** Teams that build their target list with Sales Navigator or free LinkedIn and then want to reach out by phone/email.

### 5. ZoomInfo, Cognism and enterprise data platforms

- **Price:** Sold on annual contracts, generally at budgets significantly higher than Sales Navigator.
- **What they offer:** Comprehensive firmographic data, intent signals, org charts and compliance-focused data management (GDPR compliance is an especially important criterion in the European market).
- **Who they're for:** Large sales teams and companies operating in international markets that need to manage data quality at enterprise scale.

### 6. LinkedIn automation tools

Waalaxy, Dripify and similar tools automate sending connection requests and messages.

- **Caution:** LinkedIn's user agreement restricts the use of third-party automation tools. There is a risk of your account being restricted or even shut down. In addition, non-personalized bulk messages damage your brand perception.
- **Our recommendation:** Don't think of these tools as an alternative to Sales Navigator; at most, treat them as a controlled, low-volume helper. Avoid using them on founder or senior executive accounts.

## Which option is right for you? Recommendations by scenario

To make the decision easier, let's look at the four most common scenarios.

**Scenario 1: A 1–3 person B2B startup with founder-led sales**
Start with free LinkedIn and Boolean search. Once your weekly prospecting routine is in place, activate the free trial of the Core plan. Measure how many quality meetings it generates over a month and make your decision based on that data.

**Scenario 2: A 3–10 person team focused on social selling on LinkedIn**
The Advanced plan is usually the right choice. TeamLink and shared lists prevent mistakes like two reps messaging the same account at the same time. If the Advanced seat cost feels high, consider giving licenses only to your most active reps and keeping the rest on Core.

**Scenario 3: An outbound email-heavy, volume-focused team**
A data and sequencing platform like Apollo.io can be the main tool. Sales Navigator can be kept only for senior reps who handle strategic accounts. ## 7 steps to get the most out of Sales Navigator

If you've decided to buy, follow these steps in the first 30 days of your license:

1. **Put your ideal customer profile (ICP) in writing.** Gather industry, company size, location, decision-maker titles and exclusion criteria on a single page.
2. **Create 3–5 saved searches.** Make them specific, for example "purchasing managers who started their role in the past 90 days at manufacturing companies with 50–200 employees in Istanbul."
3. **Build a target account list.** Keep it to 50–100 companies at first. Identify 2–4 decision-makers for each.
4. **Take a signal-based approach.** A job change, news about the company's growth or a post the person shared is a natural entry point for your message. You can find practical examples of this approach in our [guide to writing cold sales messages on LinkedIn](/blog/linkedin-cold-sales-message-examples).
5. **Engage first, message second.** Leave meaningful comments on your target person's posts for 1–2 weeks. Then send a connection request. Save your InMail credits only for high-priority people who don't accept your connection.
6. **Strengthen your profile and content.** The first thing someone who receives your message does is look at your profile. Regular, value-driven posts noticeably increase response rates. To make this sustainable, build [a LinkedIn content calendar tailored to B2B](/blog/b2b-linkedin-content-calendar).
7. **Measure weekly.** Track connection requests sent, acceptance rate, response rate and number of meetings booked in a simple spreadsheet. If your response rate is below expectations at the end of the month, question your targeting and messaging, not the tool.

## The most common mistakes when using Sales Navigator

- **Buying the tool without building a process:** The license is activated, used heavily in the first week, then forgotten. Don't buy it without adding a "prospecting block" to your weekly calendar.
- **Working with overly broad filters:** Searches that return thousands of results, like "all general managers in Turkey," cause you to lose focus. Narrow, signal-based lists are far more effective.
- **Using InMail like a bulk messaging channel:** Non-personalized InMails don't get replies and waste your credits.
- **Lack of coordination within the team:** Two reps sending different messages to the same person undermines your professional image. Use shared lists and CRM notes.
- **Neglecting organic visibility:** Sales Navigator doesn't build trust on its own. If your company page and your executives' profiles are weak, even the best targeting won't deliver results. If organic efforts don't provide enough visibility, you can also reach target accounts with ads. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does LinkedIn Sales Navigator have a free trial?

Yes, LinkedIn usually offers a one-month free trial of the Core plan, although this offer can change periodically. If you don't cancel before the trial period ends, automatic billing begins on the plan you selected. Preparing your target list and measurement criteria before starting the trial helps you make the most of that month.

### Can I see people's email addresses with Sales Navigator?

No, Sales Navigator doesn't directly provide people's email addresses or phone numbers. You can only see this information if the person has shared it publicly on their profile or if they are one of your connections. If you need email and phone data, you'll need to use complementary tools such as Apollo.io, Lusha or Cognism.

### What is the most important difference between the Core and Advanced plans?

The most important difference is the features geared toward teamwork. The Advanced plan offers warm introduction opportunities through your teammates' connections via TeamLink. It also includes shared lead lists, Smart Links and centralized license management. If you work alone, the Core plan is usually sufficient.

### Does it make sense to use Sales Navigator in the Turkish market?

If your target audience is large enterprises, technology, finance, consulting or export-oriented industrial companies, Sales Navigator delivers effective results in Turkey as well. A significant share of decision-makers in these sectors are active on LinkedIn. If your target audience is passive on LinkedIn, it's recommended that you first test with free search how many people you can actually reach.

## Conclusion

However, it isn't the right choice for every company. The Core plan costs roughly $80–100 per month, and the Advanced plan approximately $140–180 per user per month. Whether you'll get a return on that cost depends on your customer value, your sales cycle and how active your team is on LinkedIn. If your budget is limited, you can start with free LinkedIn and Boolean search, move up to Premium Business, or, if you're focused on email, consider data platforms like Apollo.io.

Whichever tool you choose, it's strategy, not the tool, that determines the outcome.
